# Cozy Medieval Zombies

A cute isometric medieval village, in the Habbo / early-2000s pixel style, overrun by the dead. One continuous map with real line of sight; friends play together in one world. Plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript with ES modules: raw WebGL2 for the world (`src/gl.js`; Canvas 2D generates the dev art and uploads it as textures), DOM for the interface. No build step, no engine, no framework, no rendering library. Started 2026-10-08 from two prototypes (`docs/01`), both kept as they were; nothing from them binds here until it is re-decided in `docs/02`.

## The laws of the design

- One continuous map. No cuts between inside and outside; a design that needs a scene transition is wrong for this game.
- If the game is good, people will sit for a loading screen. Never let a load time or an assumed browser limit squash an idea (`docs/00`, Laws).
- **Never harder on a clock** (Michael, 2026-10-13; `docs/00`, Laws): no world decay, no shutoffs, no scheduled horde, no danger that grows with the days; every rise in danger is one a player chose.
- Windows have shutters, not glass; closed shutters block sight. **Nobody climbs through a window** (Michael, 2026-10-16), player or dead, and a window is never a safe spot: the dead may batter a window's shutters or boards open to see in, never to pass, and come round to a door (#140). Doors start closed and block sight and bodies.
- **A sealed room is safe, and it is a cell** (Michael, 2026-10-17): nothing digs and no wall breaks, so you may hold out as long as your food and water last, but you cannot leave (#147).
- **Gatherables wobble and are outlined** (Michael's law, 2026-10-11): anything that will have a gathering action (mushrooms, rocks, trees, crops, and whatever joins them) is a game object marked `interactable`; a click sways it, its art has a dark outline all the way round, and any character walking through one that is not `solid` (a crop, a mushroom; never a tree or a rock) sets it swaying, one round at a time. Trees are excused from the outline. Decoration has neither.
- **Only trees are seen through** (Michael, 2026-10-11): the soft hole round the player behind something tall is cut in tree canopies alone; walls have their own fade.
- Villagers carry no weapons and never fight for the player; they will flee, follow, and stay (`docs/04`, not built), and they die. The one exception is the guide (`docs/34`), immune to the dead whatever the settings.
- **Only the pause menu stops the game** (Michael, 2026-10-15): with the bag, a container, a body, a station, the character screen, or the map open, the world runs and the player can still fight, cast, draw, and use E, B, and T; a click on a panel is the panel's, a click on the world is the world's.
- **Combat numbers are data** (`data/items.json` weapon blocks, `data/combat.json`, `data/tuning.json`): a ruling changes a number there, not code. The mouse in combat is `docs/21`: left the main hand, a tap of right the menu and a hold the off hand's stance, middle or Q the main hand's other mode.
- **Item art comes as a pair** (Michael, 2026-10-12): the in-world picture and the inventory icon from one design, and for anything held or worn a 38 by 38 portrait for the character screen's slot; a delivery that lacks any of them is flagged. Delivered art is data, read and never run (`docs/26`, `27`).
- Check `docs/05-someday.md` before a choice that would foreclose something cheaply, and say so before making it.
- Placeholder art is generated at boot through the same interface as real sprite sheets. Do not wait for art.
- Borrowing from the parents (`docs/01`): copy files, never link or import across repositories; read the parent's doc for a system before copying its code, so its reasons come along; a copied module starts with a comment naming its origin file and date; data (items, loot, sprites, maps) is always written fresh for this game. When a parent did something well, say so and decide again anyway: nothing from a parent binds here until it is in `docs/02`.

## The two rules of shipping

**Multiplayer is live: every addition and every change is built for a guest in the same commit** (Michael, 2026-10-13). Nothing is built single-player first with the guest's side owed for later; a change that works for the host alone is not done. Before a change is called done: (1) it runs in the world's step on the authority (the host), never in a DOM handler or a client-only module (`main.js` only reads the world; what the host does beyond the step is `host.js`, which a server world runs too); (2) a guest can do it through the same path the host's player uses (a verb in `verbs.js`, a handler in `ui/inventory-actions.js`, a key event in the snapshot, `placement.performPlacement`, a `net.send*` command), every target named by an id, an address, or an entry's uid; (3) what it changes reaches a guest, in the save (`game.serialize`, `restore`) and in the frame (a player's record, the delta of the dead, the pokes, the touched chunks, the queues), and the replica draws it as the host does; (4) world state it adds is in the sync check (`game.syncParts`); (5) per-player state lives on the player, never in a module-level variable; time comes from `loop.simTime()` and chance from `rng`; (6) it is tried with a guest in two tabs (`?nosave&relay=none`, `__game.net.open({ join })`), `check("sync")` passes, and what it adds to a frame is read from `net.status().wire`. The habits behind this are `docs/31-multiplayer/08`; a spec names the guest's side of every feature as it names the host's.

**The wiki is part of shipping** (Michael, 2026-10-14: "not worth building if it's going to immediately fall behind"). A feature, a fix, or a change to a number is not done until the wiki (`C:/Users/micha/Dev/cmz-wiki`, published at cmzgame.github.io/cmz-wiki) is checked and updated for it, **in the same session as its merge to `main`**: the sync reads `origin/main`, so it follows the merge; a pull request names its wiki update (`github.md` 8.4); a batch of merges in one session is synced once, at its end, and nothing in it is reported done before that sync is pushed. The method is that repository's `CLAUDE.md`. Whoever merges the change owns its wiki update, or labels the pull request `needs-wiki` so the wiki Secondary does the pages; a change no player can see says so in one line of the pull request and needs no sync. A bug fixed from `docs/bugs.md` moves to its Fixed section with the commit.

## What a doc is (the sweep, 2026-10-16)

A doc is a **rule** (this file and `docs/02`), a **reference** (it describes the game as it is; rewritten in place, never appended; its status block at the top replaced, never stacked), or a **record** (`docs/history/`, kept whole and never edited again). A build gets a `docs/02` entry only if it forced a ruling, and then the ruling alone; the record of a build is its pull request, the spec's status block, and `docs/history/02-build-log.md`. An overturned ruling is struck where it sits, naming what overturned it. Open work is a GitHub issue, never a doc section. Each rule has one home; everything else points to it.

## Implementing from a spec

Michael scopes with one model and builds with a cheaper one. The implementer: (1) reads the whole doc and the files it names before writing code; the doc decides, and "default" marks what Michael may overrule, not what the implementer may; (2) follows the build order one step at a time and verifies each in the browser pane through `window.__game` (`docs/testing.md`) before the next, never asking Michael to test by hand; (3) runs `node --check` on every touched module; (4) changes nothing in the rig's numbers (`BODY`, the poses) or the drawing rules in `src/figure/` beyond what the doc asks, and does not touch `data/hair`, `data/layers`, or `data/art` (Michael's art and the design agent's deliveries; a body number is tried from the console first, `docs/testing.md`); (5) runs the doc's acceptance list as a script and reports each result plainly, failures included; (6) ticks `TODO.md`, commits in the house style (plain messages, no attribution lines), pushes its branch, and opens a pull request by `github.md` section 8, naming its wiki update; (7) logs any ruling the build forced in `docs/02` under the day's date and says so in the final message.

## Running and testing

`python serve.py 8010`, then http://localhost:8010 (ES modules do not load from `file://`); the valley by default, `?map=square` the proving ground. The app's preview serves this worktree as `medieval` (`.claude/launch.json`; `medieval-second` on 8011 for when Michael's own server holds 8010, `relay` on 8012, `wiki` on 8020); `serve.py` must be restarted after it is edited. In the pane, `window.__game` is the live world and `await __game.check("<name>")` runs the instruments under `tools/checks/`; `node tools/check.js`, `node tools/world.js` (the whole world under Node and its determinism hash), `node tools/savecache.js`, and `python -I tools/validate-data.py` are the headless proofs CI runs. **Read `docs/testing.md` before scripting the pane:** it shares Michael's settings, worlds, and identities (a test restores what it toggles, in a `finally`, and opens with `?nosave`); the sim reads time only through `loop.simTime()` and chance only through `src/rng.js`; a two-tab test opens with `?relay=none`.
