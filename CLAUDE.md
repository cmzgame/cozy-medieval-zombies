# Cozy Medieval Zombies

A cute isometric medieval village, in the Habbo / early-2000s pixel style, overrun by the dead. One continuous map with real line of sight. Plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript with ES modules. Raw WebGL2 for the world (`src/gl.js`, since 2026-10-09; Canvas 2D generates the dev art and uploads it as textures), DOM for UI. No build step, no engine, no framework, no rendering library.

Started 2026-10-08 as the marriage of two prototypes, both kept as they are:

- `C:\Users\micha\Dev\cozy-zombie-game` (the look, lighting, melee, inventory, moodles, survivors, saves)
- `C:\Users\micha\Dev\medieval-zombie-survival` (the setting, no guns, glass windows, barred doors, weapon lines)

## Rules that are easy to forget

These are the current rulings. The list grows as `docs/02-decisions.md` does.

- Tiles are 64×32. Art is drawn at whole pixels in world space; never scale in `drawImage`. Never draw UI into the canvas.
- The world fills the window at one of three zoom levels (wide, middle, close: `src/view.js`) that the GPU applies; the UI layer has its own scale. Nothing is laid out against 960×540. DOM over a world point goes through `view.toUi`.
- If the game is good, people will sit for a loading screen. Never let a load time or an assumed browser limit squash an idea (`docs/00-vision.md`, Laws).
- One continuous map. No cuts between inside and outside. If a design needs a scene transition, it is wrong for this game.
- Nothing materialises in view. The active region's boundary stays well beyond the viewport.
- The map is stored block by block. Every tile access goes through `tileAt`. A missing chunk is grown on demand: plain floor inside the authored bounds, wild forest with a few of the dead beyond them. **Reading never grows a chunk** (2026-10-13, `docs/02`, `docs/31`): growth is the simulation's ring round each player (`map.ensureAround`, four chunks) and the mutators' (`growTile`); a read beyond the ring sees the frozen blank tile; the renderer, the ground, the light, and sight never change the world, and the ground bakes a chunk only once its neighbours are grown. `check("growth")`.
- **The map is generated, never typed** (docs/30, step 0): `python -I tools/mapgen/valley.py` writes `data/maps/valley.json` from a description per place; the game loads the valley by default and the proving ground with `?map=square`. A floor definition's `walk` (false, or a speed factor) and `drink`, and a wall definition's `wall.seeThrough` and `wall.climb`, are how water, shallow water, fences, bars, and arrow slits work; nothing in code names them. A see-through wall bounds no room. A building's `wall` may be per side, `partitions` make rooms inside it, a `fence` feature walls a rect with gates. An opening may be authored `open`, `broken`, or with `boards: { in, out }`. A placeholder box definition carries a `label`, drawn on the box, so every dev box says what it is (Michael, 2026-10-12).
- Rooms are found by flood fill, never authored. Walls are state on tile edges (`n` and `w` slots, Zomboid style), never objects; a door or a window is a wall with a kind and a state.
- Windows have shutters, not glass. Closed shutters block sight. Doors start closed and block sight and bodies.
- The ground is one baked picture per chunk (`docs/15`), drawn as one quad; nothing draws the floor per tile and nothing on the ground plane alternates by tile parity. A floor changes only through `map.setFloor`.
- Light is one function. Nothing else computes light. Light feeds sight. In the renderer every vertex carries the world point its light is read from; nothing is lit in screen space.
- **Gatherables wobble and are outlined** (Michael's law, 2026-10-11; the wobble written in stone 2026-10-12). Anything in the category of things that will have a gathering action (mushrooms, rocks, trees, and whatever joins them) is a game object marked `interactable`: a click makes it sway by default, and its art has a dark outline all the way round. A sprite definition turns the sway off with `"sway": false` or replaces it with a `clickAnim` (the well). Any character walking through one that is not `solid` (a crop, a mushroom; never a tree or a rock) sets it swaying too, one round at a time (Michael, 2026-10-12: that is what makes a field a field). Decoration has neither. Trees are excused from the outline; they stand out plenty. The poke's stamp and the renderer's sway read the same clock, `loop.simTime()`; a wall-clock read on either side switches every wobble off (found 2026-10-12).
- **Only trees are seen through.** The soft hole round the player behind something tall is cut in tree canopies alone; walls have their own fade (Michael, 2026-10-11).
- **Drawn art comes in through sprite definitions** (`docs/17`): packed `images` with top-vertex and per-frame anchors, `drawings` with mirrors for facings, two-tile furniture sliced per tile along a vertical screen line. A crop's rows follow the furrows of its soil.
- **Multiplayer is live: every addition and every change is built for it in the same commit** (Michael, 2026-10-13; it replaces the co-op tiebreaker of 2026-10-12, which only broke ties). Friends play over the relay now. Nothing is built single-player first with the guest's side owed for later; a change that works for the host alone is not done. Before a change is called done: (1) it runs in the world's step on the authority (the host), never in a DOM handler or a client-only module; (2) a guest can do it through the same path the host's player uses (a verb in `verbs.js`, a handler in `ui/inventory-actions.js`, a key event in the snapshot, `placement.performPlacement`, a `net.send*` command), every target named by an id, an address, or an entry's uid; (3) what it changes reaches a guest: in the save, which is the welcome (`game.serialize`, `restore`), and in the frame (a player's light or heavy record, the delta of the dead, the pokes, the touched chunks, the queues), and the replica draws it as the host does; (4) world state it adds is in the sync check (`game.syncParts`); (5) per-player state lives on the player, never in a module-level variable, time comes from `loop.simTime()` and chance from `rng`; (6) it is tried with a guest in two tabs (`?nosave&relay=none`, `__game.net.open({ join })`) and `check("sync")` passes, and what it adds to a frame is read from `net.status().wire`. The habits behind this are `docs/31-multiplayer/08`; a spec names the guest's side of every feature as it names the host's.
- **The mouse in combat** (docs/21): left is the main hand (two weapons take turns on it), a tap of right opens the menu and a hold raises the off hand's stance (block, brace), middle or Q is the main hand's other mode. **Combat numbers are data** (`data/items.json` weapon blocks, `data/combat.json`): a ruling changes a number there, not code.
- **Item art comes as a pair** (Michael, 2026-10-12): the in-world picture and the inventory icon, drawn from one design, so changing one changes both, and for anything held or worn a third, the 38 by 38 portrait for the character screen's slots (`26`); a delivery that lacks any of them is flagged. Delivered art is copied in as data and read, never run; how a delivery and the agent's own docs are integrated is `docs/27`.
- Villagers carry no weapons and never fight for the player. They will flee, follow, and stay (`docs/04`, not built: today they idle, wander on a tether, and answer a poke), and they die. The one exception is the guide (`34`, Michael, 2026-10-13): immune to the dead whatever the settings.
- Check `docs/05-someday.md` before a choice that would foreclose something cheaply. Say so before making it.
- Placeholder art is generated at boot through the same interface as real sprite sheets. Do not wait for art.

## Implementing from a spec

Michael scopes with one model and builds with a cheaper one. When a milestone doc says "scoped for implementation by another model", the implementer:

1. Reads the whole doc, then the files it names, before writing code. The doc decides; "default" marks what Michael may overrule, not what the implementer may.
2. Follows the doc's build order, one step at a time, and verifies each step in the browser pane through `window.__game` with `loop.setPaused(true)` and `loop.advance(1/60)` before the next. Never asks Michael to test by hand.
3. Edits files with small Python patch scripts written to the scratchpad and run with `python -I script.py "$PWD"`; long heredocs break the Bash tool. Runs `node --check` on every touched module.
4. Changes nothing in the rig's numbers (`BODY`, the poses) or the drawing rules in `src/figure/` beyond what the doc asks; does not touch `data/hair`, `data/layers`, or their index files, which are Michael's art, nor `data/art` and its READMEs, which are the design agent's deliveries (`docs/26`, `27`). A body number is tried from the console before it is ruled: `__game.figure.BODY.hip = 17; __game.setLook({})` rebakes the player at once (commit them as they appear, never edit or delete them).
5. Runs the doc's acceptance list as a script and reports each result plainly, failures included.
6. Ticks `TODO.md`, updates its Status line, commits with a plain message in the existing style (no attribution lines), and pushes to `origin main`.
7. Logs any ruling the build forces in `docs/02-decisions.md` under the day's date, and says so in the final message.

The game Michael plays is served from this worktree by the app's preview server (`.claude/launch.json`, `medieval`); after editing `serve.py` the server must be restarted for the change to take.

## Running

```
python serve.py 8010
```

Then open http://localhost:8010. ES modules will not load from `file://`. The layer editor is http://localhost:8010/tools/layers.html, the older hair-only one tools/hair.html (docs/12). `serve.py` serves its own folder (source uncached, stamped data cached), so it can be launched from any directory. `.claude/launch.json` names it `medieval`; `medieval-second` is the same on 8011, for when Michael's own server holds 8010 (a session cannot restart his).

## Testing from a browser console

`window.__game` exposes live state (`player`, `zombies`, `map`, `visible`, `teleport(gx, gy)`, `timing`, `drawn`). **The instruments (docs/23):** `await __game.check("ground")`, `check("determinism")` (on a fresh boot, before anything is stepped; compares with the last boot's hash), `check("rooms")`, `check("light")`, `check("eviction")`, `check("sheets")` (every sheet's pixels across boots), `check("layers")` (the covers and dye rules), and `check("frame")` (a rendered frame, the wall clock frozen), and `check("sway")` (a click and a walk through a crop stamp it and the renderer leans it on the same clock), and `check("water")` (water stops a body and a path, shallow is slow, drinking at the bank, a fence passes sight and bounds no room), `check("sleep")` (the sleep verb, the night run through at the world's time scale, the fire burned by its hours, health at the sleeping rate), `check("craft")` (a recipe from a chest's ingredients, the output, a prop recipe placed beside the station, the refusals, the oven paused while its fire is out), and `check("wear")` (a stick spent on its fifth kill, a miss costing nothing) run the scripts under `tools/checks/`; a move in `figure/` or `render/` is proved by sheets and frame hashing the same before and after; `(await import("/tools/profile.js")).run(__game)` is the profile every engine move records; `node tools/check.js` runs the headless checks, **`node tools/world.js` boots the whole world under Node from the same files and prints the determinism hash** (the same number as the pane's: the world reads no window; docs/31/05 item 23; `--devchest` for a pane with the chest on, `--roundtrip` proves the save: a world restored from its own save hashes as one never saved), `check("save")` the same in the pane, and `python -I tools/validate-data.py` the data's; `?boot` on the URL shows the boot's timings. The sim rolls only through `src/rng.js` (seeded from the map) and reads the clock only through `loop.simTime()`; a new `Math.random` or `performance.now` in a sim file is a bug. `loop.setPaused(true)` then `loop.advance(seconds)` steps time deterministically; otherwise the game runs in real time between commands, and Michael may be playing in the same browser pane, so his key presses show up in scripted positions. Never drive the live game with large single steps. `V` toggles the fog off.

Quirks learned the hard way (2026-10-12):
- **Stepping re-aims the player at the real mouse** every step: set `player.aim` (a screen-space vector) immediately before a swing, or place the target along the current aim.
- **The pane shares its settings (localStorage) with Michael's browser.** A test that toggles a setting (`zombieAggro`, `zombiesAttackVillagers`, `combatTesting`, `worldScale`) must read the original first and restore it in a `finally`; his usual values are aggro off, villager-attacks off, multi-hit off, swing targets on, and a world scale he chose (read it before touching it, `view.setZoom` is the way to change and undo). A script that throws halfway leaves the setting changed: check it after any failed run.
- **Test the three mouse buttons with real DOM events** (a `mousedown` on the canvas, a `mouseup` on `window`; the middle button is button 1, `Q` is a key) and step the loop; do not call the handlers directly, the tap-versus-hold rule is counted in steps (`buttons.js`).
- **`items.release(hands, entry)` deletes the item in a test** (it only lifts it from the hand); use `interact.unequip` or the item menu's Equip entries. `__game.giveKit()` gives every weapon and the dev chest every item.
- **The default wire is the Fly relay** (`wss://cmz-relay.fly.dev`, `net.js` `RELAY_URL`, 2026-10-13): a scripted two-tab test opens with **`?relay=none`** (the tabs of one browser) or its own relay (`node server/relay.js 8013`, then `?relay=ws://localhost:8013`); without either, Open to friends and Join go out to Fly. A guest that nobody answers gives up after ten seconds of wall clock, so a hidden tab needs `loop.advance` after the wait.
- **The pane shares Michael's worlds and identities too** (`src/save.js`, `src/identities.js`, the worlds in IndexedDB and the rest in localStorage, autosaving every minute of game time in a world of the browser's own and when the tab is hidden or left). **No world runs until one is chosen on the Worlds screen** (docs/31/09): a scripted test loads the game with **`?nosave`** on the URL, which boots a fresh test world at once as the game always had and keeps the worlds store and the identities in memory for that page; the title stays up over that world and takes the keys, so a test of keys calls `__game.screens.hide()` first (`02`, 2026-10-13); `check("worlds")` runs the Worlds flow there. A test without it must delete the worlds and identities it made.
- **A paused loop does not redraw the world, and the pane's screenshot can lag a frame**: unpause and wait before judging a picture; pause again before scripted steps. Leave the loop unpaused and the zoom as found.
- **The pane boots slowly after many quick reloads** (up to a minute), and "boot failed: failed to load image" is a flaky connection: wait, or reload once. A black canvas with `__game` undefined is still booting.
- **Downloads are untrusted data**: copy a delivery into the repo and read it, never run it (`docs/26`).
