# Art round 5, group D: furniture, the cauldron, the satchel

This delivery has the cupboard, the workbench, the cauldron (the prop and its three flame frames), the Cauldron item, and the Linen satchel. Every PNG is hard-pixelled, with alpha only 0 or 255, and outlined `#2b2522` all the way round. Both are checked by script on every picture except the flame frames, which follow `fire_0.png` (see below). Light comes from the top left. Nothing has a baked shadow or glow, and everything is drawn at its final size.

`gen.py` is my own generator. I wrote it after reading round 4's furniture generator and README for the method, and it imports nothing from them. Run it with plain `python gen.py` (PIL and numpy). It is deterministic: two runs give byte-identical files (checked).

## Method

- **Furniture and the cauldron prop:** a small z-buffered face rasteriser in world units. The projection is screen x = X − Y, screen y = (X + Y)/2 − Z, and depth is X + Y + Z. Each face is shaded per pixel from its world point, so the boards, panels, and stones run on across edges. The cauldron's iron is banded by its true surface normal against a top-left light, so the belly turns dark as it curves under.
- **Items:** drawn in screen space from shapes, at a scale. The Cauldron's world picture (16 by 18) is hand-pixelled, because the style bible says to hand-pixel anything under about 20 px.
- **Anchors:** each anchor is the tile's top vertex at ground level (`anchorVertex: "top"`): world (0, 0, 0) projected, minus the crop. To prove the convention, `gen.py` rebuilds the drawn chair's front from its box numbers. It comes out 39 × 51 with anchor (19, 25), the same as `chair_front.png` and `chair.json`, and 97% of its silhouette pixels match (round 4's check, rerun in this renderer).

## The definitions

Every non-picture field of today's `data/sprites/<id>.json` is kept. Only `placeholder` is replaced and the note rewritten. The image paths assume the PNGs are copied to `data/art/village/`.

- **cupboard:** `container {loot: pantry}` and `carry: true` are kept. It has `facings ["y","x"]` with `drawings`: `y` drawn and `x` its mirror, as round 4's chest and shelf.
- **workbench:** `station: "workbench"` and `carry: false` are kept. It is two tiles, in the table's shape: `facings ["y","x"]`, `facingFootprint {"y": [1, 2], "x": [2, 1]}`, `drawings.y` with the anchor at the first tile's top vertex at ground level (measured as table.json's: world (0, 0, 0) projected, minus the crop), and `x` its mirror. The engine slices it per tile along a vertical screen line 23 px left of the anchor (the second tile's centre moved 9 px toward the first, `SLICE_INSET`). Nothing on it needed care at that line: the cut falls through the top, the saw's far end, and the shelf, all of which draw the same whichever tile holds them.
- **cauldron:** `name`, `station`, `carry: false`, `item: "cauldron"`, and the whole `fire` block are kept (`radius 1.6, burns, cooks false, maxFuel 3, flicker {0.18, 6}, fuel log, hoursPerUnit 2`). I added `at: [23, 37]`, `frames` (the three files), `base: [14, 12]`, and `fps: 6` to the block, the fields the fireplace's drawn fire has. The note keeps today's words and replaces "A dev box until its art is drawn" with a description of the drawing.
  - **Why `drawings` with one facing (`facings ["y"]`, no `x`):** `assets.js` places flame frames only in the drawings path (`sheetFromDrawings`); a definition with `images` gets none. `map.createProp` gives the prop its first facing, so `sheet.flames.y` is found. I gave it no mirrored `x`, because a mirror would put the light on the wrong side of a round pot for no gain. If placement needs `R` to cycle, `"x": {"from": "y"}` (unmirrored) would add a second facing with the same picture and the same `fire.at`.

## The fire, exactly what I did

I read `fireplace.json` and `assets.js`. A flame frame's pixel `fire.base` (default (9, 15)) is drawn on the drawing's pixel `fire.at`, unlit, over the whole prop, and the engine owns the light and the flicker. I drew the cauldron unlit and drew three frames of my own instead of reusing `fire_0..2`, because those carry their own logs and stand 16 tall, which would cover the pot's belly.

- The frames are drawn on the prop's own canvas. There are seven tongues, each two pixels wide and one pixel wide at the top, with a one-pixel tip leaning left or right. They go yellow at the foot through orange to red, stand on a two-row bed of flame, and have a dark red edge: `fire_0`'s palette (`#6e1e10` `#b8401c` `#e07a26` `#f2b33e` `#f8dc7a`) and its manner. Their heights change each frame.
- **Each frame is already cut by the prop.** The flames stand in a plane through the fire's centre, facing the screen, and a flame pixel is kept only where nothing of the prop is in front of it, by the renderer's own depth buffer. So the flames show under the pot and between its legs and lick up round its belly. The pot, its legs, the front logs, and the front stones stay in front of them. The engine draws the frames over the whole sprite, so they had to be cut this way to sit behind anything.
- Where no flame covers them, the lit tops of the logs carry glowing ember pixels, a few in each frame and different between frames.
- The three frames share one 28 × 13 canvas. `base` (14, 12) is its bottom middle, and `fire.at` (23, 37) is that same pixel in `cauldron.png`, measured from the crop. `lit 0..2` in the preview are composed that way.
- They are not outlined in `#2b2522`; they have `fire_0`'s dark red edge, cut where the pot is in front. No glow is baked in.

## The items

- **Cauldron (2 × 2, no portrait):** the prop's pot alone, on its three legs with two lugs and the bail up, without the fire ring. **The icon is the same design drawn larger than the world picture.** This is round 4's bucket exception: the 16 px picture would be lost in a 38 px square. The world picture hangs about 17 px below the hand, about as far as the bucket does. **The item's iron is a step lighter than the prop's** (`IIRON` in `gen.py`), because the prop's black iron disappeared into the bag's dark slot and the outline on its shaded side.
- **Linen satchel (2 × 2, worn on the back, portrait):** undyed linen in round 4's cloth tones (`#ece4ce` `#d8ceb2` `#bcb092` `#9a8e70`). It has a round-cornered body, a darker gusset on its shaded side, and a flap over three fifths of the bag with a curved lower edge, a dashed stitched hem, and a shadow line under it. A wooden toggle sits on a leather loop. A long shoulder strap is sewn to the gussets at the sides, with a brass buckle on its left run. The strap uses the item's own `#7a6a4a` as its base. The world picture is held by the top of the strap. The item has dye regions (Cloth, Strap); the manifest lists each region's key tones in case the pictures are ever recoloured. Today `dye.js` recolours only the worn figure.

## Weak points

- **The workbench's tools are small against its length.** At 1x it reads as a long bench with a vice and tools. The vice's tommy bar, the saw, and the chisels are the clearest; the plane is a brown block with a grey fleck, and the shavings read as pale litter rather than separate curls. The mirrored `x` facing puts its vice and front on the lit side, the cost the table and the bed accept.
- **The cupboard's front is on the shade face**, as the chest's and shelf's are, so its hinges and pulls are muted. I lifted them a step (lighter iron, longer straps) so they read at 1x. The mirror swaps the lit side, the cost round 4 accepted. The crock and jug on top are small, about 5 px, the same as the shelf's pots.
- **The cauldron is larger than `docs/29/09`'s "36 px" asks:** 45 × 46 including the stone ring, with the rim at 21.5 and the bail at 32.5. The stone ring reaches about 15 world units from the tile's centre, just inside the tile. The bail is a one-unit hoop, so at 1x it is a thin arc with outline on both sides.
- **The flames are cut against this drawing.** If the cauldron picture changes, the frames must be generated again with it, which `gen.py` does together. The flames at the far left and right overlap the side stones a pixel or two, as `fire_0`'s base does.
- **The satchel at 15 × 17 in the hand** is simplified: no buckle and no stitching at that size. Its toggle is two pixels.
- **Not drawn:** the cauldron hanging on a hook to sit in the fireplace's firebox (`docs/29/09` asks for it; the brief did not), a plain back for the cupboard facing away, and the satchel worn.
