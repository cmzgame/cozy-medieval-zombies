# Art round 10: cliffs (a cliff wall, a cliff top, a stone ramp)

Michael, 2026-10-28, on how the bounded world rises into hills (`docs/29-world/10`, PR #558): "Imagine the build tool allowing you to build a wall but instead of wood or stone it's bare grey cliff, rounds at the top corner, and meets up with a tile above to form a cliff face. These two tiles are really no different from a wall and a floor tile on the second floor (level 1)." And: "if you sent your artist a spec for a wall that is a cliff face and rounds into the tiles above, and a floor that meets the top of that wall tile cleanly, I could model it in game pretty fast. A drawn stairs with a flat stone ramp would make them climbable. When you do not want players to climb further, stop building stairs. None of these pieces would be interactable in any way."

So a hill is: **cliff walls** standing on the ground's tile edges (layer 0), 96 px tall like every wall (`docs/52`), and **cliff-top floor tiles** on layer 1 sitting on them, exactly as a second storey sits on a house's walls (`docs/49`); the inside of the hill is unlit ground nobody reaches. A **stone ramp** (a stair variant) climbs one storey. The engine draws all of it already; you draw the pictures.

## What to deliver, in your own folder only

`C:/Users/micha/AppData/Local/Temp/claude/C--Users-micha-Dev-cozy-medieval-zombies--claude-worktrees-79-sweep-3e-23fa08/b80e4332-b754-466b-9b55-c0810f052756/scratchpad/art10/cliffs/`: `gen.py` (Python 3, PIL and numpy, plain `python`, deterministic), `out/`, `preview.png`, `README.md`, `manifest.json`.

1. **Cliff wall** (`wall_cliff`): a wall material in **the same structure as `wall_stone.json`** (the same parts, names, sizes, and anchors, so wiring it is data): bare grey rock, weathered and stratified, a few cracks and ledges, no masonry joints; 96 px tall; its **top edge rounds over** (a convex lip, a few px of rounded rock bending back) so it meets the floor tile above it cleanly with no cap line; both faces (the camera sees the `n` and `w` edges' outer faces; draw the inner faces too, plain, as the stone wall does); **posts** for the vertices (outer corner, inner corner, and a run's end) that read as rock, rounded at the top the same way, never square masonry posts; a variant or two of the face so a long cliff doesn't repeat.
2. **Cliff top** (`floor_cliff_top`): a floor tile (64 by 32 diamond, as the floor definitions are) for layer 1 on a cliff: rock with sparse grass and a little scree, its picture meeting the cliff wall's rounded lip so the two read as one hillside edge where they touch; a grass-topped version too (most hills are grassy on top); edge treatments if the floor picture needs them where it meets the lip on the n and w sides (say how they'd be drawn: the engine draws an upper floor per chunk, so a lip on the floor's own edge may be better than on the wall: decide and say).
3. **Stone ramp** (`stair_ramp_stone`): a stair variant, three tiles climbing one storey (96 px) like the drawn stairs, but a **flat stone ramp** (a sloped rock slab, no treads, a few worn steps cut in it at most), its sides closed by rock that matches the cliff, its top meeting a cliff-top tile; facings **n and w only** (climbing away from the camera, as the map places stairs), in the shape of `stair_stone.json` (anchors, facings, footprint, and a `treads`-equivalent table of the ramp's surface height along its run so a figure's feet sit on it: the engine's rise is 32 px a tile).
