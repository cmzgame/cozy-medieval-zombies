## House rules for pixels

Hard pixels only (alpha 0 or 255), a 1 px `#2b2522` outline round each piece, light from the top left, stepped ramps of colour (3 to 5 tones per material), drawn at final size (no scaling), tiles 64 by 32 diamonds, a storey 96 px, the figure about 55 px. Stone is the village's stone wall material (the grey weathered masonry of `wall_stone`): match its palette, block size and joints exactly so new pieces sit beside the old without a seam; the cliff stone of round 10 is a different, rough rock and is NOT to be used for the castle pieces (round 11's ramp is the exception: it matches the cliffs). Everything deterministic: a `gen.py` (Python 3, PIL and numpy, plain `python`) that writes `out/*.png`, `preview.png`, `manifest.json`, `README.md`, all in your own folder only. Look at every output with the Read tool (zoom: render 3x previews) and iterate until it reads at a glance, as a person looking at a pixel-art game, not as a diagram. Michael is visual: your preview must show the pieces in place beside the existing stone wall, a figure for scale, at 1x and 3x. Michael's taste: chunky, clear, characterful stone; "detailed briefs get better art", so spend time on the stonework and silhouette, not on gradients.
