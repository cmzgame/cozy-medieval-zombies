# Art round 11: the Stone ramp with its own solid landing sides

The brief as given to the artist agent, 2026-10-29 (read `../ROUND-RULES.md` first: the shared rules, paths, and what every delivery reports). Output: `ramp/` (`gen.py`, `out/`, `preview.png`, `README.md`, `manifest.json`). Approved by Michael the same day and wired in `#569`.

**Task:** redraw the Stone ramp (`stair_ramp_stone`, art round 10) so that the ramp comes with its own solid sides for its top landing tile.

**What was wrong:** a stair is three tiles that climb one storey (96 px) plus a free fourth tile, the landing, which the build lays on the storey above (layer 1) at the stair's top, as the first step of the next floor. That landing floated: the ground tile under it (layer 0) is open ground, so you saw straight under it to a dark gap, and the ramp's closed rocky sides ended where the three climbing tiles ended. Michael, 2026-10-29, on a ramp built against the end of a cliff wall: "The top top that is visible above the gap IS the free tile you get at the top for stairs, but it does not come with build in sides for that tile. You can build a wall in those empty spaces, but it bulges out and rounds off. Which we should keep for possible future ramps with cliff sides, but a ramp should keep its whole solid shape. ... Our ramp needs to bring its own free next level floor tile side walls." And: "The ramp was probably drawn before we made the free next floor up tile decision, so it may just need to be redrawn." (Project Zomboid's stairs also carry their own first step, but are mostly indoors, walled on three sides; ours is outdoors and must be self-contained.)

**What to draw:** the ramp as one solid shape from foot to landing, for both facings the map places stairs in (`n` and `w`, climbing away from the camera): the three ramp tiles as before (a flat sloped rock slab, no treads, worn steps at most, sides closed by cliff-matching rock) and, at the top, the landing tile's body: a solid block of the same rock, its top the landing's floor surface at the storey above (flush with the cliff-top floor, 96 px), its lateral faces filled with rock all the way down so there is no opening under or beside the landing. **Square-edged** (unlike the cliff wall pieces, whose top edge rounds over): plain straight vertical faces with a flat top edge, the same silhouette as the ramp's own sides, only slightly weathered. The far end (where the climber walks on) is open. It must work as a T into a cliff wall's face and free-standing: the landing block's top height equals the cliff top's, and its side faces end flush at the landing tile's edges.

**Split of the delivery:** either one picture per facing for the whole ramp including the landing body, or the ramp as before plus separate landing-side pieces on the landing tile's lateral edges. The engine's builder had already wired a `wall_ramp_side` wall (a plain stone wall face, 96 px tall, 16 thick, anchors like `wall_stone`, frames `north_plain` and `west_plain`) on three edges of the ground under the landing; the artist took the second route and delivered exactly that piece plus a redrawn slab. Deliver the treads-equivalent table of the ramp's surface height along its run (rise 32 px a tile) and the landing's height.

**Preview:** both facings free-standing with the figure at the foot, halfway up, and on the landing; a T meeting (a straight cliff wall run ending at the ramp's landing, cliff top flush); the old ramp beside the new for comparison; at 1x and 3x. Say what is weak.
