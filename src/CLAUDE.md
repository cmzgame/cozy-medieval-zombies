# `src/`: the engine's rules and the code map

Read with the root `CLAUDE.md`. This file says what is, not when; the rulings behind each line are in `docs/02` by date.

## Rules the code keeps

- Tiles are 64×32. Art is drawn at whole pixels in world space; never scale in `drawImage`. Never draw UI into the canvas.
- The world fills the window at one of three zoom levels (`view.js` `ZOOM_LEVELS`), applied by the GPU; the interface has its own scale, capped at 2x. Nothing is laid out against 960×540. DOM over a world point goes through `view.toUi`.
- Nothing materialises in view: no chunk pops in and none of the dead arrive where a player can see (`docs/36`). The active region's boundary stays well beyond the viewport. The one exception is a natural resource's comeback (`docs/46`): an herb or a mushroom may appear in view, and a rock rises out of the ground, an arrival that is seen.
- The map is stored block by block (`map.js`); every tile access goes through `map.tileAt`. A missing chunk is grown on demand: plain floor inside the authored bounds, wild forest with a few of the dead beyond them. **Reading never grows a chunk:** growth is the simulation's ring round each player (`map.ensureAround`, four chunks) and the mutators' (`growTile`); a read beyond the ring sees the frozen blank tile; the renderer, the ground, the light, and sight never change the world, and the ground bakes a chunk only once its neighbours are grown (`check("growth")`).
- **No browser dialog:** the game never asks through `window.alert`, `confirm`, or `prompt` (a browser can be told to stop showing them, and the desktop app's pane answers `confirm` false unseen); every question is the game's own, asked on the page (`ui/screens.js` `ask`). `tools/check.js` scans for them (Michael, 2026-10-26).
- **The map is generated, never typed:** `python -I tools/mapgen/valley.py` writes `data/maps/valley.json` from a description per place. A floor definition's `walk` (false, or a speed factor) and `drink`, and a wall definition's `wall.seeThrough`, are how water, shallow water, fences, bars, and arrow slits work; nothing in code names them. A see-through wall bounds no room. A building's `wall` may be per side, `partitions` make rooms inside it, a `fence` feature walls a rect with gates (on some `sides` only, if it says); a water floor rect's `group` names the water ("river", "pond"; `map.waterBodyAt`, and `data/fishing.json` has a table for each name, which the validator enforces); an opening may be authored `open`, `broken`, or boarded (`boards: { in, out }`); a placeholder box definition carries a `label`, drawn on the box.
- **A change to anything on a chunk says so:** what a guest must see marks the chunk (`map.touch`, `markDirty`, or a mutator that calls them); what only the save keeps (a fuel between units, a timer, a stamp, health short of a break, a bag made on first use) calls `map.unsaved`. The save keeps each chunk's form until one of them drops it (`game.serialize`); `game.verifySaveCache` catches a miss (the server's log says THE SAVE'S CACHE MISSED); `node tools/savecache.js` proves the list.
- Rooms are found by flood fill, never authored. Walls are state on tile edges (`n` and `w` slots, Zomboid style), never objects; a door or a window is a wall with a kind and a state.
- The ground is one baked picture per chunk (`ground.js`, `docs/15`), drawn as one quad; nothing draws the floor per tile and nothing on the ground plane alternates by tile parity. A floor changes only through `map.setFloor`.
- Light is one function (`light.js`). Nothing else computes light. Light feeds sight. In the renderer every vertex carries the world point its light is read from; nothing is lit in screen space.
- The sway: the poke's stamp and the renderer's lean read the same clock, `loop.simTime()`; a wall-clock read on either side switches every wobble off. A stamp is none only when it is unset (`pokedAt == null`, `playedAt == null`): a stamp at sim 0 is a stamp. A sprite definition turns the sway off with `"sway": false` or replaces it with a `clickAnim` (the well).
- Drawn art comes in through sprite definitions (`data/sprites/*.json`, read by `assets.js`; `docs/17`): packed `images` with top-vertex and per-frame anchors, `drawings` with mirrors for facings (a mirrored facing's flame frames mirrored with it), two-tile furniture sliced per tile along a vertical screen line, a 2 by 2 piece (the fire pit) into three screen columns at its side tiles' centres, the left tile, the middle two drawn by the front one, and the right tile, each slice sorted by its own tile (`assets.sliceSheet`; a generated piece goes through the same cut); a crop's rows follow the furrows of its soil.
- The sim rolls only through `rng.js` (seeded from the map) and reads the clock only through `loop.simTime()`; a new `Math.random` or `performance.now` in a sim file is a bug. `game.js` reads no window and no DOM: the whole world runs headless under `tools/world.js` and hashes the same.
- **The draw is between steps** (`interp.js`, #161): the world steps at sixty a second, the screen draws as often as the display asks, and everything that moves is drawn blended by how far between steps the frame falls (one step, 16 ms, behind at most). A new thing that moves on the sim's step and is drawn from its `gx`, `gy` is in `interp.mark`'s list or it will judder on a 144 Hz display.
- Per-player state lives on the player, never in a module-level variable; the world holds a list of players and the dead hunt the nearest they see.

## The world and its state

- `game.js`: the world's state, `create` from a map file, the step order (`begin`, `step`), `serialize` and `restore` (the save and the join's format), `hashText`, `syncParts` (what `check("sync")` compares), `itemCensus` (every item in the world counted, each uid's places, #101: the sync parts `items` and `census`), `verifySaveCache`.
- `map.js` in layers (`docs/49` steps 0 and 1): every tile is on a layer (0 the ground, 1 and up the floors above, -1 a cellar; `STOREY` 96 px), and every read, mutator, collision test, `canStep` (`canStepOn(layer)` for the pathfinder), and room fill takes one, 0 by default; a prop and a bracket know their layer (`touchProp`, `markProp`, `unsavedProp` mark a prop's own); above the ground an unauthored tile is the void and below it the earth, the engine's own floors with the hole (`VOID`, `EARTH`, `HOLE`, never walked), each a frozen blank a read gets with no chunk grown; another layer grows only its authored chunks (`ensureAround`) and its built tiles (the mutators); a room is on one layer (its record's `layer`), earth bounding it and void entered; a tile address names its layer off the ground (`addr`, `layerOf`); the map file's building `floors` and `stairs` are laid by `loadMap`, a stair's landing floored and its stairwell void (a hole in the ground over a cellar's), the stairs and the building index kept as data (`stairs`, `buildings`, `buildingAt`, `stairProblemsOf`); a stair walked (step 3): `stairStep(body)` after every move sets its layer and `rise` (32 px a tile, the layer changing as it crosses the top edge), a stair's sides closed on both layers in `circleBlocked` and `canStep`, `stairAt`, `stairsOn`; the renderer's reads of one storey through `withDrawLayer` (never during a step). `render/storeys.js` draws the storeys (`withLift`, the floors' pictures, the dev-art stairs, the hiding rule, the fade of a storey you stand behind, `storeyCovers` and `faderFor`, drawn by the scene under `gl.setFade` into `gl.PASS_FADE`, `liftOf`); `entities/movement.js`'s `repath` walks a goal on another layer by the stair-first rule. Then: tiles, edge walls, collision, rooms, authoring (sets, features, scatter, wild), `propById`, `feedFire`, `setFireLit` and `setLantern` (a switch or a lantern on or off; a put-out emits `firePutOut`, never the burnt-out `fireOut`), growth and eviction (`evictFar`; `dropChunk` the one way a chunk leaves memory, the host's eviction and a guest told of one alike; `takeEvicted` the dropped chunks for the frame). A prop across a chunk's edge (#341) is recorded whole with its anchor's chunk, and each of its tiles on a neighbour is named by the prop's id in that neighbour's record (`parts`), which `installChunk` stands again from the registry, so a neighbour sent alone keeps it and the sync check's map part sees a tile that lost it; `placeProp` and `removeProp` mark every chunk the footprint stands on, so a neighbour is sent and never evicted; a prop leaving the registry with its chunk (replaced, dropped) leaves its neighbours' tiles too.
- Round rooms (`docs/50`; stone only for now, Michael 2026-10-27): `round.js` the ring's geometry, pure (a circle in a 5 or 7 square, `shape`: its stepped tiles, outline edges, four door points, the eight curve-crossed tiles, the body chords one per 15 degrees, the tiles held off by the curve); in `map.js` a ring is edge walls carrying `round: [x0, y0, n]` (a door at a door point `underRound`, a wall or an opening it took in `roundTaken`, released straight when it goes), so rooms, paths, sight, light, reach, and bites read it as any wall; its record (`ringById`, `allRings`, `ringAt`, `ringOfWall`) is made from its walls on host and guest alike (`loadMap` for the map's, `buildRing` and `removeRing`, `settleRings` on a chunk installed or grown), never saved apart, and from it the index on the tiles: the chords in `circleBlocked`, `curveCrossed` (no furniture: setdown, a carried prop, a made prop), `unreachableAt` (never walkable); `ringWallProblem` (a door or a window only at a door point, no wall inside), `ringProblem`, `ringFace` and a torch's `bracketPos` at its point on the curve; no painting on a ring, no fireplace against one. `render/rings.js` draws a ring as two drawables, its far half's inner face and its near half's outer face, a segment a plane (`depth.planeThrough`), the stone wall's face wrapped round; with the player in its room the near half's stretch over the room is cut away to one translucent face at the inner line, its flanks opaque and its ends closed by the wall's thickness, over one stone base with the inside skirt (`round.floorEnd`, `floorReach`); a door or a window at a door point is a hole in the curve filled from art round 9 (`data/sprites/round_openings_5`, `_7`: per point and kind the frame, the reveal, the shut, open, `open_a0` (the east door's, hinged to swing clear), and broken leaves or shutters, and the boards, each on its own depth plane, the face's tint baked in); `walls.js` draws no ring edge, nothing at a door point, and no post on a ring's outline.
- Cliffs (art round 10, Michael 2026-10-28: Free building's pieces, for experimenting; the bounded world of `docs/29-world/10` is not built): a cliff is a wall whose definition says `wall.cliff` (`wall_cliff`, `wall_cliff_grass`; `map.wallRule(...).cliff`), on a layer's tile edges, blocking bodies and sight as stone; no door or window stamped in it (`canStampOver`, placement's "Not in a cliff"), no painting or torch on it (`paintings.segmentProblem`, "Not on a cliff"), taken apart only while Free building is on; its plateau is the tiles floored on the layer above (a cliff top, `floor_cliff_top`, `floor_cliff_grass`, laid as any built floor). `cliffs.js`, pure: `cornerBase`/`cornerAt` (a vertex where exactly one n and one w cliff edge meet with one plateau quadrant, an outer corner, or three, an inner one, named as the artist's README; `_hn`, `_hw`, `_hnw` where the corner at an edge's other end claims it too), `claimedByCorner`, `buried` (a cliff edge with plateau on both sides, a hill widened past it: drawn as nothing and no edge at a vertex), `edgeFrame` (a face variant by the edge's hash, or `back[side]` when its own tile is plateau). `render/walls.js` draws them: the face cut along its edge line at the top under its plateau's floor (the lip is the wall's, no cap), a back on its far face's surface, a corner in place of both edges and the post, in parts each on its edge's surface (`depth.wallSurface`, a halved far end's ridge at the midpoint). The stone ramp is a stair of material `ramp_stone` (`map.stairKind`: a material is wood, stone, or one whose `stair_<material>` definition exists; a `ramp` table makes it a ramp, its `landing` the floor its landing gets), walked at 32 px a tile with no treads (`map.riseOn`), drawn by `render/storeys.js` `emitRamp` (the picture on its far side's plane, the slab's top on one sloped plane, the near side's triangle on its own).
- `world.js`: the world's time scale (1 awake, `sleepTimeScale` from `data/tuning.json` while every living player sleeps; `game.timeScaleNow`) and the id counter every prop and entry takes its instance id from.
- `interp.js`: drawn positions between steps (#161): each step marks where every character and the camera stood (`mark`), and `render` puts them `loop.alpha()` of the way to where the step left them (`apply`) and takes it off again in a `finally` (`restore`); nothing in the simulation or the interface logic ever reads a blended position; a jump over `feel.blendJump` tiles is not blended; arrows and the fireball are marked too, with their height, and one loosed during a step is marked from its launch point after the step (`adopt`, #250), so its first frames draw it leaving the bow.
- `visibility.js` line of sight · `sight.js` how much of the dead in view a player sees in the dark (the client's own, from its own light; `docs/07`) · `light.js` the one light function, its per-chunk cache, portals, and the flicker (a flame's pool, and a lit room's drawn light dipping with its fire's flicker to `world.litRoomFlickerMin`, `roomFlickerWeight`, the renderer's alone: the world's lit room stays 1, which the dead's sight reads) · `clock.js` the day · `rules.js` the sim's rules (a snapshot of the settings for a browser's world, the save's for a server world; carried in the save since version 2) · `tuning.js` the entities' numbers from `data/tuning.json` · `rng.js` the one seeded generator · `events.js` the event bus · `iso.js` the projection · `pathfind.js` the A* search, its working memory typed arrays kept between searches (a search inside another's `canStep` gets its own; #124).
- `reach.js`: the one reach test for every player action (#208): distance and a clear line of sight to the nearest tile (`reachProblem`, `canReach`); a wall or a shut door or shutter stops it, a fence or an open window does not (a sealed room is a cell, #147). - `bucket.js`: the bucket item (`vessel` in its definition, its water the entry's `fill`): the verbs `fill` (at water) and `pour` (on a plot, 3 by 3 through `farming.soak`, any crop wet or dry, as the skin's Water) and the menu's entries for a tile, #199; its pour's label `POUR_LABEL`, "Water with the bucket", which a crop's menu offers first, before "Water with the waterskin" (each vessel its own entry, Michael's playtest 2026-10-26; E never waters); `pourskin` (Michael, 2026-10-26, The playtest's rulings, #506), the Use row of a bucket on a waterskin, by the bucket's uid and the skin's (both carried): as much as the skin takes, its water the mix's (`water.mixInto`), tainted into a skin holding clean water refused, `water.WOULD_TAINT`, "That would taint the clean water."

- **The frame sorts per pixel** (`docs/51`, `08`): a depth buffer, each vertex's depth (X + Y + Z toward the camera) computed on the CPU from the depth surface its drawable set from true geometry (`render/depth.js`: a wall's near face with its camera-side end on the end's own plane, a post flush with the thinnest wall it joins and a little behind, a prop's two front faces cut at its front corner and held off a wall on its tile's back edges, a canopy's or a figure's billboard through its front, a step nearer for each tile it stands across its diagonal (`depth.across`), so two on one diagonal never tie; the centre of the range moves in whole steps, so a tie never flips as the camera moves); the flat things first with no depth, then the opaque pass with the alpha test at a half, then the translucent (a colour under full alpha, an occluder, a soft sheet's quad: `sheet.soft`, set for the generators that paint shadows and glows), then the ghosts, the hit shapes, and the marker on top. A new drawable sets its surface (`gl.surface`, with its kind's bias and room) as the first thing its emit does, or it inherits the last one's; a thing walked through is a billboard through its tile's centre, a floor item's block one through its own foot; a new soft generator joins `assets.js` `SOFT_KINDS`; a quad that must draw a step behind the quad it outlines is emitted under `gl.setBehind`; a drawable's layers fit its kind's room (`gl.layer`, `gl.holdOrder` for quads that never overlap; at 16 bits a step is a quarter of a unit). The kinds' biases and rooms are two tables (`scene.js` `DEPTH_BIAS`, `DEPTH_ROOM`).
- `gl.js` the WebGL2 batch in five passes (the fifth a faded storey's, `PASS_FADE`, docs/49 section 6), the depth surfaces and the emit order, the atlas (pages of one `sampler2DArray`, opened when a sheet fits none; `frameUV` carries the page, #353, and the soft flag), the area textures · `render.js` a hub over `render/` (`scene.js` the frame, the night pool, fog, occlusion holes, the depth biases, the probe `check("depth")` drives; `depth.js` the depth surfaces; `area.js` the light textures; `walls.js` drawn walls, caps redrawn on the sky, posts, boards, leaves; `props.js` props and their animations, the ghost; `bees.js` the bees round a hive in sight by day, cosmetic, from `loop.simTime()` and the prop's `honeyAt`, #441; `entities.js`) · `ground.js` the ground baked per chunk · `assets.js` the sprite loader (drawn sheets, furniture slicing: two tiles, and a 2 by 2's three columns, `sliceSheet`, `maskSlice`; facings; a sheet baked on first sight; a sliced sheet answers for any tile count) · `sprites.js` facings and frame names · `render-util.js` shading · `placeholders.js` the openings' heights from the floor (`DOOR_TOP` 64, `WINDOW_SILL` 24, `WINDOW_TOP` 64, `BRACKET_UP` 48: the walls 96 px tall, `docs/52`; the swung leaves, the faces, and the flights read them) and the dev art generators (an animal's sheet too, `generateAnimal`, from its definition's own animations and facings) · `camera.js` · `view.js` the three zooms and `toUi`.
- `figure.js` a hub over `figure/`: `rig.js` the body's numbers (`BODY`) and the poses, `raster.js`, `draw.js` the garments and the sheets, `held.js`, `wardrobe.js` (`docs/12`, `32`; a body number is tried from the console before it is ruled, `docs/testing.md`). - `handed.js`: right- or left-handed, a leaf (`handedOf`: "left" only when it says so; `handSideOf(who, role)`: which side the main or off hand is; `handLabel`: the character screen's "Main hand (right)"). ## Multiplayer

- `netsmooth.js`: remote bodies on a guest drawn between the host's frames (#248): the last few positions with the host's sim time, drawn `feel.netDelaySteps` steps behind the newest frame, interpolated; `netTo` stays the host's truth; `drawClock` is the host time they are drawn at, which a guest's arrows are stepped on too (#250), held within two steps of the newest frame, so a stall in the host's sim (a hit-stop, a slow frame) does not run it ahead. `tools/netsmooth.js` measures it against the old walk-to-each-frame, and a guest's arrows against the old frame-by-frame replica (`measureArrows`).
- `net.js` the wire (`docs/31-multiplayer/04` status, `09`): the join a load, then frames every third step at normal speed (`FRAME_EVERY`); a player's record in light and heavy parts, the dead, the villagers, the corpses, and the animals (a lean record of their own, `animalRecord`; the homes' renewal in `animalHomes` when it changes) as deltas with a roster when membership changes; the chunks the host dropped (`evicted`, a guest drops them too; a guest back from a step away keeps its away screen, and predicts nothing, until a frame carries its player and the ring round it, `returning`); a guest's own player runs the controller in predict mode and the hit comes from the host; a guest's inputs as runs while the world runs fast; the flights (arrows, thrown things, spells) as each one's launch once and its end once (`arrows: { go, end }`, `archery.launchRecord`, `endRecord`; #250), which a guest steps itself; `PROTOCOL` (24: a flight's layer, docs/49 steps 2 and 3; 23 every sent tile address names its layer off the ground and a player's `layer` is in the light record, docs/49 step 1; 22 the `place` command's `torch` kind, #523), the browser id in the join, the owner key (`setOwnerKey`), the host secret (`setHostSecret`), `setSyncReports` and `setTestSnapshot` for `check("sync")`, `status().wire` for a frame's bytes. The default wire is the Fly relay (`RELAY_URL`, `wss://cmz-relay.fly.dev`); `?relay=none` is the loopback of one browser's tabs, `?relay=ws://localhost:8012` a local relay.
- `host.js` the authority's half, shared by the browser host and the server world: joins and whose character is whose (a join over the cap, `playerCapBrowser` or `playerCapServer` in `data/tuning.json`, is refused with `full` before anything is made or taken from the save), dormancy (a guest away sixty seconds of game time goes dormant into the save and comes back on the rejoin), stepping away (a player idle five minutes in a shared world), the world's events answered for the players they are for, the routing of a message or a log line to the acting guest; `MAX_OWNERS`.
