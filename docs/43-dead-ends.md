## 2. Pick up and Take apart (step 1)

One rule for every placed thing (Zomboid's distinction):
- **Pick up** keeps the thing whole: as an item in the bag (a campfire, a rug, a painting) or **carried overhead** (furniture, as today). **Carried overhead, a container keeps what it holds,** as a full chest, barrel, or wheelbarrow does today (`interact.js` `carryAction`): the wheelbarrow is the hauling answer (2026-10-24). **Pick up into the bag** is refused while the thing holds anything ("Empty it first.").
- **Take apart** destroys the thing and gives back **half its materials, rounded down**, and needs the tool its recipe needs (a hammer for furniture, as building). It is refused while the thing holds anything, so nothing is lost by accident. *Default (Primary's, keeping 2026-10-15, "X removes only what players made"):* **only what a player made** can be taken apart, by the `made` mark a built wall or a station-made prop already carries; the village's own furniture can be carried, never taken apart. Refusing while it holds anything overturns that ruling's "a removed station spilling its output too".
- **The build palette's Remove becomes Take apart**, under the one word and the one rule: half back (*default*; today Remove gives nothing back and drops a container's contents on the floor).
- **The village's one-of-a-kind stations** (section 5) have neither while the switch is off.

**The guest's side:** both are menu labels on a prop's address (or an item's uid on its tile), performed by the host; the materials go to the actor's bag or the floor at their feet (`map.dropItem`); the made mark rides on the prop in its chunk, and `game.syncParts` already compares props. `check("build")` and `check("slots")` grow rows; the wiki's Building and Furniture pages.

## 3. Fires and lights (step 2)

- **The oven cooks** the fire's six recipes as well as its own (ruled; a bug stops it: `src/stations.js` `stationOf` returns the oven's own station before it looks at `fire.cooks`). - **A lantern goes out.** The ruling that the village's lanterns never go out (2026-10-17) is struck: light will draw the dead (not built yet), so a light must be snuffable. A lantern on a post, or in a bracket on a wall (the 33 wall lanterns among them), offers **Put out**, and is lit again with a tinderbox or a lit torch, as a torch is. The dead tinderbox row on `lantern_post` (`data/interactions.json`) becomes the real Light.
- **"Put out" everywhere**, never "douse": a lantern, a torch (the T key's "You douse the torch." and the controls screen's line, `src/ui/screens.js`, become "put out"), a campfire, a fireplace; `docs/10` says so too.
- **Fixed home lights are switches; portable lights cost fuel** (Michael: lighting a house is "one of the least fun tasks"). **A fireplace, a hearth, and a lantern turn on and off and burn no fuel.** Turning one on still needs a flame carried, a tinderbox or a lit torch (Michael's #168: lighting from a menu needs a flame); turning it off needs nothing. A torch burns down and a campfire takes logs: carrying light or fire away from home is what fuel pays for. **The stations' fires keep their fuel** (*default*): the oven and the clamp burn logs and the forge charcoal, as `docs/30` law 4 has it for a station, because a station's fire is work, not comfort. The cost a lit home keeps is the light itself, once the dead are drawn to it.
- **The campfire**, built from the build palette (`N`): **three stone and two logs**, on open ground outdoors only, no skill; the two logs are its starting fuel. Building a prop from the palette is new (`data/build.json` holds walls, doors, windows, fences, and gates today): the palette gains a prop kind with its cost and its rule for where it may stand. It holds **six logs**, each burning **three game hours** (a full one burns eighteen). A log is added from the menu or by Use; **adding a log to a cold campfire does not light it** (#168: a flame is needed), adding one to a lit one keeps it going. **Put out** keeps the fuel left. **Pick up** only when it is out **and empty** (no log tricks): it becomes a **Campfire** item, **5 by 5 cells** (too big for the sack, a satchel's fill, comfortable in a rucksack), set down again with the ghost. **Take apart** gives back half its stones, rounded down (one); the logs were fuel, not materials. One is placed at the charcoal burners, and one at the woodcutters' camp when it is built.

**The guest's side:** every verb above (Light, Put out, Add a log, Pick up, Take apart, build) is a verb in `verbs.js` or a menu label performed by the host, the target by prop id or a wall's address; a prop fire's `lit` and fuel ride in the chunk and the frame (`map.burnFires`), a bracket's lantern on its wall (`touchWall`); a placed campfire goes through `placement.performPlacement`; the switch state is the fire's `lit`, already compared by `game.syncParts`. `check("kindle")`, `check("torch")`, `check("lantern")`, `check("flare")` grow rows; the wiki's Fire, Campfire, Lantern, Fireplace, Oven, Torch pages.

## 6. Repairs, the sawpit, and honey (step 5)

- **One repair count per item, on the entry**, for the weapons and tools that can be made (`docs/39`, Blacksmithing 4); a boss's drop is not repaired. Every repair, anywhere, lowers the ceiling of the next; skills raise the ceilings; every item still steps down to breaking (Michael: "each does inevitably decay down the ladder to death").
- **The grindstone** sharpens an edged weapon for free, a few seconds' timed action (Blacksmithing 2): up to **60%** of its condition the first time, **40%** the second, **20%** the third, then no more.
- **The anvil** repairs (Blacksmithing 4): up to **100%**, then **80%**, then **60%**, on the same count; *default:* one unit of the item's main material each time (an iron bar for iron, a plank for wood).
- **The sawpit** cuts a log into planks with no hatchet (Woodcutting 3).
- **The beehives** give **honey** every few game days (Beastlore 5); honey is food that never rots (2026-10-23) and pemmican's binder.

**The guest's side:** each is a verb on a prop id, the entry by its uid; the count and the wear ride on the entry in the save and the player's record; **`game.syncParts`' entry text gains wear and the repair count** (it compares neither today); the hive's next day rides on the prop as an apple tree's does (`fruit.js`). `check("wear")` grows rows and `check("apples")`' pattern serves the hive; the wiki's Durability, Grindstone, Anvil, Honey pages.

## 8. The dye vat (step 7)

- **All three vats in the weaver's yard work** (friends dye side by side). **Unlocked at Crafting 3**, as a reward for working on a skill.
- **The picker is a stripped-down character creation screen:** the figure turning, a swatch row for each colour region of the garment, Apply. A broader curated palette than three colours.
- **Each item declares what it may be dyed:** a `dye` block in `data/items.json`, naming its regions and the colours or **schemes** allowed for them (a chest piece of black and brown may also be red and black: a new scheme is one line of data, no new art). A single-colour garment is one region.
- **For the artists:** a dyeable garment is painted with **one fixed pair of key tones per region** plus the outline; the game recolours each region by replacing its tones (`docs/28`'s "colour is data, not art", extended to several regions). Today only hair, beards, and head items are recoloured when painted; the build extends it to every painted slot. Art painted in fixed colours before this needs repainting into key tones to dye; the generated placeholder garments recolour already.
- **Primary's reading, Michael's to overrule:** the picker is built now with a cost hook; whether a dye costs a plant (woad, madder, weld, with foraging and Herbalism, #288) or nothing is open, and the vats need no plant until #288 lands.

**The guest's side:** Apply is a command by the entry's uid and the chosen scheme; the entry's `colours` already ride on a worn item to every client (the dead's dyed hoods); **`game.syncParts`' entry text gains `colours`**. `check("hovercard")`, `check("layers")`; the wiki's Dye vat, Clothing pages.
