# 08 — Milestone 2b: the WebGL renderer, portals, and light per pixel

**Status, 2026-10-27: built (all three phases on 2026-10-09; the Canvas 2D renderer deleted; the depth buffer, `51`, on 2026-10-27, #530); a reference.** The code is the truth: `src/gl.js` (the WebGL2 core) and `src/render.js`, a hub over `src/render/`. As built: one atlas of every sheet, in pages of one array texture (`23`, Performance; #353), drawn in four passes of one draw call each; the frame sorted per pixel by a depth buffer (below, and `51`); the ground one baked quad per chunk (`15`); the world's scale is the zoom, three levels the GPU applies (1.1, 1.8, 2.7; `src/view.js`), not an integer display scale in CSS; the light, ambient, and fog textures cover as many chunks as the window needs (`render/area.js`); the see-through holes are cut in tree canopies only (2026-10-11); the debug views (light, ambient, fog) are on `L`; the fog texture is the local player's sight, each client drawing its own (`31`). The phases, their acceptance, and the parity notes are `history/08-renderer-build.md`.

## The idea

Today the night layer is painted in screen space and then patched so that each thing shows its own light. In WebGL every vertex carries the world position its light should be read from, and the fragment shader samples the light there. A floor pixel reads the light at its own world point, so pools are per pixel. A standing thing reads the light at its foot, as now. A wall face reads the light along its foot line and fades it with height, so a pool climbs the wall it touches. Flames and the HUD-less debug marks read no light at all. Fog is the same mechanism with a second, one-texel-per-tile texture of what the player can see: the floor reads it per pixel, a sprite at its foot. The fog layer, the night layer, the mask canvases, and the ordered pass all go.

## The frame: depth and the passes

Since 2026-10-27 (`51`) the frame sorts per pixel. Every vertex carries a depth of its own, the style bible's X + Y + Z toward the camera (a tile 32 units, a pixel of height one), computed on the CPU from true geometry and stored less a centre that follows the camera in whole steps of 256 units (so a depth on a wall or a prop is a multiple of 1/32 less a multiple of 256, which a float holds exactly, and walking never flips a tie between two quads on one plane: Michael's playtest, 2026-10-27, the side walls flickered as he walked); the clip z is that over 4096, so the range is +-4096 units round the camera and follows it (never the chunked light area). `a_world` and `a_height` stay the light's. Each drawable sets a depth surface before it emits (`src/render/depth.js`): a wall its true near face and, from just short of its camera-side end (a north wall's east end, a west wall's south end), the end's own face, a ridge (`wallSurface`: a segment's picture reaches past its end, and on the face's plane those pixels tied with the next segment's face, so the joints of a run showed one segment through the next and flickered; on the end's plane they lie behind it; where a drawn post stands at that end the ridge moves back by the post's reach, at a plain joint by two units, under the next segment's first column) (its cap, boards, shutters, hangings, bracket, and flame on that surface; an open door's leaf on its own plane through the hinge); a post the two faces of its square, flush with the faces of the thinnest wall it joins (proud, it drew a light line down a room's far corner and a stone post stood over the timber beginning at it); a prop's slice and a pile of floor items the two front faces of its footprint tile, a ridge cut at the front corner's column (`gl.js` cuts each quad there), held at the near face of a wall on its tile's north or west edge where a face would pass into it (`againstWalls`: the depth the most of the ridge and that wall's plane, so a table's top against a wall is never cut by it); a tree or any canopy a billboard through that corner; a figure a billboard through the front of its body, (gx + 0.3, gy + 0.3); an arrow in flight a billboard through its ground point. Each quad a drawable emits sits a small step nearer than the one before (two steps of the buffer at least, sized from `DEPTH_BITS`), so a drawable's own layering stands; an outline's copies (Object Highlight, the swing outline, an item's halo) sit a step behind their base; and the kinds have biases of their own (`scene.js` `DEPTH_BIAS`: floor items 0.5, posts -0.5, a little behind the wall faces they are flush with, so a segment that begins at a post covers it as the sort drew it, props 1, figures 2, a burning body's particles 1 more). The passes, drawn in order: **flat** (the ground, the build grid, rugs, the tile mark, tracks, lying arrows and thrown things, arrows' shadows; no depth tested or written), **opaque** (every standing quad, `LEQUAL`, written, alpha under a half discarded), **translucent** (tested, not written, in the sort's order: a quad whose colour alpha is under 1, an occluder canopy with its holes, or one from a soft generated sheet, `sheet.soft`, per quad), and **on top** (the placement and remove ghosts, the hit shapes, the landing marker; no test). The depth mask is set before every clear; `setup()` sets the test, `LEQUAL`, and the range, so a restored context has them. The key sort (`(tx + ty) * 1000` plus an offset by kind) still orders the translucent pass and every tie; a figure's key is where it stands (`besideWalls` went once `check("depth")` rows 1 to 4 passed, `51` section 6). `check("depth")` is the proof; `scene.probe` is its hook (a drawable left out, a field of the scene replaced, the drawables it names drawn in one flat colour, `gl.setMark`, so a thing's pixels are found by drawing it in two), null in play.

## Portals and transmission

- **Transmission.** `transmission(wall)` returns 0..1: a plain wall and a closed door 0, an open shutter and an open doorway 1, a closed shutter `tunables.shutterLeak` (0.05 to start), later glass most and a curtain little. `blocksSight` becomes `transmission < 0.5`. The light ray multiplies through what it crosses and stops at 0; sight is unchanged.
- **Portals as emitters.** In `light.update`, for every opening of every room (the rooms already list them): take the ambient on each side, room or outdoors; if they differ by more than a little, add a source 0.3 tiles off the edge on the darker side, intensity `(bright - dark) × transmission`, radius by kind (`door` 3, `window` 2.5), directional: it lights only the darker side. At night a lit room's open window and door glow outward; by day they pool daylight inward. Sources carry an intensity now, where before every source was 1.
- **The unlit room by day** keeps a reduced version of the openness term as a floor (`tunables.roomBounce`, 0.5 to start) and the portals pool the rest, so a cottage with its door open at noon is bright by the door and dim at the back. Tuned by eye; `roomBounce` 1 gives today's flat room.

## What this must not foreclose

- **Roofs**: a coverage value per tile, separate from whether a roof is drawn, is one more small texture read by the ambient rule.
- **Upper floors**: every lookup and cache already takes a layer; a second layer is a second set of textures drawn in order.
- **Building**: nothing here knows a building; walls are edge state, rooms are found, portals are derived from openings each step, so a wall placed in play lights correctly on the next step.
- **Zoom**: the batch is in screen pixels and the display scale is a uniform; a zoom-out is a different scale.
- **Real art**: a sheet with an image instead of a placeholder uploads the same way.
