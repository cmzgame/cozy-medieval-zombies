## What can be carried

A rule, not a list (Michael, 2026-10-11: "extend it to other items that can be picked up as a rule"). `interact.canCarry(prop)`: a prop can be lifted unless it is **rooted** (a tree, the well), **built in** (a fireplace, a fire pit, a torch post), or **a fire that burns fuel** (the campfire). Everything else can: the bed, table, chest, barrel, and the jack-o'-lantern. A sprite's own `carry` flag, true or false, overrides the rule. A lit prop goes dark while carried (it leaves the fire set) and lights again when set down. The campfire is held fixed for now because a burning fire has no good answer to being carried; flip it with `"carry": true` in `data/sprites/campfire.json`.

## Doors, windows, and shutters are built onto walls (Michael, 2026-10-17, #147)

"Make all walls solid as built, and then you can build a window or door directly over top and it stamps it into place and creates the opening" (Michael's thought, left to the build). Walls are edge state with a kind already, so **a door or a window is built over a plain wall edge** (`map.canStampOver`): the edge's kind and state are set, the new one is **of the wall's material** (a door in a wood wall is a wood door, a door in a stone wall a stone door), it remembers the wall it replaced and whether the player built that wall (`wall.under`, `wall.underBuilt`; a new door over a broken stamped one keeps both), and **the removal tool takes it out and the wall is back, as it was, never a hole** (`destroyWall(..., restoreUnder)`; the player's own wall is still theirs to remove). A gate (a door) is built over a fence or bars too; a window is not. **Over a broken door or window of its own kind a new one is built**, whole, shut, at full health, never a repair ("everything spent, nothing repaired"): this is how shutters are rebuilt, the window being the shutters' wall. A whole door or window is not built over, and a door still needs both its tiles walkable and clear (`09`). The cost and time are `data/build.json`'s; the build is the one path for the host and a guest (`placement.performPlacement`, a `build` command by the edge's address); rooms are found again and a torch bracket on a replaced wall is forgotten.

**Every entrance breaks** (Michael, 2026-10-17: "every entrance that you can use MUST be usable by zombies, and if something gates it it must be breakable by zombies. This stops a fortress."): a door and a window always have hit points, whatever they are made of (`createWall`; only a plain wall has none), so a door built in a stone wall is a stone door the dead can break, and no build can make an entrance they cannot. Placement therefore asks nothing more of a door than a wall does: a room with no door at all is a cell ("You just can't leave") and is allowed, and a door, a gate, or boards are always fine.

## Take apart (the removal tool)

The removal tool (Michael, 2026-10-11: "delete anything placed or built, highlight green, click to destroy"), since `docs/43` section 2 (2026-10-25) **Take apart**, under the one word and the one rule the made prop's own menu has (`placement.takeApart`). **X** enters it, or the Take apart row on the build palette; X, N, or Escape leaves it. Moving the cursor highlights the front-most thing under it that the player made or built, in the ghost's green, and the prompt says what comes back ("Click to take apart door (1 plank back)"); red with the refusal ("Too far", "Empty it first.", the tool) otherwise; a click takes it apart instantly and the tool stays on.

- **What can be removed** (Michael, 2026-10-15: what players made, never the village's own things): a prop made at a station (`made`, set when a station delivers it) and a wall, door, or window with `built` set (`placement.js`, `removalTarget`). Trees, the well, the village's fireplaces, the campfire, torch posts, and the cottage's own walls never highlight, so the tool is quiet over a forest.
- **What it does:** it gives back **half the thing's materials, rounded down**: a prop's recipe (`data/recipes.json`, the row whose output is the prop; an ingredient named by a tag, the bed's cloth, gives nothing back), a wall's build entry (`data/build.json`), so a door gives one plank of three and a one-plank wall nothing. They go to the player's bag, and what does not fit to the floor at their feet. **A thing holding anything is refused** ("Empty it first."; a queue still running: "It is still making something."), so nothing is lost by accident (overturning 2026-10-15's spill). **A wall built while Free building was on** carries `free` and gives nothing back. A wall is taken off its edge with its boards and bracket, and sight, light, rooms, and pathing update at once.
- **The menu:** a made prop's own menu offers **Take apart** too, its label naming what comes back ("Take apart (2 planks back)") or why not ("Take apart (empty it first)"); a guest's command by that label runs the same rule.
- **Finding the target:** a prop by its tile or its drawn rectangle; a wall by its drawn face on screen, or by the ground edge the building tool would pick, a little behind a face hit. The front-most (highest tile sum) wins.
- Hands full means no removal, like building.
- **Tools** (the tool rule, #214, #215): Take apart needs the tool the thing's recipe or build asks for, carried (hand, belt, or bag): a smith's hammer for furniture, a door, or a window, a hatchet for a log wall (`data/build.json` `tool`, a recipe's `uses`, a tool verb: `build`, `notch`). The shortfall line names it ("You need a smith's hammer.").

## Woods (#334)

Every wood and daub is a wall type of its own (`docs/02`, 2026-10-20), and a door or a window may be of another wood than its wall. The palette has a row for a log wall and one for each wall wood (Oak, Spruce, Mahogany, Daub; Winter Pine and Bloodbark only while Free building is on, the regions' woods), each one plank, and one row each for a door and a window; while a door or a window is the mode, **R cycles the wood of its leaves** (the wall's own, oak, spruce, mahogany, and with Free building the regions' two), shown under its row and in the prompt, and the ghost shows it. The choice is the `leaf` of the `build` command, checked by the host as its own click is: only a wall of a wood takes another wood's leaves, and a region's wood needs Free building. The code is `src/woods.js`; the rulings and the readings are `docs/02`, 2026-10-20.

## The palette and its tabs (Michael, 2026-10-28, `02` "The build menu is two tabs, Build and Terrain")

`src/ui/build-palette.js`, opened with N. **Two tabs.** An entry's `tab` in `data/build.json` names its tab, `build` when it names none (`placement.TABS`, `tabOf`, `paletteTabs`; nothing is chosen by an entry's id). **Build** holds the walls and openings, the props, and the floors and stairs, each under a small heading (an entry's `group`), and Take apart. **Terrain** holds the ground's surfaces (Grass, Dirt, Cobblestone, Random spawn), the water (Shallow water, Water), and the landforms (the cliff pieces: Cliff wall, Grassy cliff wall, Cliff top, Grassy cliff top, Stone ramp). Every Terrain entry is `dev`, so the Terrain tab is offered only while Free building is on, and with it off the palette is the Build tab alone with no tab bar. The last tab picked is kept for the session, also while Terrain is not on offer (Free building off shows Build and Terrain returns with it); a terrain row shows its floor's colour (a diamond) where a build row shows its cost.

**The height cap.** The palette sits under the HUD and over the log (`max-height: calc(100% - 124px - 156px)`); the title, the tab bar, Take apart, and the hint stay where they are and only the list scrolls (a thin scrollbar, `overscroll-behavior: contain`; the wheel is read on the canvas alone, so it scrolls the list and never zooms the world). The list's scroll is kept across the per-step rebuild and per tab; a picked row is scrolled into view when the palette opens again. In a short window (the UI under 560 px tall) it moves to the right of the HUD and gives up its title and hint so the list keeps room. N and Esc close it; no key switches tabs (`[` and `]` are the host's clock, the arrows and A and D walk).

**Terrain paints the ground** (`kind: "terrain"`; `placement.js` `terrainPlan`, `map.paintGround`). A terrain entry's `sprite` is the floor it paints. A drag paints the rectangle from the press to the release (at most 24 tiles a side, `TERRAIN_DRAG`, and within the entry's `reach`, 12 tiles, by a clear line), each tile its own paint with its own refusal and the ghost green or red with the reason; instant (`seconds` 0), costing nothing, **Free building only**, the ground only ("Terrain is the ground's" upstairs). It is the tilling kind of change: the tile's floor is replaced outright and **nothing is kept**, no `built` record and nothing to take apart, so the ground's bake blends it into its neighbours by the floor's id exactly as an authored one (`data/ground.json` `floors`): dirt and cobble ragged into grass, deep water ragged with a dirt shore as the river's, a shallow tile and the pink a hard diamond like the river's ford. Refused: over a laid floor ("A floor is laid there"), a stair or its landing ("Not over a stair"), crops ("Crops grow there"), a tile already that ground ("That is already dirt ground", or an entry's own `already`); and water of either kind over a prop, a rug, an item, a wall edge ("Not under something"), a body ("Someone is in the way"), or a corpse ("A body lies there": its loot must not lie on a tile no one walks). An entry without `dev` is no terrain at all (`isTerrainBuild`, and `validate-data.py` refuses it), so one can never be painted with Free building off. Tilled soil is made by tilling and is not here. A guest paints through the same `build` command (`tx`, `ty`, `tx2`, `ty2`); the host checks its Free building, its reach, the cap, and the refusals, and the change reaches the guest in its chunk's record like any floor (the chunk is marked, so a wild chunk with a painted tile is never evicted).

**Random spawn** (`floor_randomspawn`, `data/sprites/floor_randomspawn.json`; first "No random spawn", swapped by Michael the same day, `02`): a bubblegum pink flat floor (`#ff5fb4`), obvious by day and by night, walked as ground (no `walk` field), in the chart in the same pink (`mapColor`). **It does nothing yet**: it tags a tile, and nothing reads it. It is for building the map by hand: it marks where random things (trees, the dead, whatever the map's scatter and spawn rules later place) may spawn, a rule not yet written (Michael's direction, `02`); where the bounded map's dead come from may be this tile or a square of its own, not decided. A saved chunk that names the old id `floor_nospawn` loads as this one (`map.js` `RENAMED_FLOORS`).

## Free building

The world rule `freeBuilding` (`rules.js`, default off; a host's rules bind its guests, and the settings screen's row is what a browser's own worlds take as their rule): while on, every build costs nothing, the palette shows "free", and nothing is taken from the bag. Build time still applies. It is for testing structures; turn it off to test the costs. **It waives the build tools too** (the hammer for a door, a window, and Take apart; the hatchet for a log wall; #215), **but not boarding up or pulling a board**: Michael's 2026-10-11 ruling left boards out of it ("planks for boards are untouched"), and they stay a hammer's work.

## Accepted, and what is left for later

Michael, 2026-10-11, after building a one-tile house for himself and the jack-o'-lantern: "the fundamentals are here." Left for later, so nothing here forecloses them:

- **Stone.** No stone item, so no stone wall. When stone is gathered (a quarry, rubble), `data/build.json` takes a `stone_wall` row with `wall_stone` as its sheet and a great cost in stone (Michael, 2026-10-17: "costs shitload of stone"); a door or window built in it still breaks (above), and nothing else changes.
- **Tear-down of authored walls.** The removal tool only takes walls with `built` set. One line (`wall.built` ignored in `removalTarget`) opens the village's own walls to it; Michael has not asked.
- **Refunds.** Removal gives nothing back. Salvage (a log back from a log wall) is a cost table away.
- **The campfire** cannot be carried while lit or at all; `"carry": true` in its sprite file would allow it once a carried fire has an answer.
- **An in-game building editor.** Placement is its seed: a ghost, a validity rule, a palette. Floors, props from inventory, and rotation of real furniture art would grow from here. Not scheduled.
- **Placing items from the bag** (dropping a chest you carry in the bag as a prop) is not placement; the bag's Drop puts items on the floor as entries. A prop-from-item path waits for crafting.
- **Hands-on testing** of doors and windows built into real houses, and of several builders' worth of rooms, is still to do.
