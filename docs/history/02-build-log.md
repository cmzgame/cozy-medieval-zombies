### 2026-10-15 — A browser host's welcome packed: built, and the multiplayer core is Done

**Before:** a server world gzipped its welcome, a browser host sent the whole world as it was: 2.2 MB for the valley grown whole here, 4.6 MB for a well-explored world, up the host's home connection to the relay and down again for every join and rejoin, and over 16 MB a join would have failed outright (the relay's limit), for browser hosts alone. The browser packs only asynchronously (CompressionStream), and the plan written in TODO (a copy packed between joins, sent while the world had not changed) could not work: the world changes every step.

**Built:** the welcome is the snapshot at the join's tick, as text from the save's cache, **packed in the background while the world steps on**, and sent to that connection when it is done (whole, as a fallback, if the packing fails). The welcome now carries **the tick of its snapshot**. Frames go to every guest at once, and a guest not yet welcomed used to drop them; now **it keeps the frames that reach it before its welcome** (the newest six hundred, thirty seconds of them) and, once the welcome is in, applies those after its tick, in order, before anything newer. **`net.PROTOCOL` is 4,** since a guest of protocol 3 would drop those frames. A server world still packs at once (zlib), its welcome carrying the tick too. `net.status()` names the last welcome's sizes and time (`welcome`) and how many are packing.
