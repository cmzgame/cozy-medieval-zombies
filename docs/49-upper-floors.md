# 49 — Upper floors: storeys, stairs, cellars, and seeing and shooting down

**Status, 2026-10-27: a spec, revised after Primary's scan against the engine (#517) and Michael's rulings on it, not built.** Written from `origin/main` at ece297c1 and checked against bb52ef0b, from the research in #511, after `51` (the depth buffer) and `52` (walls 96 px tall) were ruled the same night; it builds on both. Michael ruled its frame in session (`02`, 2026-10-26, Upper floors: a spec): a second floor is the goal and third floors and root cellars are designed in; stairs are built by players and placed on the map, three tiles long, climbing one floor, and crossing their top "magically transitions you to the next layer"; players cannot destroy stairs for now; a stair brings its own landing tile, never built on; ladders and hatches are placed by the map only, never removed or pulled up, and ignored by the dead; an empty tile is never walkable and nothing ever falls; shooting down is for bows and thrown knives and axes, not spells or spears; no cellar is dug ("No cellar dig, only prefabs"); "all worlds are still considered expendable while we test"; nothing here waits on roofs (#512). A **default** is the spec's own (written by a Secondary), Michael's to overrule, and section 15 lists them; the rest is his ruling. Ten build steps (0 to 8, with 3b), one issue each, in order, after `51` and `52` have landed; step 9 is the later list.

## 1. Why, in Michael's words

"I assume it would have to work like Zomboid, which means level 2 is its own whole layer, etc. But it's not worth doing if you can't look out a second story window and see zombies on the ground, and in fact I am hoping we can make certain things (bow and arrows at least) work as an attack from up high." "Also consider things like third floors, root cellar basements, etc. But if a second floor is the only thing I get out of it, I would still be happy." "Spec out stairs, much like zomboid, 3ish tiles, goes up one floor, magically transitions you to the next layer of the world when you cross the boundary." And the castle has been waiting for height since its plan was written: "a castle is height, and this engine has none" (`29-world/03`), now to be had by storeys, never by taller walls (`52`).

## 2. Today

- **The data has carried a layer since day one, and only the data.** The chunk key, `getChunk`, `tileAt`, `growTile`, `peekTile`, `dropItem`, `setFloor`, and the chunk records in the save and on the wire take a `layer`, always 0 (`02`, 2026-10-08). Every character and animal carries `layer: 0`, saved and sent, and nothing reads it. Above the chunk store, almost every system calls with the default: movement, the pathfinder, rooms, collision, the dead's sight, breach, and siege, reach, interaction, building, the chart, light sources, the visible set, and the draw. #511 lists them with lines.
- **A tile on any other layer is grass today.** `getChunk` fills a non-zero layer with the default floor: there is no "nothing here".
- **Two leaks wait for the first upper tile:** the light's per-chunk build ignores the layer (an upstairs torch would light the room below through the floor), and the room fill reads layer 0 while it walks every chunk (mixed layers corrupt room ids). Step 0 closes both before anything else.
- **No stairs exist** beyond a placeholder frame nothing picks and a decorative ask (`29-world/09`); the castle plan keeps a `stairs` prop for "when floors come" (`29-world/03`).
- **After `51` and `52`:** the frame sorts per pixel by each vertex's `X + Y + Z`, and a storey is 96 px, three crates.

## 3. The model

- **Layers are whole numbers:** the ground is 0, the floors above it 1, 2, and so on, a cellar −1. **The engine handles any layer from step 0**, and the proving ground carries a test building with a cellar and two upper floors, so every step is tried on four layers. **The valley's content** (section 11) is the ground, one floor above, and root cellars; a third floor comes with the castle.
- **A storey is `STOREY` = 96 px**, one constant, the wall height of `52`: layer L's floor stands 96 L above the ground, a cellar's 96 below it. Every height in the game is measured from the ground: a body, a wall, a sill, a flight.
- **An unauthored tile** above the ground is **void**: no floor, not walkable, nothing drawn, sight and flights pass through it. Below the ground it is **earth**: solid, not walkable, opaque, drawn as nothing (a cellar is a room cut out of it). The ground stays the ground. **They are answered by the frozen blank, one per layer** (`map.js`'s `blank` is grass today, for every layer): a read on an upper or lower layer where nothing is authored or built gets the void or earth blank and **grows no chunk**; only authored and built tiles make a chunk on another layer, so nothing empty is saved or sent, and an upper floor's bake treats a missing neighbour as void.
- **Rooms per layer:** the fill (`fillRegion`) runs on one layer. **Earth bounds it as a wall does**, so a cellar is a room by its earth. **Void it enters**, as it enters any floor: a stairwell inside a room's walls is void the fill crosses and the room keeps, since the walls still bound it; **a void tile in a chunk that has not grown is the outside at once** (as a missing tile is today, `map.js` ~2224), so an open edge of a built floor, with nothing beyond it, reaches the outside and the fill is outdoors (a balcony is outdoors, as a yard is), and the 300-tile cap holds as today. The leak (`map.js` ~2224 reads `peekTile` at the default layer) is closed in step 0. **Ruled (Michael, 2026-10-26): an empty tile is never walkable, and nothing ever falls to a lower level,** whatever made it empty; there is no Zomboid-style drop through a floor, now or later.
- **A floor covers the tile below it** for sight, light, flights, and the draw: under a floor is under a ceiling of planks. A **stairwell** is void over a stair, so the stair can be climbed; a cellar's stairwell is a **hole** in the ground floor, a floor kind (`floor: "hole"`, `walk: false`, drawn as the stair's opening with a rail).
- **A character's place** is `gx`, `gy`, its `layer` (the field it already has), and **`rise`**, the pixels it has climbed up the stair it stands on (0 to 96): drawn, read by sight and flights, sent in its record when not 0 (section 12). Props, walls, items, brackets, doors, and stairs know their layer.
- **One continuous map** (`00`, the first law): a stair is walked; nothing loads, nothing cuts.

## 4. Stairs (ruled: three tiles, one floor, built and placed, not destroyable)

- **A stair is a straight run of three tiles** on its layer, climbing one storey from its low end to its high end, in any of the four grid directions: **each tile rises 32 px, one crate**, so the stair's three steps are the three crates of the storey. The map places them and players build them (section 5); **nobody destroys them for now** (Michael: destroyable stairs are a nice-to-have, step 9), so no floor is ever cut off.
- **Walking it:** a body enters at the low end, from the tile before it, and leaves at the high end, onto the **landing**, the tile beyond the third step on the next layer up. Its `rise` is the distance along the stair times 32 px a tile; **crossing the top edge** (its centre passing the far edge of the third tile) sets `layer + 1` and `rise` 0 on the landing, and the body is drawn and lit and seen as an upstairs body from that step: Michael's "magically transitions you to the next layer". Down is the same backwards: stepping off the landing onto the top step sets `layer − 1` and `rise` 96, falling to 0 at the bottom.
- **Its sides are closed:** a stair is entered and left only at its ends (*default*; a side entry at the bottom step is a later nicety). A body on a stair is on the stair's layer with its `rise`; it collides with bodies on the same stair and with nothing on the ground under the stairwell, which is above it. **A stair's three tiles never block a body on that stair, on either layer** (*default*): on the stair's own layer they are walkable only by the stair, and their stairwell void above is not solid to it (`circleBlocked` treats a non-walkable tile as solid, the water rule, and would jam the top step otherwise); **the stair's side edges block on both layers**, so nothing steps sideways off a step onto the floor beside it, and nothing on the floor above steps into the stairwell; the walls and props of the stair's own layer past the top step and under the landing (a stair built against a room's wall, the natural case) are not in a climbing body's way, since the only edge it crosses there is the top edge, where the layer changes as the body walks through it and the landing's layer takes over; coming down is the same in reverse.
- **Above a stair** the next layer is a stairwell: void over all three tiles (*default*, Zomboid's rule: no floor is built over a stair). **A stair brings its own landing tile, and nothing is ever built on it** (ruled, Michael, 2026-10-26: "the stairs should be coming with their own tile attached ala Zomboid, and that tile cannot be built on"): building a stair floors the tile beyond its top step with planks, held up by the stair itself, the plank in the stair's cost; an authored stair's landing is authored with it; no wall, prop, or stair ever stands on a landing. A stair is refused only where its three tiles or its landing are not free, and the tile before its bottom step must be walkable. **Under a stair** (the wedge below its upper steps) is not a space: the three tiles are not walkable on their own layer except by the stair.
- **A cellar's stair** climbs from layer −1 to the ground: its three tiles are cut out of the earth, its landing is a ground tile, and its stairwell is the **hole** in the ground floor over its three tiles, drawn with a rail on its three closed sides (*default*; a hatch that shuts is step 9).
- **The record** (`stairs`): an id, its layer, its low-end tile, its direction, its material (`wood` or `stone`), `built` for a player's; kept with its anchor chunk and named on the chunks it crosses, as a multi-tile prop is (#341); saved and sent. Bodies stand on it; nothing else does: no items, props, or rugs on a stair.
- **The pathfinder** treats a stair as three nodes on its layer, joined at its top to the landing on the next and at its bottom to the tile before it: an edge between layers. Everyone walks it alike: the player, a guest, the dead, a villager led up it (section 9).
- **The removal tool and the dead leave stairs alone**, as ruled.

## 4b. Ladders and hatches (ruled: placed by the map, never removed or pulled up, ignored by the dead)

A ladder is the one-tile way between layers where a stair's three tiles will not fit: a loft, a root cellar. Its height comes from time, not from walking, so climbing is an action, never a free climb.

- **The record** (`ladder`): its base tile and layer, its facing (the side it is climbed from), its material (wood), and whether it has a **hatch**. It spans two layers: the base tile below, and above it the tile the climber comes up through, which is a **hole** (section 3) or, with a hatch, a **hatch floor**: a floor kind with an `open` state. Placed and kept as one, with its anchor chunk and its tile on the other layer named, as a stair is. The hatch is part of the ladder, never a thing of its own.
- **The landing** is the tile beyond the hole in the ladder's facing on the upper layer, reserved by the map: walkable, and nothing stands on it (floor items and rugs may lie there; no prop, no wall, no building). The loader refuses a ladder whose landing is not free.
- **The hatch:** shut, it is a floor: walkable, built on by nobody (reserved), opaque to sight and light. Open, it is a hole: not walkable, sight and light pass (section 7's ray and section 10's portal). **Open** and **Shut** are E, or the menu, from the landing, refused while anything stands or lies on the hatch; the state is the world's (saved, sent with the chunk, in the sync check's map part), and the opening is drawn as the leaf swinging up.
- **Climb and Descend:** E, or the menu's **Climb ladder** at its foot (on the base tile's facing side) and **Descend ladder** from the landing. A timed action on the player (`interact.js`'s actions, run by the host's step): the walk inputs are ignored while it runs, as while carrying; `rise` runs from 0 to 96 over `ladder.climbSeconds` (*default* 1.5 s, a lever in `data/tuning.json`, `42`), and at its end the body stands on the landing with `layer + 1` and `rise` 0. Descending is the same backwards from the landing; **descending through a shut hatch opens it first** (the climber is the one leaving, so the hatch's "nothing on it" check does not count them). A guest's climb is its E or menu command by label (`performByLabel`), run on the host; its `rise` reaches every client in its record (section 12), so everyone draws the same climb.
- **The pose:** one climb pose in the figure's pose data (`32`), two frames alternating the hands, drawn against the ladder; the body is sorted by the depth buffer at its height like any other.
- **Nobody removes or raises a ladder** (ruled): it is built into the map like a wall; there is no pulling a ladder up behind you. A hatch cannot be added to a ladder later; it is the map's variant from the start.
- **The dead ignore ladders** (ruled; Michael: "they just ignore ladders", the game being a dark-fantasy RPG whose every mechanic sends you out, and a loft you never leave starves you): no ladder edge in the dead's paths, no climb state for them, no battering of a hatch; a siege whose only way in is a ladder finds no way and searches (today's `wayIn` with nothing found). Villagers and animals ignore them too.
- **The cellar by hatch:** a root cellar reached by a hatch in a kitchen floor and a ladder down is the valley's classic (section 11); such a cellar is the dead's never.
- **Later** (step 9): ladders built and removed by players, a hatch added to a ladder, a ladder as the dead's way.

## 5. Building upward (players)

- **Stairs are buildable** (ruled): a palette entry (`data/build.json`, a `stairs` kind in wood and in stone), its ghost three tiles long on the player's layer in the direction from the player to the cursor (*default*: R already cycles a door's leaf wood in build mode), refused unless its three tiles are free of walls, props, and items and its landing tile above is free (void, or a built floor already there). The cost is in data (*default*: 10 planks a wooden stair, one of them its landing; 9 stone and a plank a stone one).
- **Ladders are not buildable yet** (ruled): not until they can be removed; the map places them (section 4b).
- **Floors are buildable** (*default*, since a built stair wants a floor at its top): a floor tile on layer L may be built where it is **held up**: it touches, by an edge, a wall on the layer below, or a built floor on its own layer within **two tiles of such a wall** (Zomboid's rule in spirit). Walls, doors, windows, and props are then built on it as on the ground. Built floors are planks (*default*: `floor_wood`, 1 plank a tile).
- **Taking apart:** a built floor tile comes up with the removal tool (its plank back), refused under a wall, a stair, or anything standing on it (*default*), so nothing is left hanging and nothing falls. **A wall that holds up a built floor cannot be taken apart** (Take apart and the removal tool refuse it with why) until the floor is gone. A built stair does not come up (ruled).
- **The map's own floors and stairs** are authored and stay, as authored walls do.
- **No cellar is dug, by anyone** (ruled, Michael: "No cellar dig, only prefabs"; `00` says no digging): cellars are the map's.

## 6. What you see: drawing, hiding, picking

- **Lifted, sorted by the depth buffer.** Everything on layer L is drawn 96 L px up, and every vertex's depth is its `X + Y + Z` (`51`), so a lifted floor, a tall tree in front of a house, and a figure on a stair sort right per pixel with no new sort rule. The per-layer light, ambient, and fog textures are a set per layer drawn (`render/area.js` builds one each per layer; a vertex says its layer in its layer bits, below).
- **An upper floor's ground** is baked per chunk and layer as the ground is (`15`), void as alpha 0 in the bake (an authored neighbour not yet grown is grown first, as the ground bake grows its neighbours today, so nothing authored bakes as void), and drawn as one quad a chunk at its height **with depth write on, in `51`'s pass 0 run layer by layer from the lowest**: the ground and its flat things first as today, then for each lifted layer in view its floor quads (write on, tested) and then its own flat things (rugs, lying items and arrows, tracks, the tile mark, the landing rings; write off, tested), which the floor's written depth then protects from everything under it in pass 1. So a lifted floor hides what is under it, a rug on it stays seen, and nothing draws the floor per tile (`src/CLAUDE.md`'s rule stands); `51`'s pass list says the same in its own words. The open edge of a floor (a balcony, a wall-walk, a stairwell) shows its thickness (art, section 11). **The layer rides each vertex** in the flags float's upper bits (`layer + 1` in bits 6 and up, so a cellar is 0 and the ground 1), which picks the light, ambient, and fog textures; the depth is `51`'s own float, with the storey's lift in it, while `a_height` stays the height above the floor for the night pool's climb.
- **A cellar is drawn only while you stand in it, and then the ground layer is not drawn at all** (Zomboid's way, both ways): a cellar tile at (x, y, −96) sits on screen under the yard at (x + 3, y + 3), and the ground is `51`'s pass 0 with no depth written, so a cellar drawn from the yard would paint over the yard; below the ground you see the cellar, its earth, and nothing else, and from the ground you see no cellar.
- **The hiding rule** (*default*; roofs adopt it, #512): **you see your own layer and every layer below it down to the ground, never a layer below the ground you are not on; a layer above yours is hidden over the building you stand in** (the map's building index for an authored one; for a player's building, the tiles of the room you stand in, which is no room if it is over 300 tiles or open, and then nothing hides) and drawn everywhere else. Outdoors on the ground every storey of every house stands; inside a house its upper floors are gone and the village keeps theirs; upstairs you see your floor, the ground around the house, and everything else; in a cellar only the cellar (above). The near-wall fade and cut-away run on your layer. **A storey you stand behind fades** (*default*): an upper storey of a building between you and the camera, covering your body on screen, is drawn at the near-wall fade's alpha, as a near wall of your own room is today, so a player behind a two-storey house is not lost for six tiles; **its floor quads leave pass 0 for `51`'s pass 2, depth-tested and writing none**, as a faded wall does, since a floor's written depth would hide the body behind the house that the fade exists to show.
- **Seen but covered:** one of the dead you can see on a **lower** layer than yours (section 7), whose picture is covered by nearer geometry (your own upper storey's wall or floor), is drawn as an outline over it, in Object Highlight's style, in a last small pass with the depth test off (*default*; `51`'s copies are depth-tested, this pass is not); never on your own layer, where the walls hide the dead as today.
- **The camera** centres on the player lifted by its storey and `rise`; the names, bars, bubbles, and numbers over heads, the night pool, and the landing marker lift with their bodies.
- **Picking:** a point on screen can be over a tile on one layer and a tile three tiles on along each axis on the next; **a seen dead one's picture is read first** (the cursor over a body you can see aims at it, whatever layer it is on, as Object Highlight finds it by its pixels), then **your own layer's floor where there is one under it, else the next floor down** (*default*), so from upstairs the cursor over the yard reads the yard, and a dead one under your own floor's picture, within three tiles beyond your north or west wall, can still be aimed at by its outline (above). A click, the menu, and E act on that layer only; reach is on one layer (section 9).

## 7. Sight across storeys (Michael's bar)

- **On your own layer:** as today, a flat walk over that layer's edges.
- **Down and up, a ray with heights:** from the eye (the layer's floor, plus `rise`, plus 40 px: the figure's head centre is at 38, `rig.js`) to the target (its layer's floor plus its middle), walked edge by edge as an arrow's flight already is (`archery.js` `scanPath` and `wallStops`, which test a height against a wall's top and an opening's sill, top, and door top): a wall on any layer stops the ray where it passes inside that wall's height band, an opening lets it through between its own heights (24 to 64 for a window, 0 to 64 for a door, `52`), a floor stops it where it crosses that floor's height over a floored tile (a hole lets it through), and an opaque prop stops it below its height. **One function** serves the visible set and the dead's sight, symmetric as sight is (`07`): if you can see it, it can see you.
- **Leaning out** (*default*): a player within reach of an open window or a parapet looks from the opening itself (the eye at head height, 48, inside the window's band of 24 to 64, on the wall's line, and the window's own edge skipped by `wallStops` for the ray and the flight), so the foot of the wall below, where the dead batter the door, is in sight; from a step back a sill hides the few tiles nearest the wall. **Both ways:** while a player leans out, the dead's ray targets the lean-out point too (the symmetric rule holds), so what you can see can see you. The big window of `52` is for exactly this.
- **The visible set and the fog** are kept per layer; `seenAlpha` (`sight.js`) reads the light on the target's layer; the chart (`33`) reveals from layer 0 by its flat walk only (section 12).
- **Cost:** the ray with heights runs only for the tiles of other layers in the viewport that the flat walk did not settle, cached with the visible set as today (recomputed when the player or the camera changes tile, `main.js`); the dead run it only toward a player on another layer; the light, ambient, and fog textures are built only for the layers in view (one set for a village with no storey in sight); rooms rebuild per layer, so a change on one layer refills that layer alone. Step 4 measures all of it on the square at night with twenty of the dead and prints the cost.
- **Height reaches no farther:** the same sight range and light rule; height only clears walls the flat walk could not see past.
- **Not through a stairwell** (*default*): a stairwell joins layers for bodies, not for eyes; seeing up or down a stair is later.
- **A cellar has no windows:** seen from inside it or not at all.

## 8. Shooting and throwing from height (ruled: bows, and thrown knives and axes; not spells, not spears)

Michael, 2026-10-26: "I want shooting down to be a thing for bows and thrown weapons, I do not expect it for any spell or thrown spears though. I don't really care how it works just that it does well enough to be worth having. If it helps as a compromise, only shooting from 0 main level and 1 first floor?" And the sniper's nest is fine by him: "No matter how many zombies you snipe from upstairs, you must eventually go downstairs to get more arrows, and also food or water or you just die. The use case for a player is clearing the immediate area outside their walls from upstairs before opening down, which seems fine to me."

- **The arc fits, launched from the opening:** a flight's height is `z0 (1 − u) + zEnd u + 4 H u (1 − u)`, leaving at the shooter's floor plus `rise` plus the weapon's `z0`, and coming down at `zEnd`, the floor of the layer the cursor read. A bow on the ground is unchanged (`zEnd` 0). **From a window, the shot leaves from the opening itself** (the lean-out point of section 7, on the wall's line, at the floor plus `z0`, which is inside the window's band), not from the body a step back; the scan showed a shot launched from the body dips under the sill before the wall line for anything nearer than about 2.5 tiles (a knife 5 to 6, an axe 3.3). From a parapet or a balcony's edge the same. A guest's aim carries its layer (section 13).
- **Who shoots down:** the bow, and the throwing knife and axe (ruled). **The spear and the fireball do not** (ruled): a spear thrown from height falls as if thrown on the ground floor (`zEnd` its own floor; it lands on the upper floor), and a spell cast from height flies flat on its own layer, as today; **neither ends over void**: a flight on its own layer stops at the last floored tile along its path (the floor's edge, or the window's wall), where the spear lands and lies and the fireball flares, as against a wall. **From layers 0 and 1 only** (*default*, Michael's compromise offered): a shot from a second floor or higher is refused with a word ("Too high to aim from here"), until it is seen to work well enough from one storey; the castle's storeys may lift it later.
- **Michael's open idea, carried as an option** (*default* off; a lever when built, its switch built in step 6 with the hunt): the dead shot from above are told where a ground-floor door of the house is ("extra info from the game because of their height disadvantage"), so sniping draws them to the door to bang on.
- **The flight's checks, per layer:** `scanPath` tests at each step the walls of the layer whose height band the flight is in, an opening's sill, top, and door top offset by its storey, a floor where the flight crosses its height over a floored tile, and props by height on their own layer; a fence stops it at its own height (`52`); at the landing, only bodies on the landing's layer are hit, with the clear line tested by heights.
- **Height helps by itself:** from 96 px up the flight clears the ground floor's walls and fences that would stop a shot from the ground. No bonus is added; height adds no range and no sight.
- **Melee, the shove, and the dead's bites never cross a storey.**
- **Shooting up** at a higher layer is the same flight; the cursor reads a seen body first and then layers at or below yours (section 6), so aiming up at a dead one you see works, and aiming at an empty upper tile waits (step 9).

## 9. The dead, villagers, and animals

- **The dead see across storeys** by section 7's ray, so an archer at an upstairs window is seen, and sight-pull (`48`) draws the rest by the same sight.
- **They bite and strike only on their own layer:** today one would walk to your spot on the ground floor and bite through the ceiling; step 3 ends that.
- **They climb stairs** (*default*, as Zomboid's do): the pathfinder's nodes are `(x, y, layer)` and its hash with them, a stair's top joined to its landing on the next layer; the octile estimate adds a storey's walk for each layer between; `separate` pushes bodies apart on one layer only. **A dead one whose target is on another layer paths to the nearest stair it knows that reaches that layer,** not toward the target's `(x, y)` (today a failed path walks straight at the goal, which would park it under you); with no stair known it searches where it is. So one that saw you upstairs walks to the house, breaks the ground floor's door as today (`09`), and comes up; one that saw you in a cellar by stairs comes down. **A siege** (#147) knows an upstairs room's way in is its doors and the stairs that reach its floor: `wayIn`, `goalRoom`, and the siege's openings take a layer, and a stair is a way in.
- **No door at the top of the stairs** (ruled, Michael, 2026-10-26; this overrides the spec's first draft and keeps `02`'s "whatever closes it they can break"): the landing is a stair's own tile, never built on, so a player may wall its three free sides and put a door in one of those walls, one tile back from the stairs. "It allows zombies to walk up the stairs, stand on that first tile guaranteed, and bang on doors or windows build upstairs." A sealed room upstairs is then a cell by the same law as on the ground: the dead reach its door and break it, or batter its shutters, and you leave by your own hand. Stairs cannot be destroyed, so nothing is ever cut off.
- **Nobody climbs through a window** (law): no jumping down from an upper window, and nothing falls; void is not walkable.
- **Reach is on one layer:** `reach.js`'s test (distance and a clear line, #208) adds the layer, so nothing upstairs reaches a chest or a tile below through the floor or out of a window (#206's open window is in reach on its own layer only); every verb addressed by a tile (till, plant, pour, fish, Use on a tile) reads the actor's layer.
- **The dead may be placed upstairs or in a cellar** by the map (a sleeper in an upstairs bed, one in the dark of a cellar).
- **Villagers stay on the ground for now** (*default*; homes upstairs are step 9), but one that is led (`45`'s lead for animals is not theirs; `04`'s follow is not built) is not an issue yet. **Animals stay on the ground** and never take a stair.

## 10. Light

- **Every source carries a layer:** fires, brackets, lanterns, portals, a carried torch, a burning body. **Each layer has its own light map**, and light does not cross a floor (*default*): an upstairs torch lights its room, not the one below; an upstairs window is a portal for its own room; a cellar has no daylight and its stairwell hole admits the ground floor's light as a portal (*default*).
- **An upstairs window lighting the ground outside** is new physics and later (step 9).
- Step 0 closes the light leak (section 2) before any of it.

## 11. The valley, the data, and the art

- **The map generator** (`tools/mapgen`; the map is generated, never typed) gains a building's `floors` (a list, each `{ layer, rect, floor, wall, openings, partitions, props, brackets }`, a negative layer a cellar), `stairs` (each `{ at, dir, layer, material }`, its landing checked), and a cellar's hole placed over its stair by the loader; `map.loadMap` builds a per-layer authored source and a **building index** (each authored building's footprint, kept at load on host and guest alike from the map file every client has), which the hiding rule reads and roofs will.
- **The valley's content** (*defaults*, step 7): the inn's upper floor (three rooms over the common room, a stair by the bar); one two-storey house on the square (a bedroom over the shop); and **root cellars under existing houses**: Hollow Farm's farmhouse and the inn (*default*: two), each a small room with shelves and a barrel, reached by **a hatch in the kitchen floor and a ladder** (section 4b; *default*: hatches for cellars, stairs for upper floors), and a hayloft in the great barn by a plain ladder (*default*). The castle's gatehouse, towers, and wall-walks use all of it when the castle is generated (`29-world/03`), by storeys.
- **The proving ground** (`?map=square`) gains a test building with a cellar, a ground floor, and two floors above: two stairs, a cellar by hatch and ladder, and a plain ladder to a loft, for the checks.

## 12. The save, the wire, the sync check, the chart

- **The save: every saved tile address gains a layer**, as a rule, not a list: props, walls, brackets, doors, stairs, ladders, a hatch's `open`, a siege's `at` and openings, homes, the dead's `lastSeen`, `lastOut`, `breach`, `press`, paths and search targets, and `goalRoom` reads the right layer; a character's `rise` rides when not 0. **`save.VERSION` 4 with no migration: every world saved before it fails to load** (`save.upgrade` has no step for it and returns null), and that is accepted (ruled, Michael: worlds are expendable while testing; `02`, 2026-10-17); the PR says so in one line.
- **The wire: every sent tile address gains a layer**, the same rule: the touched and evicted chunk lists; every guest command that names a tile or an edge (build, place, set down, rug, painting, act); **a guest's aim** (`aimAt`, a bare grid point today, names its layer, and the host's `aimPoint` projects on that layer's floor); the dead's and villagers' slim records carry `layer` and `rise` when not 0; **a player's `layer` and `rise` move to the light record** (`layer` is in the heavy set today, so a stair crossing would resend bags, worn things, and hands); **`net.PROTOCOL` bumps in each step that changes the wire** (1, which gives every command and address that exists today its layer, then 3 and 3b, which add the stair and ladder records and `rise`), since each merges live; steps 5 and 8 add nothing to the wire.
- **The sync check:** chunks are ordered by layer, then position; **the players' and the dead's parts hash `layer` and `rise`** (they hash position, state, and health today, so a replica on the wrong storey would pass), and `hashText` with them.
- **The chart** (the map on M) shows the ground only (*default*): it reveals by its flat walk on layer 0, from a player on layer 0; upper floors and cellars are not charted; a map per layer is step 9.

## 13. The guest's side (every step)

Every storey is the host's world, stepped on the host: a guest's player climbs a stair by its own inputs as it walks today (the host's step moves it, and its `layer` and `rise` reach the guest in its light record); **the guest's prediction does the stair transition itself** (the same rule on the same stair record, so it does not stick at the top until a correction); a guest's aim names its layer; a guest's build, place, and act commands name their layer; the chunks of every layer reach a guest as touched chunks; the dead's `layer` and `rise` ride their records. **The drawing and the hiding are each client's own:** a guest upstairs and the host downstairs see different cut-aways, each by its own player. Each step tries its features in two tabs (`?nosave&mute&relay=none`) with `check("sync")`, and reads what it adds to a frame from `net.status().wire`.

## 14. Inside the laws

- **One continuous map:** stairs are walked; nothing loads.
- **The clock:** nothing grows with time.
- **Nobody climbs through a window; a window is never a safe spot** (#140): an upstairs window facing void is out of the dead's reach to batter, one facing a floor they can reach (a landing, a balcony) is battered as any window; the ground floor is always in reach, and they come up the stairs.
- **A sealed room is a cell:** upstairs and in a cellar as on the ground; stairs cannot be destroyed, so nothing is ever cut off.
- **Only trees are seen through:** the hiding rule hides a storey whole over your building; it is not a see-through hole.
