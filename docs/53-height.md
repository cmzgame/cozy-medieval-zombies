## 1. The ask, in Michael's words

"I did not see the full scope of what I wanted at first or cliffs would not be the last thing we built." The scope now: an intricate three-storey castle built by hand with the tools (stone walls, round towers, wall walks with battlements, gates, stairs on each storey), standing on a bluff made of cliff walls and cliff tops with a ramp, defended from the walk with a bow against the dead at the gate below, "and maybe even from one more" storey up, with a friend joining as a guest. His bar from `49`: "it's not worth doing if you can't look out a second story window and see zombies on the ground."

## 2. The verdict

**The foundation holds. The crossings do not.** Everything that lives on one layer is right and was built carefully: layers as whole storeys 96 px apart (Zomboid's model, his ask), the void above and the earth below as frozen blanks that grow nothing, floors a tile at a time with no physics and no falls, stairs as an edge between layers walked by everyone, rooms per layer, the depth buffer sorting a lifted storey per pixel, every tile address, record, command, and sync part carrying its layer. The headless castle proves it: every piece of the castle went up through the commands a guest sends, the save restored with every sync part equal, and the world hashed the same twice.

What fails is everything that must cross between layers, and it fails for one reason: **the game has two frames of height and no bridge between them.** The renderer measures height in world pixels from the ground (a storey is 96 px, a wall 96, a battlement 28, a flight's arc so many pixels up). The simulation measures it in whole layers and nothing else. So a sight ray runs on one layer with no heights at all, a flight's height is counted from the shooter's own floor while its obstacles are read on the ground, the player's visible set has no layer in its keys, the dead see across a storey only near a stair, light is per layer and the picture lights only two of them, and the one notion of "a building" the hiding rule knows is the map file's list of authored rectangles, which nothing a player builds ever joins.

In his castle, today, in the order he would meet it (section 4 has the lines): he builds it and, from the ground, his friend on the walk is not drawn at all; he climbs, and is shown the whole yard and the dead inside the castle's own ground rooms through their walls; he walks under his own wall walk and vanishes under it; he draws on the dead at the gate and the arrow stops at his feet against the wall he stands on, or flies out, hits nothing, and hangs in the air a storey up where nobody can fetch it; and the dead never look up, so the walk is both perfectly safe and useless. None of this is a bug in a line. Each is the two frames disagreeing.

**The smartest way to engineer it** is one change of frame, then everything else follows from it: **one unit of height, world pixels above the ground (a layer is 96 of them), and one walk that uses it.** Every occluder becomes a band in that unit (a wall on layer L is 96 L to 96 L + its height, a window passes its sill to its top plus 96 L, a floored tile is a plane at 96 L, a battlement 96 L to 96 L + 28, a gate's arch 96 L to 96 L + 78, a cliff face 0 to 96). Every body is a point with a height (its layer's floor plus its rise, its eye at 40, its chest at 30). One function walks a segment from one point to another, edge by edge as the flight already does, and at each crossing asks the layer whose band holds the segment's height there. That one function serves the player's sight, the dead's sight, and every flight; it replaces `layersMeet`; it makes the visible set and the fog per layer; it makes the battlement's 28 and the gate's 78 real in play; and it is what `49` steps 4 and 5 already described. The code is nearer to it than the list of failures suggests: the walk exists (`archery.js` `scanPath`), the opening bands exist (`wallStops`), every body has `layer` and `rise`, and every map read takes a layer. What is missing is the absolute frame and the per-crossing layer.

The second change is as small to state: **a building is what you built, not what the map file listed.** The hiding rule, the storey fade, picking, and later roofs read a structure derived from the world (the room you stand in and the floors above it; a slab of floor and the walls on it), never the authored index alone. With that, a player's castle hides and fades as the inn does.

Everything else in section 7 is housekeeping around those two: the dead's dozen layer-blind reads, the greedy stair choice, light per layer in view, the flat things on a storey, items that land over void, the tower that cannot hold its own stair, the ramp on a storey, the silent cancel of a wall out of sight, sound and animals that ignore layers, and checks that build their own pieces and prove each crossing, since no check today fires a flight off the ground, puts the dead at an upstairs door, or reads a pixel above layer 1.

So the answer to "have we already done it perfectly" is no, and the list in `#578` does not stand as it was: its transparency question, its floor-corner rule, and its hardening are answered or placed by this spec (section 8), and the rest of `#578` is small beside the two changes above.

## 3. The system as built, in one page

What follows is the model the four sheets found, stated once, with where each piece lives. It is the ground the spec builds on; nothing here is a finding.

- **Layers.** Any whole number; 0 the ground, 1 and up storeys, -1 and down cellars; `STOREY` 96 (`map.js:67`). Every chunk is keyed by `(cx, cy, layer)`; every read, mutator, collision test, `canStep`, and room fill takes a layer and defaults to 0; a tile address is `[x, y]` on the ground and `[x, y, layer]` off it. Above the ground an unauthored tile is `VOID`, below it `EARTH`, a cellar stair's opening `HOLE`: engine floors with `walk: false`, each a frozen blank a read gets with no chunk grown (`map.js:68-87`). `circleBlocked` treats a non-walkable floor as water, so a floor's open edge is a wall of nothing and **nothing ever falls** (`map.js:3222-3228`, `3333`). The wire caps a layer at -8 to 8 (`net.js:1494`); building caps nothing.
- **Floors.** Built a tile at a time, anywhere, with no physics (Michael, 2026-10-27); on a storey a tile must touch, by an edge, a walkable tile of that layer (`map.js:1066-1076`); a laid floor carries `built: { under, free }` and comes up to its `under` (void on a storey). Everything is built on the builder's own layer (`placement.js:244-250`).
- **Stairs.** Three tiles on their layer climbing one storey, a landing beyond the top step on the layer above that nothing is ever built on, a stairwell of void over the three steps; a body's `layer` and `rise` follow it (`stairStep`, `map.js:3138`), the layer changing as its centre crosses the top edge; sides closed on both layers; built stairs climb north or west only and **never come up** (no removal path, `placement.js:1065-1126`); the ramp is a stair of material `ramp_stone` with no treads that brings solid, unremovable sides for the ground under its landing. Ladders and hatches (`49` 3b) are not built.
- **Rooms.** Flood-filled per layer, earth bounding, void entered; a region over 300 tiles is outdoors; a see-through wall (fence, bars, battlement) bounds none; a shut gate does. A wall walk is outdoors; a courtyard of 300 tiles or fewer closed by its curtain is a room, indoors.
- **Rings, gates, battlements, cliffs.** A ring is edge walls on the stepped outline of a 5 or 7 square with a record remade from its walls; upstairs it needs a real floor under every stepped tile and no stairwell (`map.js:1687-1690`). A gate is two or three door-kind walls sharing one `open`, over plain stone only, the dead never breaking it (data flags). A battlement is a 28 px see-through wall on a storey's edge with the walkway's floor on exactly one side, its side and pieces read from the floors. A cliff is a wall with cliff art on a layer's edges; its plateau is simply the floors on the layer above; nothing in the world joins the two (`cliffs.js` chooses pictures only).
- **The building index.** `map.buildings()` and `buildingAt(x, y, layer)` list the map file's authored buildings, filled by `loadMap` alone (`map.js:41-58`, `3999-4050`). Nothing built in play is in it.
- **The draw.** Every layer in view is drawn lowest first, lifted 96 L px, each standing vertex 96 L deeper, so the depth buffer sorts it (`scene.js:263-354`, `storeys.js:17-29`); an upper floor is one baked picture a chunk drawn as a quad with depth written, void clear; the hiding rule hides the layers above the viewer over the indexed building the viewer stands in; the storey fade draws an indexed storey that covers the viewer's body as one sheet at 0.42 (`PASS_FADE`); the near-wall fade is keyed to the viewer's room on the viewer's layer; a ring's near half fades in its room; a near battlement fades on the viewer's tile; tree canopies cut a soft hole for the viewer alone; two area-texture sets exist, the ground's and one storey's, every other storey drawn as open sky (`scene.js:242`, `264`; `gl.js:362-363`).
- **What crosses.** `lineOfSight` runs on one layer with no heights (`visibility.js:12`); the player's visible set is cast on his layer with keys of `(x, y)` alone and serves every layer drawn (`main.js:1343`, `area.js:193-207`, `entities.js:204`); the dead see across one storey only when both bodies are near one stair (`zombie.js:394-406`); a flight has its shooter's layer for targets and landing but its obstacles read on layer 0 (`archery.js:136`, `209-213`, `241`, `369`, `396`); light is per layer in the world (`light.js:366-368`); reach, melee, bites, E, and the menu are on one layer; the pathfinder is 2D per layer with the stair-first rule between layers (`movement.js:44-79`).

## 4. The castle test: what a three-storey castle meets today

Ranked by how much it would surprise him; each with the line it was traced to at `e3c771ed`. "Wrong" means the code disagrees with itself; "missing" means an unbuilt step of `49`; "surprise" means as coded, not as expected. The headless tester built the keep, the tower, the walk, the gates, the bluff, and a second keep on the bluff with 392 commands (none crashed; 2346 s of build bars, about 39 minutes by hand) and probed it; the pane confirmed the two drawing findings on the proving ground.

## 5. What we were not foreseeing

These are the structural things under the list above, each a cause of several of its lines. A fix that leaves one of these in place leaves its lines coming back.

1. **Two frames of height.** The renderer's pixels and the simulation's whole layers, with no function between them. Lines 1, 2, 4, 5, 9, 15, 19 all come from it. Section 6.1 is the bridge.
2. **The default layer is a trap.** `tileAt`, `peekTile`, `wallBetween`, and friends default `layer` to the draw layer, which is 0 in a step, so any sim call that forgets the layer silently reads the ground and never fails a test. Lines 1, 8, 15, 21. The fix is not a dozen patches but a rule the checks enforce (section 7, step 0).
3. **"A building" means the map file.** The only structure the renderer knows is the authored rect list, so the hiding rule, the fade, and later roofs do not exist for a player. Line 3. Section 6.2 derives structure from the world instead.
4. **The visible set is flat.** One set per client, keyed by `(x, y)`, cast on one layer, used for every layer's fog and every body's drawing. Line 4, and the guest's half of it. It gains a layer with the bridge (6.1).
5. **Two light sets.** A compromise from `49` step 2 ("one set a layer is to come") that a three-storey castle outgrows at once. Line 10. Section 6.4.
6. **Items are not bodies.** The no-fall law was written for bodies; an item that lands over void has no rule and lies in the air. Line 2. Section 6.3 gives items the one fall the game needs.
7. **The flat pass knows one floor.** `49` section 6 designed a flat round per lifted layer and the build drew the ground's flat things once. Line 13. Section 6.4.
8. **Each see-through thing has its own rule.** Near walls by room, storeys by a box test on the index, rings by room, battlements by tile, trees by rectangle overlap, cliffs by nothing. Michael's transparency question in `#578` is this. Section 6.2 gives one rule in three mechanisms, and says what happens when two stack.
9. **Stairs are forever and rings are strict.** Together they make the tower trap (line 6). Section 7, step 6.
10. **The dead's model of a storey is "the room under me".** Line 8, and the greedy stair (line 7). Section 7, step 5.
11. **Nothing is proven above layer 1.** Every check that touches storeys uses the authored tower house on the ground or a drawable's flag. Section 7 gives every step headless rows on pieces it builds for itself, Michael's own castle is the walk after each, and the review's numbers (the fade's 0.42, the sight's 40 px eye, the cost) are read from it.
12. **The caves.** A hollowed hill (Michael: "a few thoughtful changes away") is exactly a room under a plateau: the cover rule of 6.2 hides the plateau over your room, the room's light is its fires, the dead reach it by its opening. It needs nothing of its own once 6.1 and 6.2 are built, and it is one of step 3's rows.

## 6. The model: first principles

### 6.1 One height, one walk

**The unit.** Height is world pixels above the ground, the renderer's unit, everywhere in the simulation too. A layer is `STOREY` of them. A body's foot is `96 layer + rise`; its eye `+ 40` (the head's centre is at 38, `rig.js`), its chest `+ 30` (the bow's `z0`), its top `+ 48`. Nothing in the save or on the wire changes: `layer` and `rise` already ride every body; the height is derived.

**The bands.** Every occluder on a layer L is a band of heights, read from data every client already has:

- a wall of height h on an edge: `[96 L, 96 L + h]` (stone, wood, a cliff 96; a fence 36; a battlement 28; a ramp's side 96);
- an open door: passes `[96 L, 96 L + 64]`, an open or broken window `[96 L + 24, 96 L + 64]`, a shut gate's arch `[96 L, 96 L + 78]` where its flag says shots pass; boards pass nothing; a shut shutter leaks nothing for sight (its 0.05 is the light's);
- a floored tile on L: a horizontal plane at `96 L` over that tile (a hole, a stairwell, and the void are not planes);
- a solid prop or a trunk: `[96 L, 96 L + its height]`, as the flight reads them today;
- a tree canopy: nothing (sight and flights pass the leaves, as today).

**The walk.** One function, `trace(a, b, policy)`, where `a` and `b` are `(gx, gy, z)` in that unit, walks the segment edge by edge over the grid (the flight's `scanPath` is the walk; sight's `lineOfSight` is the same traversal with no heights) and at each crossing asks for the band on **the layer whose storey holds the segment's height there**, `floor(z / 96)`, and on the layer below when `z` is within a wall's height of its floor (a 96 px wall on layer 0 is crossed by a segment at `z` 80 on layer 0, by one at `z` 130 on nothing). Between crossings it tests the floor planes it passes through: a segment going from `z` 136 down to 30 over a floored tile of layer 1 is stopped at the plane `96` unless the tile is a hole. A `policy` says what stops it: `sight` (opaque walls, shut shutters, opaque props, floor planes), `shot` (what `wallStops` says, by the bands, plus floor planes and props by height), `reach` (sight plus a shut gate's bars). The flight's arc is a curved `z(u)`; the walk takes a `z` function of the fraction along, so one implementation serves a straight ray and an arc.

**Who uses it.**

- **The player's sight:** `computeVisible` casts `trace` from the eye to each tile's middle on **every layer in view** that has a floor or a body there, keyed by `(x, y, layer)`; a tile of a layer with nothing on it (void) is never visible and never fogged. The fog texture per layer reads its own layer's keys. A body is drawn when its own `(x, y, layer)` is visible. This is `49` section 7, kept.
- **The dead's sight:** `canSee` is `trace(eye, target's chest, sight)` within range, the range scaled by the light **at the target's own layer**; `layersMeet` goes. Symmetric: if you can see it, it can see you. Sight-pull and the flame's sighting use the same walk.
- **Flights:** the arc leaves at the shooter's foot plus `z0` and comes down at the target floor's height (`zEnd`: the floor under the cursor's pick, section 6.3); `scanPath` is the walk with the `shot` policy; the landing hits bodies on the landing's layer (`bodyAt` with that layer), checked by `trace` with heights; what lands lies on the landing's floor. `49` section 8's launch from the opening (leaning out) stands as a default; its "from layers 0 and 1 only" is withdrawn (section 9).
- **Reach, melee, bites, E, the menu:** unchanged, on one layer, by ruling (`49` section 9): height clears walls for eyes and arrows, not for hands. A blow across a stair's top is section 7 step 5's small case.
- **Light:** unchanged in the world (per layer, no light through a floor, `49` section 10's default). An upstairs window lighting the ground is `49` step 9 still.

**Cost.** The visible set runs per layer in view, but only over tiles that have something (a floor or a body); for a village with no storey in sight it is today's cost. The dead run `trace` only toward a player on another layer. `49` section 7's cost row is kept: the square at night with twenty of the dead and a three-storey view of step 1's pieces, printed.

**Leaning out** (`49` section 7, a default): a player within reach of an open window or standing at a battlement's edge looks and shoots from the opening itself, so the foot of the wall below is in sight; the symmetric rule holds, so the dead see him there too.

### 6.2 One cover rule: hide what is in the way, never fade (ruled, 2026-10-30)

Michael's question, in his words: "what should be transparent, when, and how much, how to handle two transparent walls being between" your character and the camera. Today each thing has its own answer (section 4, line 3; sheet B's table). His ruling, with Zomboid as the benchmark ("I think zomboid's approach of hiding everything in the way is going to get us the most immersive game by far, I have never loved the fade"): **the picture never hides the player from himself, nor a body he can see, and what is in the way is removed, not thinned.** Four mechanisms carry it, each already in the renderer in some form, and nothing fades any more:

1. **Hide what is over your head.** The structure is derived, not authored: standing on layer L, your **room** on L (the flood fill, or, outdoors, nothing) has a **ceiling**, the floored tiles of L + 1 over it; the ceiling and everything above those tiles, layer by layer, is hidden (not drawn), together with the row and column past its south and east sides so the walls on them go too (today's rule, `storeys.js:50-55`). An authored building's index becomes one more way to find the same tiles and may stay as a cache. Inside a hollowed hill the plateau over the room is hidden the same way: caves come for free.
2. **Hide what covers your body.** A **slab** is a 4-connected component of floored tiles on one layer, with the walls standing on it and its posts and caps, as one thing, from its floor to its walls' tops plus 12; the existing box test (`storeyCovers`) runs over slabs instead of index rects, for every slab on a layer above yours that is not your own ceiling. A covering slab is **not drawn at all** (Zomboid's "collapse"); it pops in and out as you walk, which players never notice there. Two covering storeys stacked between the camera and you are simply both gone. A cliff slab (a plateau) **never hides** (Michael's ruling, `29-world/10` section 3: "a hill never fades", read now as never hides); mechanism 4 takes it. The fade pass (`PASS_FADE`) retires with the storey fade.
3. **Cut your own room's near walls to a stub.** (Taken outdoors 2026-10-30, `02` "The cutaway and walls down": a wall between the camera and you, or the build cursor, is cut to the stub too, and H puts every wall on your storey down while the build menu is open; `src/render/cutaway.js`.) The camera-side walls of the room you stand in, on your own layer (today's near-wall rule, keyed to the room, `walls.js:387-393`), are drawn **cut down to the floor**: a thin stub of wall at the foot with the cap drawn along the cut, so the room's art shows whole ("The stub is basically down to the floor, and that is the right call. We need to be able to see what is in the room and the semi-transparently wall has been hiding some of our best art."). No new art: a wall's picture is a quad the renderer already clips at posts and under floors, and this is the same cut at a stub height (*default* 8 px, a lever). **A door or a window in a cut wall is drawn whole, at full height, at low opacity** (*default* 0.3, a lever): "It needs to kinda be barely there, but definitely there when you need it ... I just don't want stub walls to mean windows are drawn on the ground and doors are like 10px tall." A shut door reads as a ghost of a door; an open door's swung leaf the same; a window whole with its shutters' state. The ring's near half, the near battlement, and the straight wall's filler take the same stub cut instead of their alphas, so there is one treatment everywhere; the posts at a cut wall's ends are cut with it. With every opening shut, the room is whole and the outside is the fog's dark: Zomboid's picture, where "the windows are drawn in full, and everything outside is black", is the reference, and the fog's darkness (`FOG_ALPHA` 0.36) is a lever to take toward it.
4. **Outline what is seen but covered.** A body you can see (your own, a friend's, one of the dead, by 6.1's sight) whose pixels are covered by nearer geometry that did not hide (a cliff, a plateau, a tree on another layer with no hole) is drawn as an outline over it in Object Highlight's style, in one small last pass with the depth test off (`49` section 6's "seen but covered", and `29-world/10`'s "the player behind a hill is drawn as an outline"). The dead behind a tree on your own layer stay hidden (the forest gives no laser vision, Michael 2026-10-11); the outline is only for what 6.1 says you can see.

Trees keep their soft hole for your own body on your layer (the law: only trees are seen through), and a tree on a lower layer that covers you on a storey is mechanism 4's. Nothing in the frame is translucent any more but the hole, the dead dimmed by the dark, smoke, glows, and the soft sheets.

**Picking** follows the same sight: the cursor reads a seen body first, whatever its layer, then your own layer's floor where there is one under the cursor, else the next floor down (`49` section 6's default): so from the walk the cursor over the yard reads the yard and a shot goes to its floor, and from the ground a dead one you see on a balcony can be aimed at. A click, E, and the menu still act on your own layer only.

**The guest's side.** All four mechanisms are each client's own, by its own player and its own visible set; nothing on the wire.

### 6.3 Items fall; bodies do not

The law is for bodies. An item has one rule: **it never rests on void.** A flight that comes down over void lands on the first floored tile below at that `(x, y)` (the ground at worst), and lies there; a thrown spear or a spell, which `49` keeps on its own layer, stops at the last floored tile along its path (the slab's edge) and lands there, as against a wall. An item dropped by any other means over void (a floor taken up under a pile; a bag emptied at a balcony's edge) falls the same way. This is the only fall in the game, it changes nothing for bodies, and it makes every arrow fetchable.

### 6.4 The picture per storey

- **Light per layer in view.** The area textures become an array indexed by layer bits (`49` section 6 wrote the bits), one set per layer drawn, built from that layer's light, rooms, and carried flames; `FLAG_SKY` goes. A village with no storey in view costs today's one set. A cap of six sets, the deepest first, with any storey beyond it lit as sky, is a default.
- **The flat round per layer.** Each lifted layer, after its floor quads: its rugs (built), its lying arrows and thrown things, arrows' shadows, splashes, tracks, and the tile mark, depth-tested against the floor's written depth and writing none; fire particles on the body's own layer and lift. This is `49` section 6 as written.
- **Seams.** Rings take the walls' `covered` and `onWall` rule (a ring with a ring over it draws no cap; the one above's face wins the seam).
- **A storey's floor blends as the ground does.** The storey bake (`storeys.js` `floorSheet`) takes the ground bake's blend (`15`, `ground.js`), so a cliff top's grass and a laid stone floor meet dirt and grass with the ground's ragged edge, not a hard diamond; with it an art round (**art round 14**, `29-world/09`) for a cliff top that is not a single-pixel floor art and a bare stone floor whose edge blends into grass and dirt (Michael, 2026-10-30, from his hollowed hill: "Those two things will get rid of the few harsh edges in that screenshot").
- **The floor bake.** A storey change re-bakes the touched tile's region of its chunk picture and uploads that region alone (`texSubImage2D`), not the chunk and not the page; the atlas page keeps a free list for a chunk's slot so a re-bake reuses it.

### 6.5 The world's rules that follow

- **A tower holds its stair** (`50` section 3): a ring's stepped tiles may include a stair's stairwell (void over the three steps) and its landing; the ring is refused only where a stepped tile is void with no stair under it. The straight wall of a stair's head stays refused, so the stair runs across the tower's middle row to a landing inside it.
- **Nothing climbs onto a storey from a storey's edge:** the Stone ramp and the cliff pieces are the Terrain tab's, for layer 0 and the plateaus they make, refused on a built floor ("Not on a floor"), unless Michael wants hills on hills (section 10).
- **A wall out of sight is refused up front** ("Nothing to see it by"), with the same line test floors have, so no bar is ever cancelled silently.
- **Everything the ground has, a storey has:** torches on walls, rugs, paintings, set-downs, the campfire, the fireplace, the fire pit, the trough, each by the same rules on the builder's layer; "Not upstairs yet" goes.
- **A layer in the simulation is never defaulted:** every sim read names it; `tools/check.js` scans the sim files for a `tileAt`, `peekTile`, `wallBetween`, `edgeBetween`, `lineOfSight`, `canReach`, or `itemsAt` call with no layer argument and fails on one (the list in step 0). The renderer keeps `withDrawLayer`.
- **Stairs come up** with Free building on, for the builder's own stairs, refused while a body stands on a storey that stair alone reaches (the "nothing is ever cut off" rule kept by a test, not by forbidding); ruled 2026-10-30. Built floors stay destroyable as they are.
- **Towers climb by ladders** (ruled 2026-10-30; `49` 4b folded in as step 6b): a player builds a one-tile wooden ladder on his own layer, against a wall or free-standing, its base tile and facing the record, the tile above it its hole and the tile beyond its landing, reserved; **Climb** and **Descend** by E or the menu as a short timed action (`ladder.climbSeconds`, *default* 1.5 s, a lever) driving `rise` from 0 to 96, the body drawn rising; a guest's climb is its command on the host; **the dead ignore ladders** (the 2026-10-26 ruling stands), so a ladder-only top is a sniper's nest by choice, paid for by the cell law; a ladder is taken apart by its builder like a stair, refused while a body is cut off; hatches wait (`49` step 9); the climb pose is one art ask. - **The castle gates break** (ruled 2026-10-30): `deadBreak` on in both gates' data, their hit points about three doors (`map.DOOR_HP` 100, so 300; today's 400 unused), a lever in `42`; the dead strike a shut gate as they strike a door, the portcullis's broken frame is one picture (art), and a broken gate stands open until repaired as a door does.
- **The caps:** building stops at layer 6 ("High enough") and the wire's cap rises to match (`layerIn` -8 to 8 becomes -8 to 16 with room); a default.

### 6.6 Not foreclosed: falling

Michael, 2026-10-30, not decided, asked while the work is open: climbing through windows again, which with six storeys means letting bodies fall, with fall damage small or none.
