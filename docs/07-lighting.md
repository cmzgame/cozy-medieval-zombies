# 07 — Milestone 2: lighting, fire, and the clock

**Status, 2026-10-16: built (accepted 2026-10-09); a reference.** The code is the truth: `src/clock.js` (a twelve-minute day, night brightness 0.22, new worlds start at 08:00) and `src/light.js` (`lightAt` as the one function, the per-chunk light maps, the day cap 0.92). Light is read per pixel by the WebGL2 renderer (`08`): where a line below speaks of the night layer, the shader does the same from the light, ambient, and fog textures, and Michael's rules for what takes which light hold per pixel (a wall face the tile it looks onto, a wall top the sky, a prop or a character its feet). Each fire kind carries its own `fuel`, `hoursPerUnit`, and `maxFuel` in its sprite definition (the table below), fed through its menu from the bag or from anything in reach (`30`, section 7). `T` lights the torch in a hand (or on the belt) with a tinderbox carried and douses a lit one; a tinderbox or a lit torch used on a campfire or a mounted torch lights it (the `light` verb, below), and takes a torch from the bag into a hand when none is there; a torch burns down while it is lit and a new one is unlit (below); a torch on the belt gives half its light and one in each hand more (`world.beltLight`, `twoTorches` in `data/tuning.json`); the carried light is one per player, each with its owner, and the light and the fires are the world's, the same for every player (`31`). The parent's system as it was read, the Canvas 2D night layer, the first torches-and-hands design, the build order, the acceptance, and the tunables are `history/07-lighting-build.md`.

**Ruled 2026-10-16, built (#88; `src/sight.js`):** darkness shortens the player's sight. With no light a player sees a short radius (`sight.darkRadius`, 1.5 tiles); past it, over a soft edge (`sight.edge`, 2 tiles), the dead fade from seen to hidden unless they stand in light (the light at their feet between `litNone`, 0.3, and `litFull`, 0.45); a carried torch gives sight back to its own radius (3, then the same edge), the dead seeing its flame being the trade; a dead one in a lit room, a fire's pool, a lamp's, or another player's torch is seen from afar, and so are you in it; a burning one is its own light; out of doors by day nothing is hidden (the light is 0.92, the night's ambient 0.22), **and a room shuttered by day is dark too** (Michael, 2026-10-17, on #150: "a fully shuttered room should be dark inside. It's a downside, you have to keep it lit with depletable resources if you want true full protection of being inside and not spotted"), while **open shutters light their room fully by day** ("so you never need to burn wood during the day if you keep the windows open"): one open window or door lights `world.daylightTiles` (30) tiles of a room, so a small room needs one and a large one more in proportion to its size, **and a room opened enough reaches the window-lit cap by day** (`unlitRoomCap`, 0.88; the shut ones' leak keeps only `roomBounce` of its light, so a shuttered room stays at the night's 0.22: `light.daylightShares`, `roomAmbient`; Primary's default, Michael's to overrule), the numbers in data; every number in `data/tuning.json`'s `sight` (Michael: "C: darkness shortens sight, the dead fading to hidden past a soft edge unless they stand in light; a torch gives sight back; numbers in data."). It is what a client draws and points at, and nothing in the world's physics: each player asks about itself (the viewer is passed in, `interact.characterAt(sx, sy, player)`, and the carried light that counts as its own is the one whose `owner` is it) and reads its own copy of the light, so a guest's sight is its own (the host's step asks only to choose which dead one a click lands on, from the clicking player's sight), and the dead's own sight of a player (`zombie.sightDarkFraction`, `flameRange`) is untouched. A hidden dead one is not drawn, has no swing-target outline, no health bar, no hover hand, and cannot be clicked or right-clicked, and its groan is a line in the log with no position ("Something groans in the dark.") rather than a bubble where it stands; **the dark hides information, never physics** (Michael, on #150): a melee swing still hits what is in its wedge and a shot or a throw what it lands on. The fallen (bodies) and the living are drawn as ever.

## The idea in four sentences

Light is one number per position, 0 to 1, and everything reads it: what you see, what the dead see, and later what the off-screen records are drawn to. Night is dusky, never black, and every fire carves a real shadowed pool into it. Indoors, light is a fact about the room: its hearth and its open shutters. A torch in your hand lets you see at the cost of being seen from far away.

## What changes for chunks and rooms

- **The light map is per chunk**, 16×16 tiles at two cells per tile, computed one cell beyond the chunk on every side. A chunk's map includes every source within its radius of the chunk, whichever chunk the source stands in, so two neighbouring chunks compute identical values for the cells they share and the layer draws without seams: each chunk's image is drawn clipped to its own diamond, and the one-cell border gives the smoothing its neighbours. No padding tricks.
- **The signature** of a chunk's map is its list of sources (kind, tile, lit) plus `worldVersion`, since a shutter or a door changes shadows. Opening a shutter rebuilds the visible chunks' maps, nine of them, about a thousand cells each, a few sources each with a ray. If it shows in the debug corner, the signature gets a per-chunk wall version.
- **The path test is `lineOfSight` from `visibility.js`**, the same edge walk sight uses, so a wall, a closed shutter, or a closed door casts a shadow and an open one lets light through. One set of blockers for light and sight: light feeds sight. Trees do not block sight today and so do not cast shadows; revisit if a lit forest looks flat.
- **A room is lit if it contains a lit hearth.** Rooms are refound by flood fill and their ids change, so nothing is stored on the room: at rebuild, each room gets `openings`, the door and window edges its fill touched, and `lit` is derived by looking up the hearths' tiles. Openness counts the room's open shutters and open doors; closed ones admit nothing.
- **Ambient is per tile, not per node.** Outdoors is the clock's brightness. A room tile gets its room's ambient. The night layer is one tint for the outdoor darkness, then each visible room erases its floor diamonds and the faces of its own walls by how much brighter it is than outdoors, so a lit cottage glows whole at night and an unlit one is as dark as the yard. Wall faces lit by the floor map (a torch pool climbing a wall) stays parked, as in the parent.
- **The hearth is both ambient and a source.** Its room is lit by the ambient rule; its radius pool spills through an open door or shutter onto the ground outside, by the ray test, so the parent's separate "lit window emits outdoors" rule is not needed. Close the shutters and the spill is gone.

## Fire is data

Every fire kind is a definition, not a case. A sprite definition declares `fire: { radius, burns, cooks, place }`; a wall edge may carry a torch; an item may be a fire in the hand.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Torch, held | 3 | **itself, while lit**: it burns down (below) | no | either hand; lit with a tinderbox (`T`); on the belt at half, unseen |
| Torch, mounted | 3 | itself, while lit, at the wall rate | no | a bracket on a wall edge, or a torch post; a fireball landing on one that is out lights it |
| **Lantern** (the village's own) | 3.4 (`lantern_post.json`'s `fire.radius`, the one number for post and bracket), steady, no flicker | **never**: it never goes out | no | a lantern bracket on a wall edge or a lantern post (`lantern_post`); placed by the map generator, not taken, mounted, or carried off |
| Campfire | 4 | logs, two hours each, holds four | yes | outdoors; a fireball landing on one with fuel in it lights it |

- **A torch burns down while it is lit** (Michael, 2026-10-17, #156; `02`: "it drains the whole time it's lit. If it doesn't deplete though then you never need to make more and never need to bring a tinderbox"): the torch's own `wear` (the meter a kill bites, 0 to 1) rises with the time it is lit, **fastest in a hand** (`world.torchHeldLife`, 1440 game seconds, two game days of continuous light) and at the slower **wall** rate on the belt, in a bracket, or on a post (`torchWallLife`, 2880); a kill costs a fifth of a torch, far more than any second of burning. Spent, it is gone from its place with the weapon's word ("Your torch is spent."). A lit torch in the bag does not burn and gives no light. It overturns 2026-10-09's "torches never burn out" (struck where it sits). **A new torch starts unlit** (made, found, or bought); **a tinderbox** (a 1 by 1 item that never wears out) **lights it**: `T` lights the torch in a hand, else on the belt, when a tinderbox is carried, douses a lit one, and with no tinderbox says "You need a tinderbox to light it."; with no torch there it takes the one from the bag into a hand. **A torch that leaves a hand, the belt, or a holder is doused** (into the bag or a container, onto the floor, out of a bracket or a post): it burns only where it is used, so there is no free douse-and-relight, and a torch dropped is an unlit item lying there (2026-10-09). **The starting kit's torch begins unlit, a tinderbox beside it in the bag; you light it when you want it** (Michael, 2026-10-17, #156; #136 makes the kit data). An unlit torch gives no light, draws no flame, and sets nothing alight. A torch mounted keeps its wear and whether it is lit, and comes out as it went in. Belt and hand never stack (one carried light per player, the held torch winning; a lit belt torch beside a held one burns for nothing). A mounted torch burns only in a chunk that is loaded, as a fire does: the world far from every player is not stepped. A mounted torch's wear reaches a guest to the hundredth (`map.burnHolders` marks the chunk at each), the save's copy is dropped every step, and a spent one says "A torch burns out." to the players within twelve tiles.
- **Lanterns are the village's light** (Michael, 2026-10-17, #156: "swap the village torches to 'lanterns'", `02`): every light the map generator authors is a lantern, a little brighter than a torch (the lantern post's `fire.radius`, 3.4 against 3) and steady, and **never goes out** (no `wear`, no `lit`), so the clock law holds with the torch burning down: a night in the village is never darker for a player's having waited. A lantern bracket (`wall.bracket.lantern`) and a `lantern_post` prop carry them, dev art like the rest (the art ask is on #105); a bracket or post a player's torch goes in is still a torch and burns out. **The fireball is a flare** (`24`, `data/combat.json` `bow`): its light in flight is `fireballGlow` (6, it was 4), and where it lands it leaves a short light of `flareRadius` (7) shrinking over `flareSeconds` (3), light only; the host's list of flares rides in each frame, so a guest sees it as the host does.
- **Burning.** A burner holds `maxFuel` units of its `fuel` and burns one per `hoursPerUnit` in-game hours (a game hour is thirty real seconds); a full night from dusk to dawn costs the hearth about three logs. At zero it goes out: no ambient, no pool, no flame drawn. Nothing shuts off on a timer; a fire goes out because it burned what you gave it.
- **Lighting and feeding.** A fire's menu offers Light (with a log, or a charcoal, in reach) or Feed, saying how full it is; the fuel comes from the bag or from anything in reach, and `E` takes it as the nearest simple action while you have fuel and the fire has room. **Lighting a fire that is out (a campfire, a fireplace, an oven, a forge) needs a flame carried too: a tinderbox, or a lit torch in a hand or on the belt** (Michael, 2026-10-17, #168: "lighting a campfire, fireplace, oven, or forge from its menu needs a tinderbox or a lit torch"), the rule `inventory.flameOf` that the light verb (below) asks as well, so the menu and the verb cannot drift apart; without one the entry says "need a tinderbox or a flame" and a click says "You need a tinderbox or a lit torch to light it.", and `E` does not take it. **Feeding a fire that is burning needs none.** So a player never lights a fire, and then a torch from it, without having carried a flame: fuel and a flame are both maintenance.
- **Fire lights what is made to be lit: the `light` verb** (Michael, 2026-10-17, #156: "fire lights what is made to be lit"; `src/burn.js`, rows in `data/interactions.json`, the verb table for host and guest alike): **a tinderbox or a lit torch used on** an unlit **campfire with fuel in it**, a **torch on a post** or **in a bracket** that is out, or **an unlit torch in a hand or on the belt** lights it; **an unlit torch used on a burning fire is lit from it** (a burning fire is a flame: the builder's reading). Through Use mode (a guest's Use is the same command the host's is, `use.applyWith`, an item in a hand or on the belt named by its slot) or, for a mounted torch, the holder's menu (**Light the torch**: a tinderbox carried, else a lit torch in a hand or on the belt). **One rule for what can be lit, `map.lightableProp`: a campfire with fuel or a post with a torch; a hearth, a fireplace, an oven, and a forge are passed by**, and a fireball's landing (`24`) asks the same rule. **It stops at a wall:** the player must have sight of the target (`visibility.lineOfSight`) within reach 2.6 tiles of its nearest tile. Every refusal says why (already burning, nothing to burn, no torch there, no flame, too far, a wall in the way, a lantern is always lit, a torch in the bag burns only where it is used). It never burns a body, a wall, or a prop that is not made to be lit, and nothing spreads. A tinderbox is never used up.
- **Flames are drawn** emissive, unlit and unfogged, over the fire: a few frames of flicker per fire kind, and the light flickers with them (`fire.flicker` on the definition; `08`).

## Who reads light

- **Zombie sight of the player**: the parent's scaled range, with `lightAt(player)`.
- **Zombie sight of fire.** New, one rule: on each sight check, if `nightFactor > 0`, a zombie looks for the nearest lit source within `FLAME_RANGE × nightFactor` tiles (twelve at full night) with a clear line, and treats it as a sighting at that tile. A carried torch is a sighting of the player. The memory rule does the rest: walk there, search ten seconds, give up, notice it again. A campfire gathers a slow crowd that mills about it; closed shutters hide a hearth; a torch in a forest brings them from four screens' width. The same number was to be the per-chunk lure of the records `03` planned, which were never built (`history/03-world-model-plan.md`, "Alarm, lure, trickle").
- **The player's sight.** At night the fog is light: a tile in line of sight but unlit is dark, and the night-vision pool shows the few tiles around you. **The dead in the dark are hidden past a short radius unless they stand in light** (`sight.js`; the soft edge fades them out, held things and all, as the picture is drawn). Which tiles count as "visible" for the fog does not change; the night layer is a second darkening on top of it, and the dead's fade is a third.
- **The debug corner** shows the clock label and `light@player`.

## The look

- **The tint is dusky purple**, not the parent's blue. Start at `rgb(36, 22, 56)` at `0.96 × darkness` and pick by eye between two adjacent steps, as the parent's tint was chosen.
- **Full day is never quite full.** The vision says Halloween never ends here: the day cap is 0.92 (`tunables.dayCap` in `light.js`), chosen by eye.

## What this must not foreclose

- **The lantern** (`05-someday.md`): the carried term has an on flag; a lantern is a second item with the same shape and a toggle.
- **Candles**: one more row in the fire table.
- **Roofs**: a room's ambient already ignores the sky; a roof changes only how the room is drawn.
- **Upper floors**: the chunk map is keyed by chunk and layer from the start, even with one layer.
- **Building**: brackets are edge state and posts are props, both placeable by the creative tool.
- **The great hall**: ambient by room may later cap by room size; one line, nothing to prepare.
