## 1. Why, in Michael's words

"The only real goal here was variety in building look, and obviously circular stone buildings were common in the middle ages: it unlocks real towers, cool castle rooms, small rounded additions, and all kinds of other stuff." He first asked about slanted walls for hexagons and octagons (#513); the research found a hexagon impossible on a square grid and an octagon's left and right slants seen edge-on from this camera, as thin pillar lines, where a cylinder reads naturally all the way round. Then: "what about actual circles? ... Doors stamped in fit on any edge of the circle that touches an outside edge of the grid? ... Straight walls would also have to be programmed to match the circle, extending a bit further than is needed for flat walls on flat walls."

## 2. The geometry

- **A round room is a circle inscribed in an N by N square of tiles,** N 5 or 7 (ruled; 3 struck). Its wall is a ring **as tall and as thick as its material's straight wall** (ruled; 96 px, `52`, and the material's `thickness`), drawn on screen as an ellipse, 2 to 1.
- **The stepped shape** is the set of tiles whose centres lie inside the circle: **21 of a 5 by 5** (the square less its four corner tiles; rows of 3, 5, 5, 5, 3) and **37 of a 7 by 7** (less three tiles at each corner; rows of 3, 5, 7, 7, 7, 5, 3). Its outline is **20 tile edges at 5 and 28 at 7**: these are the ring's walls (section 3). The corner tiles left out are the **corner pockets**, outdoor tiles the drawn curve crosses. **Inside, the curve crosses eight tiles** of the stepped shape, the ends of its outer rows and columns: (±1, ±2) and (±2, ±1) on a 5 by 5, (±1, ±3) and (±3, ±1) on a 7 by 7 (a 7 by 7's (±2, ±2) tiles are nicked at the corner by a pixel and count as whole). **No furniture stands on those eight** (ruled, Michael, 2026-10-26: "furniture can't be placed on those 8 tiles"): a 5 by 5 has 13 furniture tiles, a 7 by 7 29; floor items and rugs may lie there (*default*).
- **The drawn curve** is the circle itself, radius N/2 from the square's centre. It runs through the stepped outline: at the four door points it touches the outline's edges; at the diagonals it passes outside the steps' inner corners and inside their outer ones, by up to 0.38 tile inside and 0.41 outside on a 5 by 5, 0.58 inside and 0.30 outside on a 7 by 7 (Primary's measurement, #518). **Tiles whose centre a body cannot reach** because of the union (section 3): a 0.3 body needs to come within 0.08 of a waypoint (`movement.js`), and the curve denies that on the 7 by 7's eight pocket tiles at (±2, ±3) and (±3, ±2), whose centres lie 0.106 outside it, on a radius-2 rounded corner's pocket tile (0.121 outside), and on the two room tiles beside a radius-3 corner's pocket (0.085 inside); the 5 by 5's are all reachable (its nearest fall short by 0.036). **Such tiles are marked `unreachable` on install** (*default*): not walkable for the pathfinder, wandering, villagers' homes, and set-down, while `canStep` still lets a body pushed there stand, so nothing ever sticks on a waypoint it cannot reach; `check("round")` proves it in step 1.
- **The four door points** are the middles of the square's sides, where the circle touches it: for an odd N each is the middle edge of the outline's middle row or column, an ordinary n or w edge, so **a door or a window there is today's door or window**. The curve leaves the edge's ends by 0.05 tile at 5 and 0.04 at 7, a pixel or two, which the door's frame covers. On screen the four points sit at the ellipse's four diagonals, the angles every door is drawn at today.
- **The ring's length:** about 15.7 tiles at 5, 22.0 at 7.
- **A rounded corner** is a quarter circle joining two straight walls at a room's corner, with a radius of 2 or 3 tiles (*default*; a radius of 1 cuts no tile and is a picture only, left for later): a whole number, so the arc meets each straight wall at a tile corner. Its stepped shape takes the one corner tile out of the room (the quarter's tiles with centres outside the arc: one at either radius); that tile becomes an outdoor pocket, its two outer edges go, its two inner edges become walls of the ring's kind, and the straight edges still under the arc (one a side at radius 2, two at 3) take the quarter's id and are drawn as the arc, their straight pictures dropped. A quarter costs one unit of the material for each new edge it lays (two, at either radius; *default*), the re-flagged edges free. These are quarters of the even circles (4 and 6 across), which is no conflict with the rooms' odd sizes: a corner holds no door.

## 3. The model: the world's logic on the stepped edges, bodies stopped by both (ruled, option A)

- **The ring is edge walls.** Every edge of the stepped outline is a real wall in its tile's `n` or `w` slot, of the ring's material, carrying the ring's id. So **rooms, the pathfinder, sight, light, reach, melee, the shove, bites, flights, the E scan, the dead's breach and siege, the save, and the wire** all read it through today's code, with no seam and no new case: a sealed round room is sealed by the rules that seal any room; `wallBetween`, `lineOfSight`, `scanPath`, the light's rays, and the room fill see 20 or 28 ordinary walls. "Walls are state on tile edges, never objects" (`src/CLAUDE.md`) holds; the ring adds **one record** (`round`: an id, its square's corner and N, its layer, its material, `built`) for the drawing and for taking the whole apart, kept with its anchor's chunk and named on the chunks it crosses, as a multi-tile prop is (#341). **A rounded corner** is the same record as a quarter (`corner`: the square's corner it rounds, its radius).
- **Bodies are stopped by both** (ruled): body collision takes the union of the stepped edges and the drawn curve. `circleBlocked` knows only tiles and their axis-aligned `n`/`w` segments, so **the curve's pieces are an index on the tiles they cross**, built from the record on install on host and guest alike and never saved, each piece a short chord (one per 15 degrees, *default*) tested with `pointSegDist2` beside the tile's own segments. **At a door point the chain's vertices sit at the door edge's two ends and no piece is chained between them**: the edge blocks or passes there by itself (`wayOpen`: shut, open, broken, boarded), so the curve never depends on a door's state and no chord lies across half a doorway. So nobody ever stands in the drawn wall; the one mismatch is being held off it at the diagonals by the distances in section 2, which the player reads as the wall's thickness.
- **The door points are edges like the rest of the ring**, with one difference: they are the only ring edges where the stamp accepts a door or a window, because the curve touches the edge there and a flat leaf fits. A door point with nothing stamped is a ring wall, drawn as the curve. Stamping a door over it replaces the ring wall as a door replaces a plain wall today (`map.js`'s build over a wall, #147), **keeping the ring's id under it** (a new `under` field beside `under`, `underBuilt`, and `underFree`, restored as they are by `destroyWall`), so taking the door away restores the ring wall and not a flat one with posts; `canStampOver` and `canBuildWall` refuse a door or a window on any ring edge but the four door points. There is one source of truth, the edges, and the record holds no openings.
- **The ring counts as wall for a torch's bracket** (ruled: "the ring counts as wall for hearths and torches", narrowed by his fire rulings the same night): a bracket hangs on any ring edge's camera face, as on a straight wall. **No fireplace stands against a ring**: the fireplace of the fire rulings is a straight piece on a stone slab against a flat wall and cannot sit flush to a curve; **a round room's fire is the 2 by 2 floor fire, or none** (it lights the room as a hearth does today; "A 5x5 round room probably does not comfortably fit this and other stuff, and that is fine with me. Don't make a round room your kitchen unless it's big enough to hold the floor one"). A 7 by 7 holds it with room to spare; a 5 by 5 holds it on four of its thirteen furniture tiles. The fires themselves are their own spec (Primary's, from the same rulings).
- **No furniture on the eight tiles the curve crosses** (ruled, section 2): `setdown.js`'s stand rules and the build palette refuse a prop there (an `edge` mark set on install, read as the ring's tiles are, never saved); the stepped tiles inward of them are the room's furniture tiles.
- **No painting hangs on a ring edge** (`paintings.js`'s `segmentProblem` refuses the ring's id): it would draw flat off the curve; paintings on a curved face are section 7's.
- **The floor is the stepped tiles**: laid floors bake as today's diamonds on the 21 or 37 tiles; the ring's drawn base runs over the step corners; the pockets are outdoors, with grass and daylight. Indoor light and ambient are per tile by room and so follow the steps, with nothing leaking in at the diagonals.
- **A straight wall meeting a ring ends at a vertex of the stepped outline** (*default*; the build refuses a wall toward a ring that would end elsewhere, with why, and the mapgen asserts it): there the join is a wall corner and the rooms on either side are sealed by the ring's edges; a wall stopping at the square's own corner would end in a pocket and leave the room open. **The filler is a picture** (*default*, automatic), in two cases: where the wall's line crosses the curve inside the square, its last picture runs on along its own line to the curve, half a tile at most (Michael's "extending a bit further"); where the wall runs along the square's side, tangent to the curve, its last picture turns down the outline's step edge from the wall's end to the curve, half a tile. At a door point's side, where the wall touches the curve, there is nothing to fill. Bodies are already stopped there by the union.
- **Upper floors** (`49`): a ring is on one layer, its record carrying the layer and its edges being that layer's walls; a round tower is a ring on each storey, the floors above being its stepped tiles (so `49`'s support rule reads the ring's edges below, as any wall), a stair inside it (a 5 by 5's middle row holds `49`'s approach tile, the three steps, and the landing: five; **as built the code refuses it**: a ring on the storey above rejects the stairwell under its stepped tiles, `map.ringProblem`, and the stair is refused under a ring built first; `53` step 6 relaxes the ring's rule so a tower holds its stair, 2026-10-30). `49`'s building index holds an authored round tower's stepped tiles, so its hiding rule knows the tower. The castle's towers are planned this way when the castle is generated (`29-world/03`).

## 4. Drawing

- **The ring's segments are emitted with their own points along the arc,** as straight faces are (`world: [wa, wb, wb, wa]`, `walls.js`), so the depth buffer (`51`, as revised: depth from true geometry) sorts them per pixel against the dead, props, and trees with no per-slice tie; **the near half and the far half are separate drawables**, the near half in `51`'s translucent pass when it fades, the far half opaque. The art is therefore two half-bands, not one cylinder picture.
- **`walls.js` skips ring edges:** no straight wall picture and no post (`postAt`) at the 20 or 28 step corners; the ring's drawables draw them.
- **The near-wall fade:** when the player is inside, the ring's near half fades as the near walls of a square room do today (`walls.js`'s rule, keyed to the room).
- **The art is generated, never hand-drawn** (*default*): for each wall material and each size, a cylinder band a wall's height (96 px, `52`) in two halves and its cap, three or four flat shades lit from the top left (`art/01`), with the material's texture wrapped round, through the parametric pipeline the walls came from (`art-gen/village`). Placeholders are generated at boot first, from each material's colours, as every new picture is. Rounded corners are quarters of the 4 and 6 bands.
- **The door points** draw today's door and window parts on their edges; a frame at each end of a door covers the pixel or two where the curve leaves the edge.
- **Light** is read along each segment's foot, from the stepped tile it runs over, as a wall's is.

## 5. Building (players) and the map

- **The map first** (ruled): `tools/mapgen` gains `round(at, size, wall, openings, floor, layer)` (the openings by side: `n`, `e`, `s`, `w`, each a door, a window, or none) and a building's `corners` (`{ ne: 2 }` and so on), and `map.loadMap` lays the ring's edge walls and the record.
- **The valley's round buildings** (*defaults*, step 5): Hollow Farm's dovecote as a 5 by 5 round room (planned as a prop, `29-world/05`); one rounded addition in the village (a chapel apse, or an inn's bay); the castle's towers round when the castle is generated (`29-world/03` plans them square at 6 by 6: the nearest round size is 7). Stone rings are the map's until a stone wall joins the palette (below).
- **Players build them** (ruled) from the build palette: a `round` entry for each wall material the palette has (log and plank today; **no stone ring until a stone wall is in the palette**, since built stone walls "cost a great deal of stone", `02`, and that price is set then, *default*); ~~the ghost a circle in its square from the tile where the drag starts, growing from 5 to 7 as the drag goes out (*default*: the size is the largest the drag covers)~~ a palette entry for each size, 5 across and 7 across, its ghost the circle centred on the tile under the cursor, built by a click (`02`, 2026-10-30, Round walls by size); **refused unless the stepped tiles are free** of walls, props, and items **and every outline edge is free or a plain wall** (*default*): an existing plain wall on the outline is taken into the ring (it gains the ring's id and is drawn as the arc, its straight picture and posts dropped on that run), so a ring stands against a house as an apse with the house's wall bulging out into it; a door or window already on the outline refuses the ring unless it sits on a door point; the corner pockets may hold anything; the cost in data, **one unit of the material per edge of the outline** (*default*: 20 and 28 logs or planks, as one wall edge costs one), so the cost scales with the size (ruled). Doors and windows go only on the four door points, by the ordinary stamp. **No straight wall is built on an edge between two of the ring's tiles** (*default*: `canBuildWall` refuses inside a ring), and a ring's edge is already a wall. A rounded corner is a palette entry placed on a corner where two of the player's own straight walls meet; **a map-placed building's corner is not rounded by a player** (*default*: players remove no authored wall).
- **Taking apart:** the removal tool takes a player's whole ring at once (half its materials back, as any made thing, `43` section 2), its doors first; a single ring edge is not taken apart alone (*default*: half a ring is nothing to draw); never an authored one.

## 6. The guest's side, the save, the sync check

A ring is the host's world: built through `placement.performPlacement`, the one path for host and guest, a guest's by a `net.sendPlace` command naming its square's corner, size, material, and layer; its edges are walls in their chunks, saved and sent as walls are today; its record is saved and sent in its anchor's chunk (`serializeChunk`, touched when built or taken apart). `game.syncParts`' map part covers both. `net.PROTOCOL` is bumped once, in step 1. The drawing is each client's own. Each step is tried in two tabs (`?nosave&mute&relay=none`) with `check("sync")`, and what it adds to a frame read from `net.status().wire`. A new `check("round")` proves each step.

## 7. Build steps

1. **The ring in the world:** the record (round and quarter), the stepped edges laid as walls with the ring's id, the curve's segments in body collision, the save and the wire (PROTOCOL), the mapgen primitive, and a ring of each size and a rounded corner on the proving ground (`?map=square`). Proven headless and in the pane by `check("round")`: a body stops at the curve and at the steps, a ray is stopped by the ring and passes an open door, the inside is one room of 21 or 37 tiles, the dead path in by a door and nowhere else, no bite lands through the ring (the scan's case, #518), no path ends on an `unreachable` tile, no prop stands on a curve-crossed tile, a door removed leaves a ring wall.
2. **Drawing the ring:** the two half-bands with their points on the arc, the generated placeholder bands and caps, the near-half fade, the light along the foot, the base over the step corners; `check("round")` reads the sort's worst cases by pixels.
3. **Doors and straight walls:** the four door points taking today's doors and windows with their frames, a door restoring the ring wall when removed, a straight wall meeting the ring with its drawn filler, the bracket hanging on the ring and the fireplace refused there, the floor fire inside, the dead breaking in at a door point.
4. **Rounded corners:** the quarter record, its corner tile leaving the room and its edges re-flagged, its art, and the mapgen's `corners` placing them on authored buildings.
5. **The valley's round buildings:** the dovecote, the rounded addition, the castle's towers when it comes; the art asks filed for real bands per material.
6. **Building them:** the palette entries, the drag that grows the size, the cost, the refusals, the removal; a guest building one.
7. **Later, not now** (filed as one issue with step 1's): round fences (a pen, the same ring with see-through walls); windows on the curve; a rounded corner of radius 1; a stone ring in the palette, with the stone wall; a cone roof and a curved eave (#512); paintings on a curved face; rings larger than 7; a player rounding an authored corner.
