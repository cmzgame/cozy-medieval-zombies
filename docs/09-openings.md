# 09 — Milestone 3: openings, and the dead get in

**Status, 2026-10-27: built (accepted 2026-10-10); a reference. Walls are 96 px tall, the door's top at 64 and the window from its sill at 24 to its top at 64, the torch bracket at 48 (`52`, built 2026-10-27; `DOOR_TOP`, `WINDOW_SILL`, `WINDOW_TOP`, `BRACKET_UP` in `src/placeholders.js`); a flight passes an open door under its top and an open window between its sill and its top, and every wall stops one below its own height, a fence at 36 (`archery.wallStops`). Nobody climbs through a window, player or dead (Michael, 2026-10-16, #134; `02`):** a window is for sight and light, shuttered or open or broken, and a door is the only way through; the climb, its hold of E, and its numbers are gone, and the dead who want a room go for its doors (and batter a window still shut or boarded open to see in, never to pass: Michael, on #140). The code is the truth (`src/map.js`, `src/interact.js`, `src/entities/zombie.js`) and the numbers below hold. As built: each side's boards are a list of each board's own hit points (`wall.boards.near`, `wall.boards.far`), and a map authors them as `boards: { in, out }`; boarding and pulling are in every opening's right-click menu as well as on `B`, a plank spent from the bag; the walls and the board overlays are drawn art (`17`, `18`); **sealed** is derived and shown in the debug corner but gates nothing itself; sleep reads the siege, below, not the seal (`30`, 3.3); a guest's `E` and `B` are events in its snapshot that the host performs, and its menu entries are commands by the edge's address (`31`). The art note, the build order, the acceptance, and the tunables are `history/09-openings-build.md`.

Ruled 2026-10-10 (`02-decisions.md`, "Openings"). Until now a closed door stops the dead for good. After this milestone your walls are something you prepare, not something you hide behind: you shut the door, board the window, light the hearth, and listen to them hit the boards until dawn. If a board gives, the door goes, and they come in by it. The melee port comes next, because once they can get in you need something to swing.

## What is already true

- Doors start closed, toggle on a tap of E from either side, and block sight and bodies when shut. The dead cannot open them.
- Windows are shuttered holes. Tap E opens or closes the shutters; nothing passes through one, open or broken (they were climbable until 2026-10-16).
- Every opening carries a transmission number (`docs/08`), so a boarded shutter is one more state with a number, and the portals and the chunk maps follow it with no new rule.
- Rooms list their openings, and a room's ambient already reads them.

## The opening as a stack

An opening is a leaf between two sides, and each side may carry boards. Michael's rule (2026-10-10): one kind of thing, one number, nothing comes back.

```
wall.boards = { near: 0, far: 0 }   // planks per side, up to BOARDS_PER_SIDE (3)
wall.hp     = the leaf's hit points; wall.broken = true once the leaf is gone
```

The near side of an edge is the tile it belongs to (south of a north edge, east of a west edge); the far side is the tile beyond. An actor's side is the sign of their position against the edge, the same test `outsideSign` makes with rooms.

- **The leaf** is the door, or the shutters. It has health. Broken, a door is an opening that is never a door again, and a shutter is a window that is never shut again. Nothing is repaired; if the dead break your door you do not have a door anymore.
- **Boards** are planks nailed to one side, each with its own health. Any board on either side makes the opening opaque and solid: nothing walks or paths through it, open leaf or broken leaf alike.
- **Attack order** from a side: that side's boards, then the leaf, then the far side's boards. When nothing solid is left the way is open.
- **Everything is spent.** A board the dead break is gone. A board you pull with a hammer gives its plank back (#215, `docs/02`: pulling needs the smith's hammer carried, and so does boarding). Wood is what you go out for: planks for the walls, logs for the fires.
- **Transmission** from the stack: boards on either side 0; closed whole shutters the leak; a closed door 0; open or broken 1.
- **Bodies:** a door passes when open or broken and unboarded; a window never passes a body, whatever state its shutters are in.
- **Sealed**, per room, each step: every door closed and whole or boarded, every window shuttered and whole or boarded. Shown in the debug corner; it gates nothing (sleep is refused by a siege held on the player, below, not by this).

## What you can do

Planks were free until the inventory (milestone 4, 2026-10-10); a board spends a real plank now, like logs feed a fire. `E` keeps its meaning; `B` is boards, a stand-in key until there is more interface.

| At | Tap E | Tap B | Hold B |
|---|---|---|---|
| a closed door | open it ("boarded" if any board on either side) | nail a board on your side (a smith's hammer carried) | pull one of your boards (the hammer carried; the plank comes back) |
| an open door | close it | nail a board (over the opening) | pull a board |
| closed shutters | open them ("boarded" if boarded) | nail a board | pull a board |
| open shutters | close them | nail a board | pull a board |
| a broken opening | nothing to open | nail a board | pull a board |

Hold E does nothing: nobody climbs through a window (Michael, 2026-10-16, #134), so an open window is for looking through, never for going through.

## What the dead do

- **Finding a way in, or out.** A zombie in `chase` whose path to its goal fails, on the step the path failed (not again through the backoff after it), picks a room to breach: the one it is shut in, if its goal is elsewhere, else the one its goal is in. **Out of a room:** the door that leads toward its goal's room, else the nearest door; a door already open is no way out that was not there, so then it goes for the goal's room from outside. **Into a room:** the nearest of the room's **doors it can walk to and stand beside**, and of its **windows still shut or boarded**; it paths to the tile beside it and enters `breach`. When every door of the room opens from another shut room (a hall, a porch, a house within a house), it breaks that room's door first, a few rooms deep (`wayIn` in `zombie.js`). Nearest, not weakest: the dead are dumb.
- **Windows are battered for sight, never passed** (Michael, 2026-10-16, on #140: "The dead may batter a window's shutters or boards open to see in, never to pass."). A window whose shutters are closed and whole, or that carries boards, is a place the dead break at (boards first, then the shutters, as for a door); once it is open or its leaf broken and unboarded it is nothing to them, and the door is the way in. **A dead one that sees a player through an open window walks away from it to a door and breaks it: a window is never a safe spot.** A dead one with no door it can reach (a door whose tiles hold furniture, a house in a shut yard; an opening already open or broken is never one to break, so such a dead one does not loop at it) batters the window open and then **searches**: it leaves the goal alone for `noWayTime` (8 s) rather than press at a wall or search paths every step.
- **The siege** (Michael, 2026-10-17, #147; `02`, "The siege": "They stay at night", "the siege bites at night"): **at night the dead that saw a player go in wait until the next dawn.** A dead one that sees a player inside a room it is not in remembers the opening: the door they crossed on the way in (only when its last sighting had them out in the open, so it did see them go in: **the door on the line from where it last saw them outside to where it sees them now**, so with two doors close together it is the one they went through, and failing that the door nearest them, #167) and any window it sees them through (`noteSighting` in `zombie.js`, `visibility.openingOnLine`). **A siege starts or renews only from dusk to the dawn it ends at**; by day a sighting is the ordinary chase, and a watcher is given no siege to the next morning. A player seen out in the open ends it, and the ordinary chase goes on. **They go first to the opening they saw the player use**: `wayIn` prefers a remembered opening among those they can walk to, before the nearest (Primary's default, built). When they can get in no way, the search that used to end in giving up ends in the `siege` state: it waits about the house between the openings it knows, standing at one and breaking it if shut (a door; a shut or boarded window is battered for sight, then it comes round to a door), trying another every `siegeSwitch` (25 s); with nothing to break and no way to walk to the player (a cell with a window and no door) it waits at the opening it saw them by; when every opening is already open it goes in. **At the dawn** (`siegeEndHour`, 6, the clock's next reading of it, kept as an absolute clock time so a save and a sleep's fast time hold it) **it loses interest and wanders**, wherever it has got to, unless it sees a player, in which case the ordinary chase goes on and no new siege starts by day; the player's death ends it too. Nothing calls more of the dead: the siege is the ones that saw you. **No sleep while a siege holds the player:** the `sleep` verb refuses with "The dead that know you are in here are still about." while any dead one holding a siege on that player is within `siegeSleepRadius` (14 tiles), whichever room of the house they are in; a sleep already begun is not broken (Primary's default, Michael's to overrule).
- **The dead strike a fence down** (Michael, 2026-10-17, #147; reshaped 2026-10-18, #174: "make the gate/door/shutters damage an actual attack, with a damage number, and an animation, just like when they attack you... on a short cooldown... it might make it look less like they are vibrating against the fence and then it explodes"; `02`, "The siege": walls never break, a fence does): a fence, or a gate (a door in a fence), is a wall whose definition says `wall.fence`, with **hit points like a door**: `world.fenceHp` (90), a gate `fenceGateFactor` (half) of that (`map.createWall`). **A dead one at it strikes it**: the `press` state walks it to within `zombie.fenceReach` (0.9) of the edge's middle, and each strike is the **attack animation** (`pressHit`, at its active frame) on the **bite's cooldown** (`zombie.cooldown`, 1.2 s, so about 1.5 s a strike), taking `zombie.fenceStrike` (1) hit point (`map.strikeFence`); between strikes it stands still (it no longer nudges at the fence: nothing vibrates). **A crowd is simply more strikers**: every body that reaches the section counts, so a crowd that arrives spread out brings a section down too (the old crowd threshold, and the second body standing on it, are gone), and **the damage stays** (saved with the wall; nothing mends it). **Real seconds to the burst, measured with real bodies (the review of #185 and `check("fence")`): a fence section, one alone about 140 s (the 90 hit points at a strike every 1.5 s), two 71 s, three 48 s, five 48 s (a section holds about three strikers at a time; the rest queue or take the next section), eight 37 s bunched and 38 s spread out; a gate, one alone 71 s, two 37 s, three 25 s** (Michael's bar, 2026-10-17: a few of the dead must not bring a section down in seconds; it is a problem to solve urgently, by running or finding a weapon, not an about-to-die. **Each strike is one small jolt** (`wallStruck`, an `fx` every client draws: a pixel for a tenth of a second, in sim time; a door's or shutter's strike gives it too), and **when it gives it bursts into a cloud of splinters with a puff of dust and is gone**, with no pushed-over fence drawn: `map.destroyWall` (the save and the wire carry it) and the host's `fenceBurst`, an `fx` of eighteen tumbling splinters (dev art, `fire.js`). A dead one seeks a gate first, as the opening the fence has, when it has no way to a player it hunts, whether it is chasing, searching, or holding a siege; else it walks to the fence on the way (`zombie.fenceWay`, `map.fenceAhead`; a gate standing open is no fence, and **a gate opened while they strike it ends the striking: they walk through it**, #397), so a house in a fenced yard is no safe spot. **A fence takes no boards, a gate included** (Michael, 2026-10-24, `02`): the menu offers none and `B` at a gate says so (`map.addBoard` refuses it). **A dead one at a fence still bites a player in reach across it** (a fence is no wall to a bite, only to a body: otherwise it would be exploitable): the strike hands to the chase's own bite when the player is in reach, and the bite reaches `fenceBiteExtra` (0.5) further across a fence its dead one is striking from its reach. **It goes on while it knows the player is in a room** (its last sighting inside one, or a held siege), as breaching a door never gives up unseen, so a fenced house is no safe spot by day or night; only for a player it last saw in the open does it give up, after `pressGiveUp` (20 s) unseen. **A window is a place to be bitten from, never a way in** (the window law, #167): a player standing at an open window is within the reach of one waiting outside (contact 0.7, a body stopping 0.3 from the edge), one a step back is not; the code is as it was and `check("siege")` says so. Doors, shutters, and boards were already struck this way (`breachHit`, `breachDamage` 5 a hit on the same cooldown); they now give the same jolt. Nothing else breaks: a plain wall, a door, and a window are as they were (`02`, 2026-10-10).
- **A door's two tiles stay clear** (the builder's default, for Michael to overrule, #140): nothing is put down on either (a carried prop, a built door's own tiles), and the generator keeps furniture off them and keeps both tiles of every door reachable on foot (`check.js` floods every map from the spawn with every door and gate open, and proves both), and what a station makes lands off them, because a prop where a door is stood at hides the door from the dead and leaves the window as the only thing to batter.
- **Breaching.** Facing the opening, it attacks on the bite cooldown, `BREACH_DAMAGE` (5) into the stack in attack order. Hit points to start: a board 40, a door 100, shutters 60, and a strike comes every 1.45 s or so (the swing and the cooldown), so one shambler takes about 29 s through a bare door and about 100 s through one boarded three deep on both sides; three of them a third of that. When the way is open it repaths. If the goal moves to another room or it sees the player, it drops the breach and chases.
- **Nothing breaks a wall.** The dead never attack a plain wall. A room with no openings is a room they cannot enter.
- **Noise** stays out. If the hammering on a door ever wants to draw the others, that is question 18 reopened, not this milestone.
- **What breaks is gone**, theirs and yours alike.

## Castle gates (art round 12; Michael, 2026-10-29, `02`)

A **castle gate** is a 2 or 3 tile stone gate with a portcullis, in the stone wall: `wall_stone_gate_2` and `wall_stone_gate_3` (`data/sprites/`), each a drawn wall whose definition says `wall.gate` (its `span`, its `opening`'s heights, its `leaf`'s plane, its `swing`, and the flags the rulings fixed) and `wall.run: "wall_stone"`.

- **One wall on each edge of its run.** Each carries `kind: "door"`, the gate's sprite, and `gate: { i }` (its place in the run, 0 the first: the smallest `tx` on a north run, `ty` on a west one); one open state is kept on all of them (`map.setGateOpen`, the only place it changes, touching every edge so the save and the wire carry each chunk it stands in), so every path that reads a door reads a gate: bodies, paths, rooms, light, the save, the chunk delta, the sync check. `map.gateMembers` finds the edges from any one; `map.buildGate`, `removeGate` (`destroyWall` of any edge takes the whole gate, each edge giving back the plain stone wall it was stamped over), `gateProblem`.
- **Built** over plain `wall_stone` edges, Free building only, at once and free (`placement.isGateBuild`, `startGateBuild`, `gateRunAt`: the ghost spans the run, the edge under the cursor and the one beside it on the cursor's side for 2, the hovered one in the middle of 3); the refusals in their words ("Over a stone wall", "Over a plain stone wall", "Already a gate", "Take what hangs there down first", "Take the torch down first", "No room", "Step back", "Someone is in the way", "Too far", "Castle gates are not in the game yet"). A guest builds by the `place` command's `build` with the first edge (`what`, `tx`, `ty`, `edge`); `net.PROTOCOL` 27.
- **Opened** by E or the right-click menu like a door, labelled "Open gate" and "Close gate", on any edge of it; the whole gate moves together. No boards (`addBoard` refuses a gate); the dead cannot break it (`deadBreak: false`: `needsBreaking` is false for it, `damageOpening` does nothing, `breachTargets` never lists it, so a room closed by a gate is a cell for them).
- **Shut**, it stops a body (`blocksMove`, as a door), a blow and a bite (`map.blocksReach`, `visibility.lineOfReach`; `bitesPass: false`), and fire is flagged not to spread through it (`fireSpreads: false`; nothing spreads fire today); it passes **sight and light** (`transmission` 1: `grille`, its own flag and never `seeThrough`, which bounds no room), so a room it closes is still a room (`fillRegion`), **sealed** (`roomSealed`), lit through its bars as through an open window (`light.js` portals), and it passes **shots** under its crown (`shotsPass: true`, `archery.wallStops`, 78 px).
- **Drawn** from one frame as wide as the run (81 by 137 and 113 by 153), each edge drawing its own 49 by 121 window of it, the rectangle a plain stone wall's picture is on that edge (`render/walls.js` `gateSlice`), so the depth plane, the cap redrawn on the sky, the fade of a near wall, and the cut under a floor are the wall's own; the portcullis is a flat grille cut into the columns each edge owns, on its own plane six units behind the edge line (`emitGateLeaf`), in three frames (`closed`, `mid`, `raised`), the one in-between frame all through a swing of `wall.gate.swing` seconds (1.2), in sim time, each screen's own picture. No post stands at the gate's ends or inside its arch (`sameRun` reads `wall.run`).

## What this must not foreclose

- **Placing a new door or shutters** is building (question 14), not repair, and is built now (`13`, #147): a door or a window stamped over a wall, or a new one over a broken opening of its own kind.
- **Glass and curtains**, if ever: one more leaf kind with its own transmission and hit points.
- **Building**: an opening is edge state, placed the same way a wall is.
- **Noise**: a breach is an event with a position; nothing else is needed to make it loud later.
- **Villagers**: fleeing through an open door uses the same passability (a window is no way out).
