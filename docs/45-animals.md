## 1. Which animals, and where

- **Seven kinds in the valley:** hens, rabbits, deer, cows, sheep, pigs, and boar, with your own beehives (built, `docs/43` section 6). The snow region's beasts wait for that region.
- **Wild game lives mostly in the woods ringing the valley, never fenced in by a rule** (Michael: "let's be careful about restricting game to certain areas. It would be a problem if most of the deer were clumped in the city center ... but that also does not mean they can never leave the forest"). Deer spawn in herds of two to four (*default*) and rabbits near the forest edges, and wander from there as any animal would; a deer may cross a field, a rabbit may come into the village. *Default:* each species has a home (a wood, an edge) it drifts back toward, not a wall.
- **Game comes back slowly, up to a cap per wood** (*default*), as a fruit tree bears again: hunting is renewable, never endless, and it is renewal, not a danger on a clock.
- **Your first livestock comes from the farms** (*default placement*, as `docs/39` already places the buildings): Hollow Farm's hen house keeps hens, its byre cows and its pigsty pigs; the croft's sheepfold keeps sheep, with the shepherd. At the Beastlore level for the kind (hens 1, cows and sheep 4, pigs 7), a player leads one home on a **lead** (*built:* the levels are recorded here and not enforced: anyone with a lead leads any tame animal, open until the skills are built, `docs/39`). **A pen is any fenced yard with its gates shut** (the fences of `docs/09`): a led animal let go inside one stays.
- **A pen that opens** (a gate left open by a friend, a fence the dead burst): *default:* the animals wander out but stay near, drifting back toward the pen as their home; the dead still ignore them; a player leads them back.
- **Whose they are, in a shared world:** *default:* everyone's, as a chest is: any player may lead, feed, collect from, or butcher any animal.

## 2. Hunting

- **Deer flee on sight**, from a player and from the dead. **Boar fight back** when a player attacks one (*default:* a short charge and a bite), so the danger is the player's choice; deer and rabbits never fight.
- **The weapon decides the yield** (ruled): the bow the full yield, a melee weapon less, a halberd ruins the kill.
- **Tracking** (Beastlore 3, ruled): *default:* within a few tiles of game a player at Beastlore 3 sees its faint tracks on the ground pointing the way it went (a client-drawn decal, read from where the game is).
- **Snares** catch rabbits: made at the workbench (*default:* Crafting 1; set at Beastlore 3, ruled), set on the ground near the woods, holding a rabbit after a while (*default*). A snare is a placed prop like a torch post: not a gatherable (no sway, no outline of its own), shown by Object Highlight. Bow and snares both work, so hunting touches Ranged without needing it.
- **What a kill leaves:** a **carcass**, the animal's own record turned dead (*default:* animals keep one list, a dead one marked so, never the dead's `corpses`), carrying how it was killed (the weapon's class, for the yield) and whether it is skinned and butchered; in the record, the save, and the sync check.
- **Skinning and butchering** need a dirk carried: raw meat, hides, and feathers.
- **Livestock is hurt only on purpose** (*default*): a swing aimed at a tame animal hurts it; a multi-hit swing, the shove, an arrow meant for one of the dead, or any spell passes a tame animal by (*default:* every spell, not only the fireball's flare: `src/archery.js` `bodyAt` skips a tame animal for any shot marked a spell, aimed at it or not). Wild game (deer, boar, rabbits) takes arrows and blows as anything does.

## 3. Keeping animals

- **Penned animals never starve and never die of neglect:** nothing punishes being away.
- **Fed animals produce; unfed ones simply stop.** A **feeding trough** (`feed_trough`, its own prop; not the water trough, which stays a source of water) in the pen, built from the palette (*default:* planks), is filled with grain, vegetables, or **hay** (cut with the scythe at Farming 5, ruled 2026-10-23); fed animals produce on a daily cycle: cows milk with a bucket carried, sheep wool with **shears** carried (*default:* shears made at the anvil, Blacksmithing 1).
- **Hens need a hen house:** the chicken coop (built art) is a hen's home in a pen; *default:* a fed hen lays its egg (and its feather) into its coop's grid, where a player collects them; the coop's placeholder rolled table (`coop`: rags, sticks, a little grain) is emptied, so a coop holds only what its hens lay (worlds are expendable, so nothing migrates).
- **Feathers are generous, so arrows can be farmed** (Michael: "If bow and arrow is supposed to be usable as a staple item, we should probably be a little more generous with feathers. Just so you can keep arrow production going and its ingredients farmable"). *Defaults:* a fed hen gives a feather most days besides its egg; a hen butchered gives three to five; a rabbit or a deer gives none. The arrow's fletching (Crafting 4, `docs/39`) costs one feather for five arrows.
- **Breeding** (Beastlore 6, ruled): two of a kind, fed, with room in the pen, make a young one every few game days, up to a cap per pen (*defaults*). **Pens that give more** (Beastlore 6, ruled): *default:* a fed animal whose keeper is Beastlore 6 gives one more of its yield some days.
- **Livestock is safe:** the dead ignore animals, wild and tame; no zombie animals, no predators at the pens. A cosy home stays cosy, and every rise in danger is one a player chose.
- **An idea, not ruled:** rabbits wandering into unfenced crops and eating them, a reason to fence a field (Michael: "Not sure if that is worth building, but an idea"). Filed in `05`.

## 4. What they give, and the recipes

- **Every product is an item** with its picture pair and hover card (the item-art and card laws): egg, milk, wool, hide, feather, and **raw meats:** hen, rabbit, venison, beef, mutton, pork (pigs and boar both give pork; a boar more, and a hide); bacon and lard from a pig (Beastlore 7). **The tools** too: the lead, the shears, and the snare, each with its pair and, for the lead and the shears held, a 38 by 38 portrait. Animals themselves get Object Highlight and a name on hover, not the gatherables' outline and sway (*default*).
- **Raw meat goes into recipes, and onto a fire as it is** (Michael: "I don't want a deer to yield only 'Cooked Deer Meat' to cooking, although throwing it directly in to cook should still be possible for the desperate or impatient"). *Defaults:* each raw meat cooks alone at a fire into its cooked meat; and goes, raw, into recipes: **the plain pottage keeps its meatless day-one recipe** (2026-10-23), and a second pottage with meat sits beside it; the hearty stew (Cooking 4) takes any meat; eggs and bacon (Cooking 4); a meat pie at the oven; smoked meat at the smoking rack (Cooking 5); pemmican (meat, a plant, lard or honey; Cooking 7). ## 6. Art

*Primary's approach, accepted by Michael (2026-10-25: "I like this overall"; `02`):*
- **Animals are drawn sprite sheets, not a second rig.** The human rig (`src/figure/`) exists so characters can wear and hold items; animals wear and hold nothing, so frame-by-frame sheets, as most pixel games draw their animals, are simpler to build and to commission. The sprite loader already reads named frames, facings, and timed animations (`docs/sprite-definitions.md`); step 1 adds the one missing piece, an entity that plays its sheet's own named animations (idle, walk, run, eat, dead) as it moves, where today a character's animations come from the rig.
- **Per species, per facing:** idle 2 frames, walk 4, dead 1, eat 2 where it grazes, attack 3 for the boar; **four facings, the four diagonals of the iso grid, two drawn and their mirrors**, as furniture mirrors. Characters move in eight directions (`src/entities/movement.js`); an animal draws the nearest of its four diagonals for its heading. About ten to fourteen drawn frames a species, fewer for hens and rabbits. The companion, later, is the same sheet with an attack.
- **Owed for the brief:** the lead's and the shears' 38 by 38 portraits for the character screen's slots (they are drawn in the hand by placeholder shapes today, #444); the item pairs of step 4's sixteen (#445): a roast hen, rabbit, mutton, and pork, a venison and a beef steak, the meat pottage, the hearty stew, eggs and bacon, the meat pie, butter, smoked meat, pemmican, woollen yarn, woollen cloth, and leather; and the props of the smoking rack and the tanning vat (dev boxes today).
- **Dev art first:** placeholder animals generated at boot through the same interface (root `CLAUDE.md`), so no step waits; real art replaces them with no code change.
- **The brief** (`docs/art/12-animals.md`, written when the art is asked for): a sheet spec for each of the seven species, and the item pairs for every product and tool (egg, milk, wool, the six meats, bacon, lard, hide, feather; the lead, the shears, the snare, with portraits where held). **It points at the chicken coop and the beehive** (`data/art/village/chicken_coop_*.png`, `beehive_*.png`) as the two most recent pieces to guide the work (Michael, 2026-10-25), and keeps the detail of the earlier briefs (Michael: "your detailed descriptions for those briefs produced better work than I would have, so keep that up").
- **The style question comes first:** how bright the valley is drawn and Plants vs Zombies as a reference (#426) are settled before real animal art is commissioned, so nothing is drawn twice. When an artist is hired, the same brief is the commission.

## 7. The guest's side (every step)

Animals are characters of the world, stepped on the host as the dead and the villagers are: in the frame as a delta with **a roster of their own** (a fourth beside the dead's, the villagers', and the corpses'), with `net.PROTOCOL` bumped; in the save; and in **their own part of `game.syncParts`**, `animals`, comparing each one's id, species, position to two decimals, state, health, dead, skinned and butchered, how it was killed, its home, and its fed and produce days. Their wander and flight read `loop.simTime()` and `rng`. Every verb (lead, let go, feed, collect, milk, shear, skin, butcher, set a snare, check a snare) is a verb in `verbs.js` or a menu label performed by the host by the animal's or prop's id; a feeding trough, a coop, and a snare are props in their chunk; products land in the actor's bag or at their feet. Each step tries its features in two tabs with `check("sync")` and reads what it adds to a frame from `net.status().wire`; animals are many and move, so the wire's cost is measured, not guessed.
