# 31.04 — The wire as built: one authority, a replica per guest

**Status, 2026-10-16: built, and this page describes it; the code is the truth (`src/game.js`, `src/host.js`, `src/net.js`, `server/relay.js`, `server/world.js`).** The design is the hybrid the plan recommended: the world written once as a headless module and run either in the host's browser page or in a Node process, with each guest's browser a replica that renders, predicts its own walking, and sends commands. The plan's options, its authority module (`src/world/`, never made), its wire of commands, events, and snapshots, the interest region, and its forecasts of lag and cost are the record, `docs/history/31-multiplayer-record.md` (`04` as it stood). Not built: interest (every guest is told the whole world), the bounded rewind, the client's own hit-stop hold (`05-someday.md`).

## The authority

`src/game.js` is the world: its state, `create` from a map file, the step order (`begin`, `step(dt, snapshots)`), `serialize` and `restore`, `hashText` and `syncParts`, `timeScaleNow`, and the player list (`addPlayer`, `takePlayer`, `putPlayer`, `removePlayer`). It runs in the host's page, headless under Node for the checks (`node tools/world.js`), and headless as a server world on the relay (`server/world.js`, since 2026-10-14). What a host does beyond the step, shared by the browser host and the server world, is `src/host.js`: the joins and whose character is whose, dormancy, stepping away and back, the routing of a message, a log line, an opened container, the spent word, and the kick to the player they are for, and the save on a death. Joining, leaving, the commands, and the frames are `src/net.js`. A guest's command runs on the host on arrival through the very handler the host's own click uses; there is no command queue in the world.

**Time.** The loop steps at 60 Hz from the browser's frames, and from a Worker's timer once the frames stop (`loop.setBackgroundDriver`), so a covered or hidden host window does not slow a world with friends in it. The time scale is the world's, computed each step from who is asleep (`game.timeScaleNow`: the sleep speed only while every living player is in bed). Pause is not a time scale of zero: a screen pauses the loop only off the wire, and a hosted world with nobody in it whose host has stepped away is held (`loop.setHeld`), answering the wire so a join starts it again.

## The wire

A guest sends its input snapshot every step (`input`, numbered for the acknowledgement: walking, clicks, buttons, the aim as a vector and a ground point, the key events E, B, and T) and its commands by type (`act`, `item`, `use`, `place`, `craft`, `say`, `poke`, `away`, `back`, `leave`). The host holds at most ten of a guest's inputs (`MAX_BACKLOG`) and applies one a step; while the world runs fast a guest's inputs go as runs.

The host sends the `welcome` (the whole save, `game.serialize`, gzipped; a browser host packs it in the background and the welcome carries the tick it is of) and then a `frame` every third step at normal speed (`net.FRAME_EVERY`, about twenty a second), paced by a running count over the world's time scale (one frame per hundred and eighty steps at sixty times). A frame carries: the chunks touched since the last frame, whole (a chunk the host grew too, since a replica grows nothing); the chunks the host dropped since the last frame (`evicted`, `[cx, cy]` each, only when there are any), which a guest drops too (`map.dropChunk`), before the frame's chunks go in; the players' records, a light part each frame and a heavy part when its shape changes; the dead, the villagers, and the corpses as a delta, with a roster when a list's membership changes; the pokes (the sways and the well's bucket); the stations' queues, the carried lights, and the rules when they change; the flights (arrows, thrown things, spells) as each one's launch once and its end once (`arrows: { go, end }`, only when there are any; #250), which a guest steps itself every step, on the clock its remote bodies are drawn at, making a fireball's flare where its drawn flight ends; the clock and the time scale. Then `log`, `message`, `opened`, `fx`, and the camera kick to the guest they are for. No event list travels: a mutator's duty is to touch its tile, and the chunk travels.

**Versions.** The join carries `net.PROTOCOL` (15 since 2026-10-25, a player's record carrying the hand chosen per belt slot, `beltHands`, #285; 14 the same day, a chunk's record naming the tiles of props anchored in another chunk, #341; each bump's reason is beside it in `net.js`) and the save's version, each bumped by hand when a message or the save changes shape, and the build id, which a server world and a page compare so that the site and the server of different commits refuse each other ("The game was updated"). The data's stamps do not travel.

**Measured** on 2026-10-13, before the deltas and the pacing: about 120 KB/s to each guest with 22 of the dead near the living, against the plan's forecast of a few kilobytes a second. The bytes of a frame are read from `net.status().wire`, never guessed.

## The replica

The client is `main.js`: boot, the data, the art, the figure sheets, the ground, the renderer, the UI, the input, the camera, the view, the checks. A guest holds a replica world: the same `map.js` chunks, installed from the welcome and the frames, the characters it has been told about, the fires, the arrows in flight, the clock. The replica is stepped for presentation only (animation frames, the smoothing of others' positions, flames, sways); it decides nothing, and it grows no chunk, since a guest's own settings could shape a chunk differently from the host's.

**My player** is predicted: the guest runs the player's controller in predict mode on its own input every step, so the walk is instant, keeps the inputs the host has not yet acknowledged, and replays them over each frame (`net.js`, `pending`). Everything else about my player (health, hunger, the swing's hit, the draw's release, the action bar) is the host's and is shown as it arrives: a round trip of latency on a swing's result, none on the swing's animation (`14`: the animation is cosmetic).

**Others** are drawn between the host's frames (`src/netsmooth.js`, #248; it replaced a walk to each frame's position in a fixed three steps, which stood still on a late frame): each remote body keeps the last few positions with the host's sim time, and is drawn `feel.netDelaySteps` (4) steps behind the newest frame, interpolated between the two either side; a frame's position is still the body's `netTo`, the host's truth the sync check hashes.

**Sight, light, fog, the ground, the sheets** are the client's own, built from the replica as they are built from the world on the host: all deterministic from the data.

**The two-tab test.** `?relay=none` is a loopback over a BroadcastChannel between two tabs of one browser (`?nosave&relay=none` on both, `__game.net.open({ join })` in the second); `?relay=ws://localhost:8012` is a local relay (`node server/relay.js 8012`, the pane's `relay` launch). The default is the Fly relay (`net.RELAY_URL`). The method is `docs/testing.md`.

## Determinism's job

Not lockstep. Three jobs:

1. **A save that loads the same** on a browser host or a server world: `game.serialize` and `restore`, proved by `check("save")` and `node tools/world.js --roundtrip`. The welcome is this save.
2. **The replica check:** `check("sync")` (`tools/checks/sync.js`) compares the world in parts (`game.syncParts`: the map, the chart, the registry, the fires, the players, the dead, the clock, the rules; not the generator or the id counter, which a replica never uses) on the host and each guest at the ticks of the frames the host marks, every tenth frame with reports on (`SYNC_EVERY_FRAMES`), and names the first part that differs. This is the test that says the wire is complete: a change that bypassed its mutator shows up as a part that differs. It found two ghosts in the host's registry on its first run.
3. **The same world under Node:** `node tools/world.js` boots the world with no window and prints the pane's hash, and CI compares it, so the step is proved without a browser.

The host's rolls stay on `rng.js`; the replica has an rng too and uses it for nothing that matters. The wild is grown by the host alone and travels as chunks, so wild chunks need not grow the same everywhere.
