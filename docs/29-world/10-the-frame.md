# 29/10 — The frame: a bounded world in an engine that grew forever

**Status, 2026-10-28: a plan, revised after Primary's scan and Michael's answers to it (#558); his rulings logged in `02` (2026-10-28, The world is bounded); nothing built.** Written from `origin/main` at 26a0a8f6, with `49`'s storeys and stairs already built there (#533, #534, #549) and its ladders, cellars, sight and shooting down, and the valley's upper floors not. It answers one question: how a hand-designed world of a valley and a few regions lives in an engine that, until now, grew forest chunks forever. A **default** is the Secondary's own, Michael's to overrule; section 10 lists them and the one open problem that is his to solve. Nothing here is urgent ("not mega high priority"); it exists so the map tools are designed with it in mind.

## 1. The conflict, in Michael's words

"The current game is a chunked world that right now builds out infinite chunks of woods with 2 zombies lol. The vision for the post-demo, post-valley game however is a valley-as-starting-zone with 4ish outlaying regions, accessible through some kind of passage, with their own borders, and therefore the valley needs borders else you could like wander around the back of the regions. We have an endless chunked world a a finite game, more zones like in wow in a way, where the valley is large but not the whole game. How do we square this circle? ... We also have no elevation right now ... Which means no mountains to block off passage like in wow. This need of ours for a bounded designed world is very different from zomboid, which has a map but endless forest at the edge."

His answer, in two parts. The frame: **"the whole area would be fully bounded on the outside too, so even if we leave the ground flat around the outside, we will river or tree or stone if off. There may be forest outside for a smidge but it's just decoration."** And, when the first draft kept the endless wild beyond it as scenery: **"we were simply never going to have an endless off-screen map with a plan for bounded regions. It was never going to work, else you could walk out of the valley, around the Crimson Whatever, and view the back of that region. It must be a bounded map."** On the materials: "I liked all 3 options (wall of trees, actual rock elevation, and rivers) just about equally, especially rivers because we already have bridges in the game so we can separate all of them by water if needed, maybe trees for the snow region, TBD."

## 2. The shape

- **The map is bounded, full stop** (ruled). The world is one file's worth of authored ground: the valley and every region as lobes of one continuous map (`00`, the first law), with the map's own scatter rules filling the spaces the authoring leaves empty (`02`, 2026-10-16: "a hand-authored world with procedural filling of the spaces it leaves empty"; its "the infinite grown wilderness ... stays open" is overturned by this ruling and struck there). There is no wild rule any more: no chunk is ever grown by hash beyond the map, not even as scenery. Beyond the map there is nothing, and the camera never shows it (section 5).
- **A frame** closes the world: a line of things that block bodies, made of river, tree wall, or cliff by stretch (section 3), between each region and the valley, between region and region, and round the outside of all of it. Nothing crosses the frame but at a **passage** (section 4).
- **Outside the frame, a smidge of decoration:** an authored band of the same forest, rock, or water a few chunks deep, part of the map, never walkable because the frame is between; it is the world's last tiles, not a wild.
- **No invisible walls** (the plan's rule, from his "Invisible walls?"): every edge of the world is a thing the player can see and name, made only of materials the engine has or has planned, each a wall by the rules every wall obeys: walls are state on tile edges (`src/CLAUDE.md`), water is a floor no body walks (`30` law 8), a thicket is a solid prop.
- **Ground is always layer 0** (ruled, Michael's "earth always 0": "ground level is always layer zero, aka the level where the ENTIRE game was until we added second floors. This is the system zomboid uses. Upstairs are positive integers, downstairs are negative ones. The root cellar floor would sit at −1"). Nothing in this plan moves the ground.
- **A word:** `01-geography` calls the valley's nine districts "regions". Since 2026-10-16 "region" is the word for a place beyond the valley (`02`), so this doc says **district** for the valley's parts and **region** for the lobes beyond; `01` is not rewritten for it until it is next rewritten for something else (*default*).

## 3. The three frame materials, per stretch

Michael likes all three about equally; the frame is chosen **per stretch of edge**, not one material for the world.

| | River | Tree wall | Cliff |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blocks bodies | yes: `walk: false` for every walker, the dead included; nobody swims | yes, as a **thicket kind** (below); an ordinary tree does not | yes: a wall edge |
| Blocks sight | no | no (trunks do not cut sight; only the canopy's soft hole) | yes, opaque |
| Blocks arrows | no (a flight crosses water; the bow's `range` is 22 tiles) | low flights hit trunks (`trunkHeight` 40); a lobbed arrow clears | to its height; the bow's `arcMax` 150 clears a one-storey cliff |
| Its passage | a **bridge** or a **ford** (both built by mapgen today) | a **road through the thicket**, two tiles wide | a **gorge** (a gap in the cliff line) or a **slope** (a stair with slope art; as built a stair climbs north or west, #549, so a hill is climbed from its south or east foot) |
| New engine | none | none (a sprite kind) | none beyond `49`: a wall material and floor tiles |
| New art | none (`29/09` has the river and bridge) | one thicket kind per register (thorn; dense conifers for a snow region) | a `cliff` wall material, its top rounding into the floor above, its rounded corner (`50`'s quarter), slope art for the stair, `ground_rock` |
| Suits | most borders; the valley's north and south, where the river already runs | the snow region ("maybe trees for the snow region, TBD"); the valley's west | wherever height is wanted: looking down into the valley, or up at a region |

- **A river border lets you see across** (*default*: keep it, a teaser) **and shoot across**: a bow on the bank can pick off a gated region's dead, which a failed path walks to the water's edge; the sniper's-nest ruling (`02`, 2026-10-26) accepts the same from a window. Michael's call whether that is fine or a border river wants a tree line on its far bank where the far side matters (section 10).
- **A tree wall is a thicket kind, not a tree.** An ordinary tree collides as a trunk circle (`collide.radius` 0.22 for an oak against a body's 0.2), so two side by side leave 0.16 of slack and a walking body slips between, and with the world's Trees rule off an authored tree is never placed at all. The thicket is a sprite kind with **whole-tile collision** (no `collide.radius`), **placed whatever the Trees setting says**, never felled, giving no sticks, not `interactable` (so it never sways; trees are excused from the outline law), with the canopy's soft hole as any tree. Two tiles deep so no canopy gap reads as a path. A path through it is a road feature with the thicket cleared, which the thicket closes beside.
- **A cliff is a wall and a floor, nothing new** (ruled, Michael, with a drawing: "Imagine the build tool allowing you to build a wall but instead of wood or stone it's bare grey cliff, rounds at the top corner, and meets up with a tile above to form a cliff face. These two tiles are really no different from a wall and a floor tile on the second floor (level 1)"). A **cliff wall** material on layer 0 (96 px, `52`: every wall one storey tall, a cliff taller by storeys as the castle is) with the plateau's **floor tiles on layer 1** sitting on its cap; the next storey the same again; a stair with slope art where the land climbs; and where the world should end upward, no stair ("no more stairs. Done"). **The inside of a hill is ordinary layer-0 ground** behind the cliff walls: unlit, with no entity, unreachable, and so dark ("big empty dark space. That is fine with me"). The pieces tile by construction, since a wall is a storey and a storey is a floor's height; the art meets at the cap and the corner as house walls do.
  - **What a plateau asks of `49`**, none of it cliff-specific: seeing and shooting down from it is `49`'s steps 4 and 5, the same work the second floor needs ("it should be no more difficulty than shooting from map level +1 on the 2nd story of any building, perhaps easier because there is no wall with a window to navigate"); an overlook makes every ground tile in view a height ray, which step 4's cost row should measure with a plateau as its worst case. The hiding rule and the storey fade read the building index as one rect a layer (`render/storeys.js`), and a hill is not a rect: `plateau()` wants a **per-tile footprint** there, or a player in a notch hides the whole plateau. `chart.step` skips a player off layer 0, so a plateau is uncharted until the per-layer chart.
  - **`plateau()` reserves its footprint on layer 0** (the scan's finding): no scatter tree under a plateau (one taller than 96 px would poke through its floor), no resource comeback there, and the interior marked so the room fill does not count it as a room or, past 300 tiles, as outdoors.
  - Whether a plateau top is buildable ground: `49`'s support rule lets an authored floor hold anything, so a house on a hill is allowed unless ruled otherwise (*default*: allowed).

## 4. Passages and gates

- **A passage is the only gap in a stretch of frame:** a bridge or ford in a river, a road through a thicket, a gorge or slope in a cliff, or a **tunnel** on layer −1 (section 6). It is one to three tiles wide, so it is a place, not a border: a boss can hold it, a siege can press it, and a player can watch it from a window.
- **A gate** bars a passage until its boss falls: an authored thing in the passage (a portcullis, a fallen gate, an arch grown over with thorn, a landslide on a slope, a flooded ford that drains) that **is not a door**: the dead's breach and `wayIn` never look at it, a siege never presses it, E does nothing to it, nobody builds or takes it apart, and the stamp refuses it. It opens by its `key` alone, the boss's death, in the world's state (saved, sent with its chunk, in the sync check's map part), **and stays open** (ruled already, `02` 2026-10-13: "the gate opens once for the world"; the boss can be fought again). Opening is a player's deed, never a clock's (`00`, Never harder on a clock).
- **Where the boss stands** is the passage itself or the ground before it on the valley's side (*default*): the fight is at the door, so a player who is not ready can turn round, and the frame is why it cannot be walked round.
- **The register changes at the passage, not as a gradient** (`00`: "a change of region, not a gradient"): each region is a **zone** (`05`, Zones; `data/ground.json`'s zones, `map.zoneAt`): its ground palette and scatter mix (built), plus its ambient light and fog colour, its sound bed, and its weather, when those exist, crossfaded over a few tiles at the passage's mouth (*default*: three). Inside the passage the register is the far side's from its midpoint.
- **The valley's first gate** is the one `02-water-and-roads` already wrote: the waystone at the end of the old road east ("The old road. Nobody goes this way now."). Its passage and gate are authored with the valley's frame from the start, shut, with nothing behind it until the east region's file supplies the far side (section 7).

## 5. The edge of the world, and a big map

- **Beyond the map there is nothing, and the camera never shows it:** the view is clamped so its far corner stays inside the authored map (a 3840 by 2160 window at zoom 1.1 reaches about 58 tiles out along an axis from the camera's tile), and `getChunk` beyond the map answers the frozen blank and grows nothing (`49`'s mechanism, already built: a blank per layer, no chunk for the empty). The decoration band of section 2 is what the clamp lets you see past the frame. (*Default*: a clamp; the alternative is a band wider than any view, which is four chunks of nothing on every side.)
- **The explored world is finite.** Everything a player can stand on, chart, build on, or change is inside the frame, so a world's size is bounded by the map's area. **The frame is also the end of the chart:** the map on M draws the frame's line as the world's edge (*default*).
- **A big map loads only what is near a player** (ruled, Michael: "We are going to need a big map, we're gonna have to figure out a way to not load the whole thing at one time and only load chunks near an active player"). Today every loaded chunk is saved (`game.serialize` writes `map.savedChunks`) and `evictFar` drops only wild, untouched chunks, so authored chunks are never dropped and the save and a guest's welcome grow with every lobe walked. The plan's step 4: **an untouched authored chunk far from every player is evicted and regrown from the map file**, exactly as a wild chunk is today, and the save keeps only touched chunks; a touched chunk is never dropped. The simulation's four-chunk ring round each player (`02`, 2026-10-13) is unchanged. This is engine work, not a property the game has.

## 6. Underground: mines, cellars, and tunnels

- A cave or a mine is `49`'s cellar model, on layer −1: below the ground every unauthored tile is earth (solid, opaque, never walkable, never dug; drawn as `49` says), and a gallery is a floor carved out of it, reached by a stair or a ladder from a mine mouth above. The rock faces along a gallery's walls are laid by the loader on the boundary between floor and earth, as `50` lays a ring's stepped edges from an outline: ordinary `kind: "wall"` edges of the `cliff` material, refusing the stamp.
- **Ore stays on the ground** (Michael: "still only on the ground, so no change there"): the resource nodes are the props they are today, placed by the same rules in a gallery's rects. No veins on walls.
- **A tunnel is a passage** on layer −1: a gallery from one lobe's mine mouth to another's, with earth as its frame on both sides. It is the cheapest border there is, and a mine region needs no frame at all.
- The inside of a hill (section 3) is not earth and not a cellar: it is layer-0 ground nobody reaches.

## 7. Mapgen and the loader

- **The loader's bounds become the map.** `map.js` keeps one authored rectangle (`bounds`) and treats everything beyond it as wild. It takes instead the map's own extent (the union of its lobes' rects) and **the frame as a mask**: inside the frame is the playable world (authored where authored, the map's scatter rules where not, plain floor only where a lobe authors nothing at all), the decoration band outside it is authored and unreachable, and beyond the map is the blank. Every reader of `bounds` (the resource rects, the scatter zones, the room fill's "out past the bounds is open ground", the tree test, the chart) reads the mask. The `wild` rule and `ensureAround`'s wild growth go.
- **Three mapgen primitives:** `frame(points, material)`, a closed polyline laid as a feature (a river by `river`, a thicket as a band two deep, a cliff as `cliff` edges with `50`'s rounded corners), with `passage(at, kind, gate)` cutting its gaps and naming their gates; and `plateau(outline, storeys, slopes)` laying a hill's walls, corners, floors, and stairs by storey, reserving its footprint. **Where stretches meet** is a corner piece the primitive lays (a river into a cliff: the cliff ends on the bank; a thicket into a river: the trees run to the water); **a road never ends at the frame** but at a gate, and **a river crosses the frame as itself** (water is frame), so the river entering the valley from the north runs through a gorge in the north cliff, and leaves south through the south frame as water.
- **A region is its own file,** stitched into the world by an offset. The scan's findings and their fixes: prop ids are the world counter in load order and resource ids `res_<kind>_<i>` by load index, so **every file's ids carry its prefix** (`east.res_stone_3`) and never collide or shift; there is one `wild` rule and one `spawn` a map today, so the scatter and decoration zone is **chosen per stretch and per lobe**, not once; and **a shared stretch of frame belongs to the valley's file**, which authors its passage points and their gates shut, so a region file supplies only what lies beyond and the gate's key: the valley's generator is not edited when a region is built.
- **The valley's four edges**, as `01` promised them, answered by material (*defaults*, corrected to the map as built): **north**, where the river enters at the top edge: a cliff line with the river through a gorge, the quarry's conifer slope rising into a north region when there is one; **east**, the old road: a thicket, the waystone its gate, the gloom beyond (`02-water-and-roads`); **south**: the ford at y 206 is the drove's crossing *inside* the valley, with the croft beyond it, so the south frame runs south of the croft as a thicket, the drove through it toward "the next village" with a gate, and the river leaving through it as water; **west**, the old way past the standing stones: a thicket, the path petering out at it with no passage until a west region exists.

## 8. The guest's side, the save, the wire, the sync check

The frame is map: walls, floors, and props in chunks, saved and sent as every chunk is, nothing new. A gate is a record in its chunk with its `key` and its state, in the sync check's map part; its opening is the host's step (a boss's death in `host.js`'s world), reaching every client as a chunk touch. A region's zone is data every client loads. Evicting and regrowing authored chunks (section 5) is the host's and the save's: a guest's welcome carries the touched chunks only, as it carries the save. `net.PROTOCOL` moves for the gate's record.

## 9. Laws and rulings this leans on

One continuous map and no scene transitions (`00`); never harder on a clock (`00`); bosses gate regions and can be fought again, the gate opening once for the world (`02`, 2026-10-13); "region" is the word (`02`, 2026-10-16); a hand-authored world with procedural filling (`02`, 2026-10-16, its grown-wilderness clause overturned here); water stops every walker (`30` law 8); walls are state on tile edges (`src/CLAUDE.md`); nothing digs and no wall breaks (`00`); ground is always layer 0, upstairs positive, cellars negative (`02`, 2026-10-28); upper floors, round rooms, the depth buffer, and walls at 96 (`49`, `50`, `51`, `52`). `05-someday`'s Zones are bounded by this.

## 10. Michael's: one open problem, and the defaults

**The open problem, not a default:** **where new dead come from inside a bounded map.** Wild chunks are the only source of new dead today (the valley authors 62; nothing respawns them), and with no wild a cleared valley stays empty for good, against "the game is too easy" (`02`, 2026-10-15) and `05`'s Endless. Michael: "We'll need to fix this." Candidates, none chosen: the graveyard rising (`01` promises it); a spawn rule on the map's own forest rects, in bounds, as the wild rule spawned (so many per chunk of deep wood, never in view); the dead walking in through an open passage from a region's stock; the Starting population setting as a floor the world refills to. His to rule; step 9 builds it.

The defaults, his to overrule:
1. The valley's four edges by material (section 7).
2. A river border seen across: kept as a teaser. **Shot across, and lobbed over a one-storey cliff:** his call (a tree line on the far bank where it matters, or accepted).
3. The boss stands at the passage, on the valley's side.
4. Three tiles of crossfade at a passage's mouth.
5. The camera clamped to the map, with a decoration band a few chunks deep.
6. A plateau top is buildable ground.
7. The chart ends at the frame.
8. "District" for the valley's nine parts.
