## Performance: how to tell what is cheap (Claude's answer to Michael's question, 2026-10-12)

**The atlas is a capacity limit, not a speed one.** All sprites are packed into one 4096 by 4096 texture (a 64 MB RGBA texture on the GPU and a 64 MB canvas copy on the CPU, both paid in full from the start) so the whole world draws with almost no texture switching. Running out means a new sprite does not fit (skipped with one warning; `gl.atlasInfo()` reads the fill: 42 percent on 2026-10-12). The fix is a taller atlas (8192 is 256 MB a copy, heavy for integrated GPUs) or a second page (a second texture and draw call: cheap). **The real cost is uploads:** whenever a sprite is placed or a sheet redrawn the whole canvas is re-uploaded (`gl.js`, `texImage2D` in `end()`), measured 1 to 14 ms for the full atlas against 0.2 to 4 ms for one sheet's rectangle (`texSubImage2D`): the fix when it matters; step 1's up-front baking means it happens at boot, not in play.

**A rule of thumb: is the thing a definition, an instance, or per-frame work?** *Definitions* (items, recipes, tags, loot tables, text) are data and cost almost nothing: thousands are fine. *Contents of a bag* are DOM nodes only while a panel is open: hundreds are fine. *Static instances* (a wheelbarrow, a crate, a station) are one quad each, drawn only in view: cheap. *New art* costs atlas space, a one-time upload, and boot time (one request per file; step 1's manifest and bounded batches are the fix). *Things that move, think, light, or update the DOM every frame* are the real per-frame cost, proportional to how many are near the player: the dead and villagers (AI, pathing, sight; the far dead stand still, and after step 2 the corpses cost nothing), **light sources** (each casts rays per chunk it reaches, and after step 2 only the chunks near a change rebuild), particles, and DOM overlays updated every frame (bars, bubbles; hundreds would hurt). GPU fill rate is not a concern for 2D sprites.

**Where the budget is (2026-10-12, the pane):** at the middle zoom the step is 0.2 ms, sight 0.2, render 0.3; at the wide zoom 0.5, 0.6, 0.5, with 67 dead and 158 drawables: **about fifteen times headroom in a 16.7 ms frame.** After a big addition, report the profile. For the big pass (`22`): stations, a wheelbarrow, tools, items, recipes, and seeds are the cheap kinds; the two to be deliberate about are light-emitting stations (a dozen in view is fine, hundreds is not) and the number of separate image files at boot, which the manifest makes a non-issue up to the server's queue.
