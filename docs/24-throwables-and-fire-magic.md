## 1. Throwing knives and throwing axes (ruled 2026-10-25)

**Ruled by Michael, 2026-10-25** (`02`, Throwing knives and axes ruled): shown Primary's proposal, he answered "Drop axe damage to 30 and knife to 15. Otherwise adopted, you can write the spec", asked for "a spinning knife or axe art to send along the arc because it'll look awesome", and set the breaks: they lie "on bodies of dead zombies or ground like arrows", "knives having a 30% chance to break, and axes like a 10% chance to break". **His:** each one's job, the damage, the ranges, the knife's stagger chance and no knockdown, the axe always staggering and its knockdown at 0.35, the breaks, the stacks, Blacksmithing 3, the spin, landing and lodging like arrows. A **default** is Primary's, marked where it stands, his to overrule.

**Each has a job.** The knife pulls: cheap, fast, far, and light, one of the dead drawn out of a group at range. The axe stops: slower and shorter, but every hit staggers and some floor the dead.

**The numbers** (each item's `thrown` block in `data/items.json`, levers in `42`):
- **Throwing knife:** damage 18 at a full draw (nine to kill one of the dead at 150; 15 until Michael's playtest of 2026-10-26, `02`, Throwing knives do 18); range 13 tiles; stagger chance 0.3 (a stagger stops the walk and ends a bite already landed; every landed hit, staggering or not, cancels a bite's wind-up, `zombie.js`); no knockdown; breaks 30% of throws; stacks 20. *Defaults:* speed 22; a low arc (`arcMin` 4, `arcMax` 20, `z0` 30: an arc like an arrow's but lower, not the spell's `flat`); it peaks about 38 px up at full range, under a wall's 96 px a shot must clear (`wallStops`, `archery.js`), so a wall stops it; a fence stops a flight only below its own 36 px (`52`, 2026-10-27), so a knife clears one only near its peak; draw 0.2 s; push 0.15.
- **Throwing axe:** damage 30 at a full draw (five to kill); range 9 tiles; always staggers (`staggerChance` 1, within the dead's stagger immunity of 0.6 s, as every stagger is); knocks down at 0.35 (the mace's chance); breaks 10% of throws; stacks 10. *Defaults:* speed 13; a short arc (`arcMin` 6, `arcMax` 60, `z0` 36), peaking at about 79 px at full range, under a wall's 96, so a wall stops it too, and over a fence's 36 mid-flight (a fence stops a flight at its own height since `52`, 2026-10-27); the spear's, about 102 px at full range with its `arcMax` 80 (70 and about 93 px with the 80 px walls), clears a wall; draw 0.45 s; push 0.35.
- For scale: an arrow 38 at 22 tiles and never breaks; the spear up to 150 at 6.5 tiles, breaking 25%; a sword's swing 34.

**Made at Blacksmithing 3** (ruled 2026-10-23, open until the skills), at the anvil with a hammer in a hand (the anvil rows' `tool: "hammer"`). *Defaults:* **one iron bar makes ten knives**; **one iron bar and a plank make two axes** (a plank for the haft, where other hafts are sticks, so the axe is dearer). The axe costs more to make per item at five knives a bar or ten. Primary's reading of "Axes ... must cost more to make" is that it should cost more **per throw** too, the cost a player feels: a break is rolled on every throw, hit or miss (the spear's rule, `archery.js`), so a knife lasts 1 / 0.3, about 3.3 throws, and an axe 10; at ten knives a bar a knife's throw costs about 0.03 of a bar and an axe's 0.05, where at five it was 0.06, more than the axe's. Per kill, with every throw landing, knives cost about 0.27 of a bar (nine throws, about 2.7 knives) and axes 0.25 (five throws, half an axe): the knife is the dearer way to kill at either count, as a puller should be.

**How they work, with what exists.** The draw, the cursor read, the aim guide, half-speed walking while drawn, `Esc`/panel/sprint cancel, the right-tap cancel, landing-only hits by `hitRadius`, the obstacle scan, and lodging are all the spear's. What is new:
1. **The left button throws them.** They have no melee block, so a pure throwable's primary mode is the throw (`launcherOf` returns `button: "primary"` for an item with a `thrown` block and no `weapon` block, which `player-controller.js` already reads; the spear, which also stabs, keeps the throw on its other mode). From the main hand only; a stack drawn into the off hand (from an off-hand belt slot) is only held (*default*: no off-hand throw; dual throwing is noted, not planned). The pose is the spear's `throw` style, held one-handed (*default*; a one-handed throw pose is owed if it reads wrong).
2. **A throw takes one from the stack.** The spear leaves the hand whole; a stack in the hand loses one a throw, and the hand empties only when the count reaches nothing. **The thrown one is a new entry with a fresh uid** (`nextId("e")`, `count: 1`), never a copy carrying the stack's uid (today `releaseDraw` copies the entry, `archery.js`, and `map.dropItem` mints a uid only when none is set), so a guest's addressing by uid holds and the item census sees no duplicate; lodged knives still stack in the loot, as `grid.insert` merges stackables. An emptied hand is not refilled from the bag (*default*: the belt's hand key draws the next stack). The belt's roles (`02`, The belt's slots have roles) hold them as any one-handed weapon.
3. **The axe's knockdown is new work.** A shot carries only `staggerChance` to `damageZombie` today (`shoot` in `archery.js` has no `knockdown`, and `damageZombie`'s `knockdown` is a boolean its caller rolls, as `combat.js` does), so the build carries `thrown.knockdown` through `launcherOf`, `releaseDraw`, and `shoot` to the landing, rolls it there on the host (`rng.chance`), and passes the boolean.
4. **They spin along the arc** (Michael: "it'll look awesome"): a projectile sheet per kind generated from its drawn art (`data/art/items/throwing_knife_world.png`, `throwing_axe_world.png`, read as data) by turning the one picture through eight steps of 45 degrees with the nearest-neighbour sampling `src/figure/held.js` uses for a held item's angles, so every frame is whole pixels and nothing is scaled in `drawImage`; registered as `knife_spin` and `axe_spin` and named by each item's `thrown.projectile`, so a flight's launch record (`p`) carries it to a guest; played at `thrown.spin` turns a second (*default*: 2 for the knife, a flat blur, and 1.5 for the axe, end over end); the landed one is its picture lying at the landing angle, as an arrow's is. The art agent's own spin frames replace the generated ones when drawn: an issue in the Art owed milestone, filed with the build.
5. **Where they end up: like arrows** (Michael). A hit lodges it in the body: it comes out in the body's loot, stacking, as arrows do; a miss lands it on the ground at the landing point, as a floor item. A broken one is gone (the roll is made as it comes down). **E on a pile** takes every throwable of that kind on the tile and its neighbours, each floor entry by its uid (a key event performed in the host's step, `interact.keyEvents`), and the menu's Pick up on one takes the same pile (*default*), so a fight's worth of knives is not a hundred presses.
6. **The cards** say each one's damage, range, stagger, knockdown, and break chance from the data. `item-info.shotLines` today writes "Throw (middle button or Q)" for every thrown thing and no stagger or knockdown line: the build reads the button from the item (the left button for a pure throwable) and adds the two lines. The `toCome` note goes, and with it the asserts that read it (`tools/check.js` and `tools/checks/hovercard.js`, the thrown cards' rows).
7. **Art owed:** as real held weapons they need a 38 by 38 `portrait` each for the character screen's slot (the art-pair law); until drawn, each slot shows the item's square bag icon, as `portraitOf` (`ui/inventory-panel.js`) already falls back to it, and the issue in the Art owed milestone names both.

**The guest's side.** A guest's draw and release are resolved on the host as the spear's are (`31`, tier 2); the stack's count changes on the host's copy of the hand and reaches the guest in its player's record; a flight's launch and end reach the guest as every flight's do (`arrows: { go, end }`), and the guest draws the spin from the launch record's `p` on its own clock; the landed ones are floor entries in the touched chunks, the lodged ones entries in the body's bag; the sweep is a key event; the hit, the knockdown, and the break are the host's rolls (`rng`). No new world state is added, so `game.syncParts` is unchanged; its item census asserts no duplicate uid after a run of throws. A two-tab test throws from the guest and sweeps up on the host.

**One build issue**, beside the potions' steps (it touches `archery.js`, the items, the cards, and the render of flights, not the potions' files). `check("throwing")`.

## 2. Fire magic: the staff and the book

Built as the status says. Of the summary's design, what still stands to be decided is the burst's reach and the cost:

- **The landing: a burst.** Flame, ember, and smoke particles at the landing point (`fire.js` has them), and **since #14 (2026-10-15) the body a fireball hits is always set alight, a real burn** (`burn.js`, the spell's `ignite`). The particles are made in the host's step, so a guest sees the burst only if the frame carries the landing (a point and a kind, as the pokes carry a sway): the flames of a burning body were missed that way until the playtest of 2026-10-13 (`02`); the burning flag already rides to guests.
- **The landing: a flare** (#156, Michael, 2026-10-17: "a fireball lights the way a bit as it travels... a second life as a utility flare for lighting areas up quickly. Especially if it lands on something it can set on fire"): a fireball throws `fireballGlow` (6 tiles) of light in flight and leaves a short light of `flareRadius` (7) where it lands, shrinking to nothing over `flareSeconds` (3), light only through the one light function; the landing lights a campfire with fuel or a mounted torch that is out within `kindleRadius` (1) (`map.kindleNear`: fire lights what is made to be lit; **kindling stops at a wall (the line from the landing to the torch or fire must be clear, as the hit's is) and passes hearths, ovens, and forges by: a campfire or a torch only**; nothing else burns, nothing spreads). All four numbers are `data/combat.json` `bow`; the damage is the book's `spell.damage`, 45, to be tuned with #133.

**Cost and limits.** A spell without a cost is the bow without arrows. Options: **a cooldown only** (2.5 s, shown on a swing-style bar, the third in the stack of bars); **stamina** as a stand-in (a cast costs 40, the orange bar); **mana** (a new blue bar, the colour is reserved; regenerating slowly, restored by a potion or a rest). Recommendation: a cooldown and a stamina cost to start (no new bar), mana when there is a second spell, because a bar is only worth building for a system.
