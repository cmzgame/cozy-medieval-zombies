## 1. Throwing knives and throwing axes (ruled 2026-10-25)

**Ruled by Michael, 2026-10-25** (`02`, Throwing knives and axes ruled): shown Primary's proposal, he answered "Drop axe damage to 30 and knife to 15. Otherwise adopted, you can write the spec", asked for "a spinning knife or axe art to send along the arc because it'll look awesome", and set the breaks: they lie "on bodies of dead zombies or ground like arrows", "knives having a 30% chance to break, and axes like a 10% chance to break". A **default** below is Primary's, his to overrule.

**Each has a job.** The knife pulls: cheap, fast, far, and light, one of the dead drawn out of a group at range. The axe stops: slower and shorter, but every hit staggers and some floor the dead.

**The numbers** (each item's `thrown` block in `data/items.json`, levers in `42`):
- **Throwing knife:** damage 15 at a full draw (ten to kill one of the dead at 150); range 13 tiles; speed 22, a flat flight; draw 0.2 s; push 0.15; stagger chance 0.3 (it may interrupt a bite on a friend); no knockdown; breaks 30% of throws; stacks 20.
- **Throwing axe:** damage 30 at a full draw (five to kill); range 9 tiles; speed 13, a short arc; draw 0.45 s; push 0.35; always staggers (`staggerChance` 1); knocks down at 0.35 (the mace's chance, `knockdown`); breaks 10% of throws; stacks 10.
- For scale: an arrow 38 at 22 tiles and never breaks; the spear up to 150 at 6.5 tiles, breaking 25%; a sword's swing 34.

**Made at Blacksmithing 3** (ruled 2026-10-23, open until the skills), at the anvil with a smith's hammer carried, the axe dearer: *default*, **one iron bar makes ten knives** and **one iron bar and a plank make two axes**. Primary's arithmetic for the ten: a break is rolled on every throw, hit or miss (the spear's rule, `archery.js`), so a knife lasts 1 / 0.3, about 3.3 throws, and an axe 10. With ten knives a bar a knife's throw costs about 0.03 of a bar and an axe's 0.05, so the axe costs more a throw; a kill of one of the dead with every throw landing costs about 0.3 of a bar in knives (ten throws, three knives) and 0.25 in axes (five throws, half an axe), so neither is the cheap way to kill. At the five knives a bar first proposed, a knife's throw cost more than an axe's (0.06), against "Axes ... must cost more to make".

**How they work, with what exists.** The draw, the cursor read, the aim guide, half-speed walking while drawn, `Esc`/panel/sprint cancel, the right-tap cancel, landing-only hits by `hitRadius`, the obstacle scan, and lodging are all the spear's, unchanged. What is new:
1. **The left button throws them.** They have no melee block, so a pure throwable's primary mode is the throw (`launcherOf` returns `button: "primary"` for an item with a `thrown` block and no `weapon` block; the spear, which also stabs, keeps the throw on its other mode). From the main hand; one held in the off hand is only held (*default*: no off-hand throw; dual throwing is noted, not planned).
2. **A throw takes one from the stack.** The spear leaves the hand whole; a stack in the hand loses one a throw, and the hand empties only when the count reaches nothing (`releaseDraw` copies the entry with `count: 1` and takes one from the held). The belt's roles (`02`, The belt's slots have roles) hold them as any one-handed weapon: a hand slot's key draws the stack.
3. **They spin along the arc** (Michael: "it'll look awesome"): a projectile sheet per kind generated from its drawn art (`data/art/items/throwing_knife_world.png`, `throwing_axe_world.png`) by turning the one picture through eight steps of 45 degrees, played along the flight at about two turns a second for the knife and one and a half for the axe (*default*), the knife a flat blur and the axe end over end; the landed one is its picture lying at the landing angle, as an arrow's is. The sheets are generated at boot from the delivered art (rotated whole pixels, never scaled in `drawImage`), registered as `knife_spin` and `axe_spin`, the art agent's own spin frames replacing them when drawn (owed in `docs/26`).
4. **Where they end up: like arrows** (Michael). A hit lodges it in the body: it comes out in the body's loot, stacking, as arrows do; a miss lands it on the ground at the landing point, as a floor item. A broken one is gone (the roll is made as it comes down). **E on a pile** takes every throwable of that kind on the tile and its neighbours, each floor entry by its uid (a key event performed in the host's step, `interact.keyEvents`), so a fight's worth of knives is not a hundred presses.
5. **The cards** say each one's damage, range, stagger, knockdown, and break chance from the data (`item-info.shotLines`), and the `toCome` note goes.

**The guest's side.** A guest's draw and release are resolved on the host as the spear's are (`31`, tier 2); the stack's count changes on the host's copy of the hand and reaches the guest in its player's record; a flight's launch and end reach the guest as every flight's do (`arrows: { go, end }`), and the guest draws the spin from the launch record on its own clock; the landed ones are floor entries in the touched chunks, the lodged ones entries in the body's bag; the sweep is a key event; the hit and the break are the host's rolls (`rng`). A two-tab test throws from the guest and sweeps up on the host.

**One build issue**, beside the potions' steps (it touches `archery.js`, the items, and the render of flights, not the potions' files). `check("throwing")`.

## 2. Fire magic: the staff and the book

Built as the status says. Of the summary's design, what still stands to be decided is the burst's reach and the cost:

- **The landing: a burst.** Flame, ember, and smoke particles at the landing point (`fire.js` has them), and **since #14 (2026-10-15) the body a fireball hits is always set alight, a real burn** (`burn.js`, the spell's `ignite`). The particles are made in the host's step, so a guest sees the burst only if the frame carries the landing (a point and a kind, as the pokes carry a sway): the flames of a burning body were missed that way until the playtest of 2026-10-13 (`02`); the burning flag already rides to guests.
- **The landing: a flare** (#156, Michael, 2026-10-17: "a fireball lights the way a bit as it travels... a second life as a utility flare for lighting areas up quickly. Especially if it lands on something it can set on fire"): a fireball throws `fireballGlow` (6 tiles) of light in flight and leaves a short light of `flareRadius` (7) where it lands, shrinking to nothing over `flareSeconds` (3), light only through the one light function; the landing lights a campfire with fuel or a mounted torch that is out within `kindleRadius` (1) (`map.kindleNear`: fire lights what is made to be lit; **kindling stops at a wall (the line from the landing to the torch or fire must be clear, as the hit's is) and passes hearths, ovens, and forges by: a campfire or a torch only**; nothing else burns, nothing spreads). All four numbers are `data/combat.json` `bow`; the damage is the book's `spell.damage`, 45, to be tuned with #133.

**Cost and limits.** A spell without a cost is the bow without arrows. Options: **a cooldown only** (2.5 s, shown on a swing-style bar, the third in the stack of bars); **stamina** as a stand-in (a cast costs 40, the orange bar); **mana** (a new blue bar, the colour is reserved; regenerating slowly, restored by a potion or a rest). Recommendation: a cooldown and a stamina cost to start (no new bar), mana when there is a second spell, because a bar is only worth building for a system.
