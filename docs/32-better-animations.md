# 32 — Better animations: the poses the game lacks, as data

**Status: specified 2026-10-13 from Michael's list; nothing built. Scoped for implementation by another model (see `CLAUDE.md`, "Implementing from a spec"): the doc decides, "default" marks what Michael may overrule.** The rig is final until a human artist (`02`, "We have a body"), so every item here is a pose on the rig the game already has, not a change to the rig: a new pose set in the poses data, an animation hook where the game plays it, and the sheets rebaked. More frames are not the point (Michael): the idle and the walk are at the fidelity he wants; the exceptions are named below.

## What exists, and the rules that bind every item

- **The rig** (`src/figure/rig.js`): a pose is a handful of numbers from `restPose()`: `bob` (px down), `lean` (body units forward), `armPitch` and `armOut` per arm `[left, right]`, `stride` and `lift` per leg, `headD`, `crouch` (a scale on heights above the feet), `lying`, `flash`. `pose(variant, anim, i, holding, style)` builds the frame's pose: the generic animations are a `switch` in code; **the attack's four keyframes come from the poses data** (`POSES[style]`, `data/poses.json`: `one_handed`, `zombie`, `bow`, `throw`, each `{ fps, activeFrame, frames: [{ pitch, out, lean, bob, offPitch, offOut }] }`), and `animsFor(spec)` gives the attack the style's length, speed, and active frame. `figure.setPoses(json)` loads the file; the layers editor's **Reload poses** re-reads it and redraws, so a number is tried by editing the file and pressing Reload, or from the console (`__game.figure.setPoses(...)`, then `__game.setLook({})` rebakes the player).
- **The animation table** (`ANIMS` in `rig.js`): `idle` 2 frames at 2 fps, `walk` 4 at 8, `attack` 4 at 12 with the active frame 1, `hurt` 2 at 10, `die` 4 at 8, `carry_idle` and `carry_walk`. The carry pair is left off every sheet but the player's (`noCarry`): **that is the pattern for any animation only the player plays** (sleep, sit, cast): off the villagers' and the dead's sheets, so the atlas pays only once.
- **The sheets**: every outfit is rasterised once into frames named `${anim}_${facing}_${i}` over the eight facings (`draw.js` `generateFigure`); `describeFigure` gives the same table with no picture for the headless world; a sheet's `animations` carries `fps`, `frames`, `activeFrame`. **The atlas is the budget**: 72 percent of 4096 square with 179 sheets (2026-10-13). Each added frame is eight frames on every sheet that carries it. Measure with `__game.gl.atlasInfo()` before and after an item that adds frames to every sheet (the death), and say so in the commit.
- **What reads frames by number must keep working**: a bite fires on the dead's attack `activeFrame` (`zombie.js`, `isActiveFrame`); a corpse bakes when the fall's animation is `done` (`updateZombie`, `dressCorpse`); the bow's draw poses are the bow style's attack frames 0 to 3 set by draw fraction (`archery.js` `setPose`: below 0.35 frame 0, below 0.8 frame 1, else 2; the release 3), the throw the same with its style; a swing's hit resolves on the click, the animation is feedback (`combat.js`).
- **Everything is data and every number is tried before it is ruled**: new keyframes go in `data/poses.json`; new animations are rows in `ANIMS` with their pose in `pose()` reading numbers from the data where a style exists; thresholds that today are literals (the draw's 0.35 and 0.8) become fields on the style. `__game.figure.BODY` is not touched (the rig is final). Commit the numbers as they appear.
- **The proofs**: `check("sheets")` and `check("frame")` change when frames are added or poses move; re-baseline once per item, on a fresh boot, and say so. The determinism hash does not move (poses are pictures). `node tools/world.js` must still boot: `describeFigure` must know every new animation. On the wire a guest sees `anim.name` and `anim.frame` in the player's record, so a new animation needs nothing new there.
- **The habits** (`docs/31/08`) hold: a pose the game plays because of a world change (sleep, sit) is played from the sim's state (`player.sleeping`, a `sitting` field), never from the client; the client only draws.

## The list, in order

### 1. The held-out pose

A named pose the rig lacks: the main arm forward at chest height, the item presented in front of the body, the off arm at rest. **Default numbers** (tried first): `armPitch [90, 0]`, `armOut [2, 1.5]`, `headD 1`, no lean. It is a one-frame animation `present` on the sheet, player-only (the `noCarry` pattern), and **three things play it**: the book when held in the main hand with nothing drawn (the book's `held.mode` is `fixed`; the pose replaces the hold pitch for that item: a `held.pose: "present"` field on an item says so), a torch held to something while a Use on a fire resolves (later; the field is enough for now), and drinking and eating for their action's duration (the `drink` and `eat` paths in `entities/player.js` set `player.anim` to `present` for `T.drinkTime` [0.6 s, default] and go back). The held item draws at the hand joint as always: `held.js` reports the joints per frame, so a new frame needs the item's angle for that pitch (`angSwing` / `ang` are read from the pose; nothing to add).

### 2. The halberd's two attacks

The halberd swings with the one-handed style and its thrust (the `alt` block on its weapon, the middle button or Q, `docs/21`) plays the same animation. **A second attack animation on the sheet**, `attack_alt`, for any weapon whose `alt` names a `style`; the thrust style `thrust` in the poses data, four keyframes, **default**: frame 0 a draw back (`pitch -20, out 1, lean -1`), frame 1 the lunge (`pitch 95, out 0, lean 3, bob 1`, the active frame), frame 2 held (`pitch 90, lean 2`), frame 3 back to rest. `animsFor` adds `attack_alt` from the alt style as it adds `attack` from the main one; `combat.tryAttack(..., "alt")` plays `attack_alt`; sheets of outfits whose main hand holds no alt weapon leave the animation off (as `noCarry` does), so the atlas pays only for the halberd's bearer. The halberd's item row gets `"alt": { ..., "style": "thrust" }`.

### 3. The cast: the staff and the book

The spell is `docs/24` (not built); the pose is this item and lands first. **The mechanics are the bow's**: the left button held on a staff or a book is a draw (`archery.launcherOf` returns a launcher for an item with a `cast` block as it does for `bow` and `thrown`; `cast: { drawTime, style, projectile: "fireball" }` on the item; the projectile exists as a stored sheet, `data/art/items/fireball.png`), the release the cast; until `24` the release does nothing but the pose and a log line ("The staff hums." default). **Two styles**: `cast_staff` with the staff raised high (frames 0 to 2 the arm rising from `pitch 60` to `pitch 160, out 2`, the head up `headD -1`; frame 3 the release, the arm thrust forward `pitch 100, lean 2`) and `cast_book` with the book held out (frames 0 to 2 the present pose of item 1 with the off hand coming up to the book, `offPitch 80, offOut 1`; frame 3 the release, both arms forward). The staff's `held.mode: "angles"` art already has the five angles the raised arm needs. Player-only animations.

### 4. The braced draw as its own style

The bow has two draws as mechanics: the plain one at `bow.drawTime` (1.4 s on the bow) and the braced one at `bow.braceDrawTime` (0.6 s) on the held right button (`archery.updateDraw`, `braced`). Both play the `bow` style. **A second style `bow_braced`**, chosen while `ctx.brace` holds (the sheet carries both as `attack` and `attack_alt`, the braced one the alt), with its own four keyframes: **default** a lower, planted stance (`lean 2, bob 1`, the bow arm lower `pitch 80`, the string hand closer `offPitch 70`). **The thresholds become data**: `POSES[style].at = [0.35, 0.8]` (the draw fractions where frames 1 and 2 begin), read by `setPose`'s caller in place of the literals, so each draw has its own rhythm. The speeds are already the bow's own numbers; this item adds none.

### 5. The spear's throw, checked

The `throw` style exists (four frames, active 3). Michael looks at it on the pane at the close zoom, from the console (`__game.give("spear")`, equip, hold the middle button) before anything is changed; if it reads wrong, the numbers move in `data/poses.json`, nothing else. Written down so it is not forgotten, not because work is known.

### 6. Arrows that land upright

A landed arrow or spear is a floor item drawn from the projectile sheet at the angle it flew (`render/props.js`, `e.angle`), lying flat. **A second landed picture, planted**: the projectile sheet gets frames `${name}_up_${k}` (the generator draws the shaft foreshortened to a short upright with the fletching at the top, leaning a little along the flight's angle `k`), and `land()` in `archery.js` decides `stuck: true` when the arrow's arc was steep enough: **default** the landing angle of the flight (the arc's descent at the end, from `peakHeight` and the distance) steeper than 50 degrees, and then with a chance `tunables.plantChance` [0.7, default], **rolled on `rng.chance`** so a host and a guest see the same arrow; a flat shot always skids. The floor item carries `stuck` beside `at` and `angle`; the renderer draws the `_up_` frame for it. Nothing in picking an arrow up changes (it is taken by its tile). An arrow that hit a body is not affected (it stays in the body, `docs/20`).

### 7. Sleep

The sleeper stands by the bed today (`02`, step 2: the rig had no lying pose at the time). It has one: `lying` is what the fall's last two frames use. **A `sleep` animation of one frame, `lying: true`** with the arms at rest and no crouch, player-only, played while `player.sleeping` is set (`updatePlayer` sets `anim` to `sleep` and skips the idle and walk while asleep), **and the sleeper is drawn on the bed**: `startSleep` moves the player to the bed's tile centre (the bed is two tiles; the head end is the anchor tile, `prop.tile`) and `wake` steps them to the nearest walkable beside it (`map.nearestWalkable`). The sim does the moving, as a verb's effect (`docs/31/08`); the facing while lying follows the bed's `facing`. The save already carries `sleeping`, so a restore lies them down again.

### 8. Sit

**A `sit` animation of one frame**, player-only: `crouch 0.55`, `lift [3, 3]`, `stride [3, 3]` (the legs forward and bent), arms at rest, **default** numbers. **A verb `sit`** in the table (`verbs.define("sit")`, `docs/31/08`): the target a prop's id whose definition has `seat: true` (the chair, the bench, the pew: the definitions gain the flag; the bench is a box still, which is fine), validated by reach (`INTERACT_RANGE`) and the seat being free; performed by setting `player.sitting = { prop }` and placing the player on the seat's tile facing the seat's `facing`. Any movement key, a swing, or a menu action stands them up (`player.sitting = null`), as the sleep's wake does. The menu's entry is "Sit" on a seat; `E` offers it as the nearest simple thing. Sitting restores stamina at the resting rate [the sleep's stamina rule at half, default] and nothing else yet.

### 9. The death, for every model

The one item that earns frames. Today: two crouches then lying. **Six frames at 8 fps**: a stagger back (`lean -2`), a knee (`crouch 0.7, lift [0, 3]`, the arms out `pitch [40, 40]`), the second knee (`crouch 0.5`), the collapse forward (`crouch 0.35, lean 3`), then the lying frame twice (the last is what the corpse bakes from, so `dressCorpse` reads the same `anim.frame` it does now: the animation's last). The dead, villagers, and the player all play it (the player's death screen comes after the fall as it does now). **Measure the atlas** before and after: two more frames on every sheet is sixteen more frames each; if `atlasInfo().fraction` passes 0.9, the item waits for the sheets-on-demand engine move (`docs/23a` B8) and says so.
