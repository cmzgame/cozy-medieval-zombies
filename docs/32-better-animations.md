## What exists, and the rules that bind every item

- **The rig** (`src/figure/rig.js`): a pose is a handful of numbers from `restPose()`: `bob` (px down), `lean` (body units forward), `armPitch` and `armOut` per arm `[left, right]`, `stride` and `lift` per leg, `headD`, `crouch` (a scale on heights above the feet), `lying`, `flash`, and `wrist` (degrees a held thing is cocked up from the forearm in a swing, 2026-10-13: the blade stays level as the arm drops). `pose(variant, anim, i, holding, style, left)` builds the frame's pose: the generic animations are a `switch` in code; **the attack's four keyframes come from the poses data** (`POSES[style]`, `data/poses.json`, whose styles are `walk`, `one_handed`, `present`, `thrust`, `zombie`, `bow`, `bow_rest`, `cast_staff`, `cast_book`, and `throw`; an attack style is `{ fps, activeFrame, frames: [{ pitch, out, lean, bob, offPitch, offOut, wrist }] }`, with `at` where a held draw's frames change and `bend`/`offBend` where an elbow bends; `present` and `bow_rest` are held poses of one frame), and so do **the walk's four** (`walk`, `{ frames: [{ stride, lift, arms, bob, lean, headD }] }`, item 10); `animsFor(spec)` gives the attack the style's length, speed, and active frame. `figure.setPoses(json)` loads the file; the layers editor's **Reload poses** re-reads it and redraws, so a number is tried by editing the file and pressing Reload, or from the console (`__game.reloadPoses()` re-reads the file and rebakes the player; or `__game.figure.setPoses(...)`, then `__game.setLook({})`).
- **The animation table** (`ANIMS` in `rig.js`): `idle` 2 frames at 2 fps, `walk` 4 at 8, `attack` 4 at 12 with the active frame 1, `hurt` 2 at 10, `die` 4 at 8, `carry_idle` and `carry_walk`. The carry pair is left off every sheet but the player's (`noCarry`): **that is the pattern for any animation only the player plays** (sleep, sit, cast): off the villagers' and the dead's sheets, so the atlas pays only once.
- **The sheets**: every outfit is rasterised once into frames named `${anim}_${facing}_${i}` over the eight facings (`draw.js` `generateFigure`); `describeFigure` gives the same table with no picture for the headless world; a sheet's `animations` carries `fps`, `frames`, `activeFrame`. **The atlas is the budget**: 72 percent of 4096 square with 179 sheets (2026-10-13). Each added frame is eight frames on every sheet that carries it. Measure with `__game.gl.atlasInfo()` before and after an item that adds frames to every sheet (the death), and say so in the commit.
- **What reads frames by number must keep working**: a bite fires on the dead's attack `activeFrame` (`zombie.js`, `isActiveFrame`); a corpse bakes when the fall's animation is `done` (`updateZombie`, `dressCorpse`); the bow's draw poses are the bow style's attack frames 0 to 3 set by draw fraction (`archery.js` `setPose`: below 0.35 frame 0, below 0.8 frame 1, else 2; the release 3), the throw the same with its style; a swing's hit resolves on the click, the animation is feedback (`combat.js`).
- **The elbow** (2026-10-15, `02`): every arm pose may bend the forearm at the elbow, `BODY.elbow` of the way down the arm (the middle): `bend` and `offBend` on a style's frame (the main and the off arm, degrees the forearm turns from the upper arm's line, forward and up the way the arm pitches; 0 straight, about 150 folds the hand back to the face), `bends: [left, right]` on a walk frame, and `bend`/`offBend` on a held pose (`present`, `bow_rest`). A held thing follows the forearm. Nothing bends until a pose asks: the item that first does says so. Tried from the console: the bow's string hand to the chin at full draw (`offPitch 80, offBend 150`).
- **Everything is data and every number is tried before it is ruled**: new keyframes go in `data/poses.json`; new animations are rows in `ANIMS` with their pose in `pose()` reading numbers from the data where a style exists; thresholds that today are literals (the draw's 0.35 and 0.8) become fields on the style. `__game.figure.BODY` is not touched (the rig is final). Commit the numbers as they appear.
- **The proofs**: `check("sheets")` and `check("frame")` change when frames are added or poses move; re-baseline once per item, on a fresh boot, and say so. The determinism hash does not move (poses are pictures). `node tools/world.js` must still boot: `describeFigure` must know every new animation. On the wire a guest draws by `anim.name` and `anim.frame` alone: they ride in a player's light record and in the slim record of the dead's and the villagers' delta (`net.js`), so a new animation needs nothing new there, as long as the guest bakes the same sheets (it does: the sheets come from the outfit and the poses data, which both sides load). The sync check's `dead` part hashes the animation's name and frame; its `players` part does not.
- **The rules for multiplayer** (`docs/31/08`) hold: a pose the game plays because of a world change (sleep, sit) is played from the sim's state (`player.sleeping`, a `sitting` field), never from the client; the client only draws. Set the animation in `updatePlayer` from that state: the host runs it for every player, and a guest re-runs it over its own unacknowledged inputs (the prediction), so its own figure agrees with the host's. A new field on the player such as `sitting` rides the light record by itself (every field not listed as heavy does) and the save; add it to the sync check's `players` part (`game.syncParts`). A verb such as `sit` reaches a guest as every verb does, the menu entry or `E` as a command by the prop's id, performed on the host. What lands on the ground (item 6's `stuck`) is a floor item's field, so it travels in the touched chunk and is hashed in the `map` part.

### 4. The braced draw as its own style

### 6. Arrows that land upright

A landed arrow or spear is a floor item drawn from the projectile sheet at the angle it flew (`render/props.js`, `e.angle`), lying flat. **A second landed picture, planted**: the projectile sheet gets frames `${name}_up_${k}` (the generator draws the shaft foreshortened to a short upright with the fletching at the top, leaning a little along the flight's angle `k`), and `land()` in `archery.js` decides `stuck: true` when the arrow's arc was steep enough: **default** the landing angle of the flight (the arc's descent at the end, from `peakHeight` and the distance) steeper than 50 degrees, and then with a chance `tunables.plantChance` [0.7, default], **rolled on `rng.chance`** so the world stays deterministic (only the host rolls; a guest sees the landed arrow in the touched chunk); a flat shot always skids. The floor item carries `stuck` beside `at` and `angle`; the renderer draws the `_up_` frame for it. Nothing in picking an arrow up changes (it is taken by its tile). An arrow that hit a body is not affected (it stays in the body, `docs/20`).

### 7. Sleep

The sleeper stands by the bed today (`02`, step 2: the rig had no lying pose at the time). Two can sleep in one world now (time skips only when every living player is in bed, `30` section 3.3), so two sleepers may want one bed: the verb takes a bed already slept in today, and whether it should refuse a second sleeper is for this item to decide with Michael. It has one: `lying` is what the fall's last two frames use. **A `sleep` animation of one frame, `lying: true`** with the arms at rest and no crouch, player-only, played while `player.sleeping` is set (`updatePlayer` sets `anim` to `sleep` and skips the idle and walk while asleep), **and the sleeper is drawn on the bed**: `startSleep` moves the player to the bed's tile centre (the bed is two tiles; the head end is the anchor tile, `prop.tile`) and `wake` steps them to the nearest walkable beside it (`map.nearestWalkable`). The sim does the moving, as a verb's effect (`docs/31/08`); the facing while lying follows the bed's `facing`. The save already carries `sleeping`, so a restore lies them down again.

### 8. Sit

**A `sit` animation of one frame**, player-only: `crouch 0.55`, `lift [3, 3]`, `stride [3, 3]` (the legs forward and bent), arms at rest, **default** numbers. **A verb `sit`** in the table (`verbs.define("sit")`, `docs/31/08`): the target a prop's id whose definition has `seat: true` (the chair, the bench, the pew: the definitions gain the flag; the bench is a box still, which is fine), validated by reach (`INTERACT_RANGE`) and the seat being free; performed by setting `player.sitting = { prop }` and placing the player on the seat's tile facing the seat's `facing`. Any movement key, a swing, or a menu action stands them up (`player.sitting = null`), as the sleep's wake does. The menu's entry is "Sit" on a seat; `E` offers it as the nearest simple thing. Sitting restores stamina at the resting rate [the sleep's stamina rule at half, default] and nothing else yet.
