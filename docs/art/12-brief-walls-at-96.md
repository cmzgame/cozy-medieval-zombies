# Art brief: the walls redrawn at 96 px, with two-crate doors and big windows

*A brief, written 2026-10-26 for `docs/52` and not yet run. Michael either reruns the kept generators himself (`tools/art-gen/village/`, `tools/art-gen/woods_rugs_paintings/`) or pastes this whole file into Claude Design. Hello again. This is a request to redraw the whole wall set you drew before, in exactly your style, at a new height: walls 96 px tall instead of 80, doors 64 px tall instead of 48, windows from 24 to 64 px instead of 22 to 51. Nothing else about the look changes. Michael's reason: a one-tile crate is 32 px tall, and he wants Zomboid's predictability, three crates from floor to ceiling, a door two crates tall, and a window big on purpose, "for fun, not for realism", so the player sees more through it and shoots through it more easily.

Please read the whole brief before drawing. At the end are three questions; answer them first if you can, or draw and say what you chose.

**What Michael attaches** (in case you no longer have them): your style bible, `docs/art/01-style-bible.md`; your village generators, `tools/art-gen/village/` (`village.py`, `iso3d.py`, `anchors_walls.json`, `README_village.md`); the woods, `tools/art-gen/woods_rugs_paintings/` (`woods.py`, `wood_tones.json`); and the delivered set, `data/art/village/`.

## The rules, unchanged (`01-style-bible.md`)

- Hard pixels only: every pixel alpha 0 or 255.
- Light from the top left, three or four flat shades per material, a darkest-of-its-own-colour edge on the lower or outer side, no gradients, no dither, no baked light.
- Walls have no outline; doors and shutters (things the player uses) keep their 1 px `#2b2522` outline.
- Drawn at final size; nothing is scaled by the engine.
- Tiles are 64 by 32 diamonds; the character is 48 px to the crown and about 55 with hair, in a 48 by 72 frame with the feet at (24, 66).

## The numbers that change, and only these

Your generator already holds them as parameters (`village.py`):
- `H_WALL = 80` becomes **`H_WALL = 96`**.
- `DOOR = (6, 26, 48)` (along the wall from, to, height) becomes **`DOOR = (6, 26, 64)`**: the same width, 64 tall.
- `WIN = (8, 24, 22, 51)` (along the wall from, to, sill, top) becomes **`WIN = (8, 24, 24, 64)`**: the same width, the sill at 24, the top at 64 (the door's line).
- The torch bracket's height on a wall is the engine's (`BRACKET_UP`, 32 to 48); you draw no bracket.
- Thickness stays: `THICK = {stone: 16, wood: 14, log: 18}`.
- **Fences are not in this request:** `wall_fence`, its gate, and its boards stay 36 px as you drew them (`11-brief`).

Everything that depends on those follows by itself: the plain and opening variants, the corner posts (96 tall), the open-door leaves (64 tall) and far-side leaves, the shutters (40 tall, hinged at the sill and top as now, folding back about 160 degrees), and the board overlays (one to three planks over a door and over a window, sized to the new openings). The cap stays inside the wall sprite, redrawn at the new top, as before.

## How it drops in (the engine's side, for your information)

The game reads each wall's `height` from its sprite definition (`data/sprites/wall_<material>.json`, `wall.height`), the images and anchors from your JSON, and the openings' heights from four constants; all of those change in the same pull request as your delivery (`docs/52`). The delivery checklist is `docs/27-art-integration.md` section A: the zip's layout, the anchors file, the previews, and a note saying which version and why. The engine then proves it: `check("sheets")` against your files, `check("build")` with the crate stack, and `check("woods")` against the leaf woods.
