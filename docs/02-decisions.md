### 2026-10-12 — The third-hit rule off for testing, and the finisher cut to 1.5 (Michael)

To test the melee without them: **the third-hit knockdown is switched off** (`combo.enabled: false` in `data/combat.json`; the code and its hit counting are kept, `hits` and `window` still there) and **the downed-enemy bonus is 1.5, not 2** (`finisher.mult`). **Effect, measured:** the sword's two-swing kill (34 + 68) is gone, as the mace's: with a 1.5 finisher a first-hit knockdown gives 34 + 51 for the sword and 38 + 57 for the mace, both short of 100, so a fresh 100-hp dead one now takes 3 swings with either, every time (79 of 80 trials; one outlier from the test's own repositioning). The mace keeps its 60 percent knockdown chance, so a mace still floors them more often, it just does not shorten the kill. The knockdown remains the "crit without being one" for what it does next (the dead one on the floor for 2 s, hit at 1.5x), but it no longer shortens the kill. Both are one line each to put back.

### 2026-10-12 — Dual wielding on the left button, with an exposure price (Michael, question 23)

The right button is the menu, so an off-hand swing there never worked; **ruled: dual wielding is on the left button.** Any one-handed weapon (sword, hatchet, mace, stick, torch) goes in either hand; clicking faster than the main weapon's recovery swings the off hand, so spamming goes back and forth at up to twice the rate with two strong weapons. **The drawback, Michael's: glass cannon.** The first idea was an anti-shield (more damage taken in front, more at the sides, fatal from behind). **Built as state, not loadout (Claude's refinement):** an off-hand swing exposes you for 2.5 s, and exposed you take 1.5x from the front, 2.5x at the sides, a hundred times (fatal) from behind; carrying a torch or two weapons costs nothing, because a loadout penalty would tax the ordinary night kit (a sword and a torch) and the torch only swings at a click rate the sword cannot keep. Open: whether the fatal back should be a big multiple that kills in two bites instead (`behind` in `data/combat.json`). Answers question 23 (option b, with a price).

### 2026-10-12 — The knockdown is the game's crit (Michael's finding, `14`)

Michael: a sword knockdown on the first swing makes the follow-up a two-shot, "it feels like a crit without actually being one." **Measured (120 kills of a fresh 100-hp dead one per weapon, real swings, 2026-10-12):** sword 2 swings 32 percent of the time and 3 otherwise (mean 2.68, 1.34 s); mace 2 swings 67 percent (mean 2.33, 1.52 s); hatchet 3 or 4 (mean 3.5, 1.93 s); spear stab 3 or 4 (3.55, 2.13 s); stick 5 or 6 (5.5, 2.75 s). **The rule behind it:** every hit has a knockdown chance (0.3; the mace 0.6); a hit on a downed one is a finisher at twice the damage; so a first-hit knockdown kills in two when `damage + 2 x damage >= 100`, which is `damage >= 34`: the sword (34) and the mace (38) have it, the hatchet (30), spear (30) and stick do not. **It is emergent, not designed:** the sword clears the line by two hit points (34 + 68 = 102 against a 100-hp dead one; at 33 it vanishes), and the dead's hit points moving, a tired swing (half damage), or a guarded one (35 percent) all break it. **Ruled: keep it, and call the knockdown the crit**: the chance is visible (the dead one hits the floor) rather than a hidden roll on damage, which is why it feels fair. What to decide only if the sword proves too strong overall: the sword's knockdown chance (0.3), its damage by one point, or its stamina; and if the dead gain varied hit points, whether the two-shot line should be a stated rule (a `heavy` weapon tier) instead of an accident of 34.
