# 02 — Decisions and open questions

Rulings with reasons, newest first, within a day as well as between days. If a ruling is reversed, strike it and add the new one. The tiebreaker is `00-vision.md`. **The parents' logs do not bind here.** A parent's ruling becomes a rule of this game only when it is written in this file.

## Decisions

### 2026-10-10 — Openings (Michael)

The milestone after the renderer: the dead get in. Design in `09-openings.md`.

- **Doors and windows can be barred from either side.** Michael's call. A bar holds the leaf shut for both sides and only the side that put it there can take it off, so you can bar your own door from inside, bar the storehouse from outside, or shut a zombie in the shed.
- **An opening is a stack, read from the attacker's side.** Michael asked how "bars, door, bars inside" could work when the dead attack. Whatever is on the attacker's side breaks first, then the leaf, then whatever is on the far side. A zombie outside a door barred inside breaks the door before it ever touches the bar; one trapped in a barred shed breaks the shutters and climbs out. One rule, no special cases.
- **The dead climb through open windows**, slower than you and unable to bite while they do. Michael was about to ask for this; it is this milestone.
- **The dead attack the room's nearest opening.** When the way to what they want is shut, they go to the nearest opening of the room it is in on their own side, and hit the stack until it gives. Nothing ever breaks a wall. Noise stays out (question 18).
- **What the dead break returns nothing.** The sibling's rule. A broken door is a doorway until repaired, a broken shutter an open window that cannot be closed. Repair comes with the inventory.
- **Sealed is derived** from a room's openings each step: every door closed and whole, every window shuttered or boarded and whole. It gates sleep later and costs nothing now.

### 2026-10-09, night — The renderer goes to WebGL (Michael)

Built the same night, all three phases (`08-renderer.md`). Michael at Phase 2: "MILES better, and I could not produce a single weird polygon bug anymore. Wall shading suddenly feels way more intuitive." At the end of Phase 3: "THIS is the right system. It looks amazing." Accepted; the Canvas renderer is deleted. The light debug view is "like looking at the map with infrared goggles on."

Michael took the lighting problem to an outside agent with only the lighting and scaling parts of the game described, and brought back a spec (`medieval_pixel_world_game_spec.md`, in his downloads) recommending PixiJS on WebGL. Read together on 2026-10-09; most of its architecture is what we already have (authoritative world data, derived light maps per chunk, materials apart from art, emitters as data, rooms found not authored). Rulings:

- **Raw WebGL2, not PixiJS, not Canvas 2D for the world.** Michael: libraries like PixiJS exist to make this easier for humans to write; with me writing it, raw WebGL costs little and we own the pipeline. The reason it is worth doing now: everything he was still dissatisfied with is light per pixel (a pool climbing a wall, his own pool on the wall beside him, a tree lit at the trunk and dark at the crown), which Canvas 2D cannot do and a shader does by sampling a light texture at each pixel. The Canvas 2D ordered pass stays until the WebGL renderer reaches parity, then goes. Canvas 2D keeps generating the placeholder art, which uploads as textures. The design is `08-renderer.md`.
- **Portals are emitters.** An opening is a light source on its darker side with the brighter side's ambient as its intensity, times its transmission. At night a lit room's open window and door cast warm patches outside whether or not the hearth is within its radius of them; by day the same openings pool daylight on the floor inside, which replaces the flat per-room openness number with light by each window. One rule for both directions. Michael: matches what he wants from windows, daylight pooling included.
- **Openings carry a transmission scalar**, not a yes or no: a plain wall and a closed door 0, an open shutter and an open doorway 1, a closed shutter a small leak to tune, and later a glass pane most and a curtain little. Light multiplies through; sight is transmission above a threshold. Same one set of blockers, now with a number.
- **The floor field exists.** Ruled on 2026-10-08 and never added: chunks and lookups take a layer, always 0 for now.
- **A pause on gameplay for this is accepted.** Michael: get this right while we are very early, since openings and combat will be written against the drawable interface this produces. He also ruled that my wall-clock estimates are worthless and I should give scope instead.
- **From the spec, taken:** flicker as emitter data, a light-map sampling toggle (nearest is his per-cell backburner, now one line), a debug view of the light map and blockers. **Ignored, as Michael agreed:** TypeScript, 32-tile chunks, top-down Y sorting, Tiled, building as first-class now (question 14 stays open at zero cost), and its vertical slice, which ours exceeds except for construction and save.

### 2026-10-09, afternoon — Torches, fire, hands, and the clock (Michael)

The conversation that opens Milestone 2. Its design is `07-lighting.md`.

- **Every holdable item has a hands designation: `main`, `off`, or `both`.** A torch and a shield are off-hand; a sword, a hatchet, a knife, and tools are main; a bow and a great axe need both. A two-handed item empties both slots and an off-hand item is refused while both are full, so a torch at night costs you the shield and the bow. Michael: assign it to everything now, it preps the character gear screen he may ask for. The spear is tabled until combat. No item system exists yet, so lighting brings a minimal item registry and two hand slots on the player ahead of the inventory port; the torch is item number one.
- **Torches never burn out.** Lit while held or mounted, unlit in a bag. No fuel, no tinder, no lighting from a flame. Michael: torches burning down is the unfun kind of busywork, and the pillar agrees: things run out because you used them, and a torch on a bracket is not being used up, so a timer on it would be the shutoff the pillar forbids. The wood economy lives in the hearth and the campfire. The Minecraft trade is the cost instead: **a torch cannot be placed on the ground.** It mounts in a **bracket**, which is state on a wall edge like a shutter, or on a **post**, a prop built from wood outdoors. Lighting the whole grounds costs a post per pool, once. A torch dropped on the ground is an unlit item lying there.
- **At night the dead see fire from far away.** One rule for every flame: a zombie within a long range (twelve tiles to start) with a clear line to a light source walks to it, treating the flame as a sighting, so the memory rule does the rest; a person is noticed at the normal short range, scaled by the light on them as in the cozy parent. A carried torch, a campfire in the yard, a hearth seen through an open shutter, and a jack-o'-lantern all pull the same way; closed shutters are how you hide a fire. Michael's intent: a lit guy in a forest is far more visible than a guy in the pitch dark, and fire should pull from off screen too. No memory-length rule yet; longer sight already makes them lose you less. Off screen, the same number becomes the per-chunk lure in `03-world-model.md` once records exist.
- **Fires are data.** Each fire kind declares its light, whether it burns wood, whether it cooks, and where it may be placed. A **campfire** burns wood, goes out, cooks (a hanging pot, no model needed), lights a medium pool, and is built on any outdoor walkable tile with no prop. A **hearth** burns wood, goes out, cooks, lights its whole room and spills a pool through its openings, sits indoors against a wall, is rare in authored houses, and is buildable later. A mounted or held **torch** lights a small pool, burns nothing, cooks nothing. The **jack-o'-lantern** is a tiny source with no fuel. A **furnace** comes later with the smithy, burns, and forges rather than cooks. Michael: people who truly want the grounds lit can maintain a ring of campfires.
- **A lit hearth lights its whole room.** The cozy parent's room-light rule, with the hearth as the switch. A great hall may want a cap by room size; decide when one exists.
- **The clock comes first.** Lighting has nothing to do without a night, so Milestone 2 starts with the parent's clock: a twelve-minute day, brightness from full to about 0.22 at night, never black, a debug key to skip an hour. The night tint is our dusky purple, not the parent's blue, and whether full day is ever quite full is decided by eye, since Halloween never ends here. Sleep, routines, and the trickle hang off the same clock later.
- **The fog becomes light.** At night an unlit tile in your line of sight is dark, and the dead standing in it are drawn dim, never hidden; the parent found that not drawing them read as spawning. The player's night-vision pool is small and weak: going dark is possible but blind.
- **Milestone 2 accepted, "good for now."** Michael, end of 2026-10-09, after four rounds of play and fixes on the night layer: not perfect, "this is why people use game engines", and good enough to build on. Noted for later: switching the near walls back to low stubs (still on the `C` key) removes the translucent-wall mixing that caused most of the day's lighting oddities, since a stub has no face for the room to show through. Nothing more is tuned on the night layer until something else needs it.
- **What takes which light.** Michael, from play on 2026-10-09, after a shed's hidden back wall kept the dark of the room behind it and a canopy over a lit room lit up: exterior walls light like the ground and trees, seen or not; the top of a chunky wall is never shaded by the room; and everything standing on a tile takes the light of its own tile. Michael asked whether per-cell light would fix it; it would not, this is about which thing takes which light, and per-cell stays on the backburner for the look of the pools only. Written up in `07-lighting.md`, "How things are lit".
- **Logs are free until you can cut a tree.** Michael: pretend you always have logs on you; `E` feeds a burner as if from the inventory, and consumption rates are not worth testing until trees fall and logs go in a bag. No woodpile prop. The burn rate still runs so a fire still goes out.
- **The light layer may be smoothed.** Michael liked what the parent was going for: the two-cell-per-tile erase map drawn through the iso transform with image smoothing on. An exception to "never scale in `drawImage`" for that one layer, which is a gradient and not pixel art. Per-cell chunky light stays on the backburner in case the first pass does not please.
- **Campfires go on any outdoor walkable tile with no prop.**
- **Open question 8 is closed**: a carried radius, not a cone or a shuttered lantern. The lantern moves to `05-someday.md` as a toggle upgrade. **Open question 17 is closed**: the hearth burns wood and goes out.

### 2026-10-09 — Milestone 1 passed, and the vibe pass (Michael)

- **Lighting is next, and it finishes the vibe pass.** Michael, end of 2026-10-09: the last thing needed to complete the vibe is a real lighting system like the cozy parent's, where candles changed the light of a room rather than glowing; from there every gameplay decision is more fun to test. The parent's art looked good but felt a little sterile; this is a totally different place. Milestone 2 is lighting. Torches, carried and placed, are discussed first (open question 8).
- **Fog lighter again** (0.36) and **visibility samples a tile's corners** when its centre is hidden, which removes most of the pizza-slice slivers behind wall ends. **The player has sleeves** and a worn, yellowed shirt (`#c9b98e`, sleeves `#bfae84`) instead of new linen. This supersedes the off-white shirt below.
- **Sets and features.** Michael's terminology (2026-10-09), adopted: a **set** is any authored thing with a footprint and a padding ring that random spawns never enter, from a wagon to a castle; a **feature** is a road, river, or bridge, linear, reserving only its own tiles. A road joining two sets does not merge them, because a set is defined by its own outline. A set's footprint may be any shape: a rectangle, a union of rectangles, or a text mask. The map file now has `sets` and `features`; scatter and the wild fill the rest. Density lowered by about 28 percent at both ends the same day: everything had read as dense forest.
- **Forest density drifts, buildings get a padding ring, and the wild is forest.** Michael, 2026-10-09, after the trunk fix landed where he wanted it (most things passable, some slow you, few impassable): three tree sizes by four colours in an even mix, density that wanders from thicket to one tree in six or eight tiles, and no trees on or beside buildings. Built as: a smooth noise field that scales each scatter rule's density; a `padding` ring (default one tile) around every set and `reserve` rectangles for yards and fields, which take no scatter; and a `wild` rule for every chunk outside the authored bounds, which grows harvest forest and seeds zero to two of the dead per chunk. The world is grown three chunks ahead of the player so nothing appears in view. Biomes are a named set of kinds and a noise scale; the village and the wild both use `harvest`, so gloom is always a place you choose. The dead beyond forty tiles stand still until the records milestone.
- **Cutting trees down is not precluded.** A tree is a prop on a tile; felling one is clearing that tile's prop reference and dropping wood. Nothing in the renderer or the map caches a tree anywhere else.
- **Trees block only their trunk.** Whole-tile collision made the wood impenetrable and would get you killed (Michael, 2026-10-09). A sprite definition may declare a collision radius: small, medium, and large trees are 0.14, 0.18, and 0.22 tiles, a giant 0.3, a barrel 0.26, and against round things a body's radius counts as 0.2 instead of 0.3, so two trunks a tile apart leave real room. A step into a trunk deflects along its tangent instead of stopping. Tiles with a solid prop stay unwalkable for pathing, so the dead go around; Michael wants forests to be a place you can lose them, since Zomboid's are too dangerous to be fun. Revisit if it ever feels overpowered.
- **Harvest first, gloom later, by zone.** The blue and purple trees beside the orange looked like Halloween candy. Biomes are the answer, so for now the whole map is the harvest register (dusky orange, yellow, red, dark green, plus a few old giants) and the gloomy register's trees stay on file for a zone added later, along with the plague doctor and the rest. Get "harvest zombies farming simulator" fun first, then expand. Zones are in `05-someday.md`.
- **Authored structure, scattered filler.** Michael (2026-10-09): author the roads, the buildings, and any water; randomly place the rest, trees as forests, overturned carts to loot, events. That is Zomboid's and Stardew's split too. Built as a `scatter` rule in the map file: a rectangle, a density, a weighted mix of kinds, a seed. Scatter lands only on the default floor, never on a wall's tile, never inside a set's padding, never on a placed prop, and the same seed grows the same wood. Carts and events are later kinds of the same rule.
- **Gloomwood, two registers.** Michael's vibe statement is in `06-art.md`: an aggressively autumn cosy register and a Tirisfal gloomy register, balanced so farming never feels bad. Trees are big and there is more forest than plains. ~~Trees are in green, dusky blue, purple, and the odd amber.~~ Superseded the same day by "harvest first, gloom later": blue and purple are on file for a zone of their own.
- **Occlusion is a hole, not a fade.** A character behind a tree shows through a soft hole cut in every canopy over them, so overlapping big trees never stack into mud. The silhouette pass is gone.
- ~~**The player wears a dirty off-white shirt and brown breeches.** No denim.~~ Superseded the same day by the yellowed shirt with sleeves, above. Brown breeches and no denim still hold. The dead keep their dusty purple.
- **A jack-o'-lantern**, because it is October: a carved pumpkin with a stem and a flickering fire inside, three on the square. The first animated prop, and a light source once lighting lands.
- **The pumpkin's glow joins the lighting pass.** When dynamic lighting lands, the jack-o'-lantern lights the ground around it the way candles did in the cozy parent; the flicker inside stays as drawn. Noted in the lighting milestone.
- **Open shutters and doors swing out on the outside of the wall**, perpendicular to it at the jambs, as if pushed open from inside. History be damned. The renderer knows which side is outdoors from the rooms.
- **Fittings are one wood everywhere.** Doors and shutters are the same wood whatever the wall, as vertical planks with dark metal strips across them, two on a door and one on a shutter leaf. Michael: "I know this is not a thing but I like how it sounds."
- **Doors, second.** Doors are real now that windows work: tap E opens or closes, closed blocks sight and bodies, the dead cannot open them yet (the openings milestone decides how they get through). Doors start closed.
- **Dark medieval fantasy, not strictly medieval.** Michael: skeletons with sword and shield, plague doctors, scarecrow men would fit; medieval must not be a ceiling that shuts down ideas, and the dark-fantasy vibe suits the RPG leanings. Tone rule updated in `00-vision.md`. Nothing fantastical is scheduled.
- **It is a Place.** Michael, after testing zombie memory: better than either parent, it feels like playing Zomboid, the contrast with the art is noticeable and fun, and the cottage and shed read as grounds he hangs out at rather than a test space. The probe's purpose is served.
- **Corner posts.** Where walls meet at a corner, end, or junction, a post of the wall's thickness fills the notch the thickness leaves. Stone wins over wood where they meet. A straight run gets no posts.
- **Every window has wooden shutters, worked the Zomboid way.** Tap E to open or close, hold E to climb through once open. Checked against Zomboid's own description: a quick press does one thing, a hold does the other. Closed shutters block sight. Climbing takes 0.9 s and cannot be interrupted: a window is a desperate exit, not a shortcut. Shutters start closed. This replaces the probe's glass: there is no pane in this village (`06-art.md`), so the sibling's curtains and broken glass do not carry; boards may.
- **The dead know only where they last saw you.** Michael drew the case: he ran a route around a house with solid walls and the dead cut the corner straight to him, as if they had his coordinates. They did: chase was pathing to the player's live tile. Now a zombie remembers the last place it saw you, walks there, searches around it for ten seconds, and gives up. No biting through walls either: a bite needs line of sight. **Noise is deliberately not a factor yet**; Michael is unsure it fits the cosy idea and with no guns there is nothing loud. Open question 18.
- **The stamina bar is the first UI**, because Michael sprints everywhere. Sprint lasts about six seconds instead of two and a half.
- **The fog is lighter.** It shades what you cannot see clearly but never distracts from what is there. 0.74 alpha was near black; now 0.46 in a dusky purple. (Lowered again to 0.36 later the same day, above.)
- **Dev art gets the game's visual identity now.** Spooky medieval, a bit haunted, never permanently night; Tirisfal Glades, not Stardew. Thick walls, planks and bricks, open windows, dirt roads, a farm tile, dull grass. The character placeholders were kept at first; the player's clothes changed later the same day (above) and the dead are unchanged. See `06-art.md`, which also records the genre test for any new colour.
- **One screen is a few huts, a small square, part of a castle, or barely all of a small one. That is plenty.** Michael: all of those are scenarios where one screen would be generous. 960×540 stands, no zoom. The countryside stays in `05-someday.md`.
- **Players can go through windows.** Michael, on seeing the wedge: "now we can let players go through windows!" Open question 5 is settled: the living and the dead both, with the details (slow, loud, broken or open) decided at the openings milestone.
- **Near walls draw full height and translucent when you are inside.** Michael likes seeing the full wall. Stubs stay on the `C` key for now and may come back later. This supersedes the stub plan that `03-world-model.md` carried until the docs pass later on 2026-10-09.
- **All four proofs hold.** Michael's words: the continuous map does not feel more "real" than the dioramas, which were legible, but it feels cohesive and spatially real the way Zomboid does; the window wedge is "spooky how much like Zomboid it feels"; the viewport is plenty. The engine question is answered. The next question is whether the game is fun.

### 2026-10-08 — The project exists (Michael)

- **A new project, not a rewrite of either parent.** Both parents stay playable as they are. This game borrows by copying. Reason: the two things Michael missed, knights and moving fluidly between buildings, belong to one game that is neither parent, and a fork leaves nothing broken behind.
- **New game, new gospel.** Rulings from either parent are things to consider, not things to obey. Each is re-decided here when it comes up. Michael's words.
- **Medieval, low fantasy, no guns.** Michael's reasons, in order: a modern setting invites "worse Zomboid" in every sentence and medieval ends the comparison at once; he wants knights and swords; guns felt too powerful. On the last we agreed the gun's real fault was an illegible cost (alarm elsewhere, minutes later) that was fixable in place, so it is not the reason.
- **One continuous map with line of sight replaces the dollhouse.** Fluid movement from building to building is the thing neither parent gives and the reason this one exists. Accepted costs: the whole-room-at-once identity, the 12×12 node, the two-far-walls convention. The cozy parent's lighting is what made real line of sight look worth having.
- **Cute wins over grim.** The dead are drawn like everyone else. Deaths are still stated plainly.
- **Light feeds sight.** Darkness hides and lamps reveal, as in the cozy parent. The whole point of carrying its lighting is that it is mechanical.
- **Milestone 1 is a probe, and nothing else is built until it passes.** Four proofs in `00-vision.md`. If one fails, stop and talk.
- **Chunks replace nodes.** Michael's proposal, ruled 2026-10-08 with these specifics:
  - **The map is stored block by block, from the start.** Michael: one file cuts off too many opportunities, as long as it does not impose on the browser or multiply the code. It does neither: a map that is a dictionary of 16×16 chunks with one `tileAt` lookup is the same amount of code as a flat array, and loading on demand is one fetch per chunk plus "a missing chunk is empty field". A ring of chunks is fetched ahead of the active region so nothing hitches. This makes authored-versus-procedural a live question (below): a hand-authored village with generated fields between villages is now possible.
  - **Active radius 2 chunks.** The dead become real two screens out and take about 40 s to arrive. Michael's preference. Cost is trivial.
  - **Break-ins happen only slightly off screen.** Inside the active region the dead are real and break in as usual, seen or not. Beyond it they are notes and do nothing to buildings. Michael: the dead breaking into houses that do not contain the player is not a priority. No "note chews on a lured house" rule.
  - **Rooms by flood fill, never authored.** Same reason as block storage: it keeps building and the dev tool possible.
- **Fire and resources are the economy; nothing shuts off on a timer.** Michael, 2026-10-08, on what separates this from Zomboid while the chunked world moves toward it: the setting, and the absence of Zomboid's infrastructure arc (water shutoff, power cut, broadcasts going dead). "Fire is everything, and every natural resource is so important." Things run out because you used them: wood for the hearth, water from the well, seed in the ground. Added to `00-vision.md` as a pillar. My lean on the first consequence: the hearth burns firewood and goes out without it, which is the cozy parent's parked "candle burn time" made central rather than fiddly.
- **Farming is in, and it is load-bearing.** Michael: simpler than Zomboid's, closer to Stardew; big for him and for cozy games in general; "if it doesn't work the game probably won't." Cooking comes with it, in some simple fashion. Both are now in the vision and the milestone sketch. The cozy parent's take (planters with three growth stages that need water; cooking as one step with no recipes) is an input.
- **NPCs are in, killable, and not crippled for the browser's sake.** Michael, 2026-10-08. They carry no weapons and never fight for the player; that is the compromise and it holds. They **flee** from the dead they see until they cannot, then die and turn, so a single wanderer in your base does not kill a man standing still. The player can tell one to **follow** or to **stay** on a tile, which is how you bring a villager home and keep him. Beyond the active region they are frozen notes like the dead. See `04-npcs.md`.
- **Quests: never say never, but not for a good long while.** The game has to be playable, fun, and content-rich first. RPG-like elements were part of the original medieval wish and are not forgotten. Logged in `05-someday.md`.
- **Probe viewport 960×540, no roofs.** Michael accepted both. Nothing may be written in a way that forecloses roofs; they sit in `05-someday.md`.
- **Single storey first.** Michael agreed on 2026-10-08. Tile data carries a layer index from day one so upper floors can be added without a format change; how they render is decided after the probe.
- **The browser can do this.** Checked before starting: Canvas 2D drew the cozy parent's shadowed light layer at 60 fps with headroom; a scrolling camera draws a few hundred diamonds a frame; visibility is the same ray march as the light map with the player as the source; A\* for fifty dead on a few thousand tiles is trivial. The hard parts are art rules, not browser limits.
