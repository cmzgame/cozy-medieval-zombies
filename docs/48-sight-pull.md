# 48 — Sight-pull: one of the dead drawn in brings the ones that see it

**Status, 2026-10-25: a spec, ruled by Michael in session (`02`, 2026-10-25, Sight-pull ruled); not built.** Written from `origin/main` at 6c9e740 or later. One build step, one issue. A **default** is Primary's, Michael's to overrule; the rest is his ruling.

## 1. Why, in Michael's words

Project Zomboid draws the dead by sound; this game does not, and stays so: "I think sound never being an aggro thing is probably the right call but who knows in the future" (the Noise someday stays in `docs/05`). What sound does there, sight does here: "I think we need sight-pull to replace Zomboid's sound-pull. And I think this is especially crucial for long range weapons that go further than any other way a zombie could find you, so it's a safe attack on one zombie at a time. If you have social aggro, it stops being safe and adds some of the pressure back that not caring about sound is getting us."

Of the two ways to build it Primary laid out (a pulled one broadcasting once, or the idle dead watching for a chase and following it), Michael chose the first: "Let's do the one time broadcast to nearby then no further chain reaction as you suggested."

## 2. Before

The dead notice (`src/entities/zombie.js`): a player they see (`canSee`: `zombie.sight` 8 tiles, scaled by the light on the player, a clear line), a villager they see when no player is in sight (`nearestPrey`, with the setting on), a flame at night (`nearestFlame`, `flameRange` 12 times the night), and a blow (`damageZombie`'s `from`: it learns where the blow came from); one may also be made hunting (`createZombie`'s `aggro`). Each sets `aggro`, which only `giveUp` clears. A pulled one brings no one; pulling from the edge of a group always works.

## 3. The rule

**When one of the dead first turns to hunt a player, it calls once to the dead near it that can see it, and those join the hunt. The ones it calls never call others.**
- **Hunting a player is its own mark,** not `aggro` (which a flame, a villager, and a spawn set too, so the dead round a torch-bearer or a campfire would turn by the flame first and never pull): **`huntsPlayer`**, set on a dead one when it turns to a player first-hand and on every one a pull draws in, cleared in `giveUp` (every end of a hunt goes through it). It lives on the host alone, as `aggro`, `lastSeen`, and `siege` do, and is saved with the dead one's record as they are.
- **The pull:** a dead one whose `huntsPlayer` is false turns to a player **first-hand**: it sees one (the sight check in `updateZombie`, `nearestSeenPlayer`), or a player's blow lands on it and it lives (`damageZombie` with `from`; a killing blow calls no one, as `damageZombie` returns before it learns where the blow came from). That turn sets `huntsPlayer` and is **the pull**. A dead one already walking to a flame or after a villager that then sees a player pulls; one spawned hunting is not hunting a player until it sees one.
- **Where the call is made:** in `updateZombie`, which has the list of the other dead (`others`): the sight check calls at once; a blow sets `pullDue` on the struck one (`damageZombie` has no list of the dead; its callers in `combat.js` and `archery.js` pass none), and its next `updateZombie` makes the call first thing, before a stagger or a fall returns early, so a struck one calls on the step after the blow even while reeling or down.
- **Who hears it:** every other dead one that is not hunting a player (`huntsPlayer` false), not down, and not dead, within **`zombie.pullRadius` 4 tiles** (*default*) of the caller, with a clear line of sight to it (`lineOfSight`, the walls and shut doors and shutters that stop a player's sight). **The light counts as it does for a player:** the radius is scaled by the light on the caller, by the rule `canSee` uses (`sightDarkFraction` 0.35 in the dark, the whole of it lit), so a pull in the dark draws fewer (about 1.4 tiles unlit), as Michael's "safe" asks of a careful player (*default*). The scaling becomes one helper, `sightRange(base, gx, gy)`, which `canSee`, `canSeeCharacter`, and the pull share (it is written out twice today). `pullRadius` 0 skips the scan.
- **What they do:** each joins the hunt as if it had been struck from where the caller learnt of the player: `huntsPlayer` and `aggro` true, `lastSeen` the caller's `lastSeen` (the player's place when seen, or where the blow came from), `sincePlayer` 0; from idle or wander it turns to the chase on its next step, as `updateZombie` already does for a dead one with `aggro` (one getting up does too). It knows where to go, not who: its own sight check then finds the player, or it reaches the spot and searches, as the dead already do.
- **No chain:** a dead one drawn in already has `huntsPlayer`, so seeing the player itself later is no turn and calls no one; nor does it while that hunt lasts. After `giveUp` a later first-hand turn is a pull again.
- **Once:** the call is made at the turn and never repeated while that hunt lasts; a caller walking past another group calls no one (Michael's choice: "one time broadcast to nearby").
- **When two turn on the same step,** the order the world holds the dead decides which calls first; the check is staged so its rows do not depend on it.
- **The setting:** nothing when Zombie aggro is off (no turn happens). No world rule to switch sight-pull off is added (*default*); `zombie.pullRadius` 0 switches it off for tuning (`42`).

**What it means in play:** a long throw or arrow at one at the edge of a group, out of the others' line or in the dark, is still a safe pull of one; the same shot into the middle of a group brings everyone round it that can see it. Bring a pack on yourself and that was your choice: no danger here rises with time (the clock law).

## 4. Seen and heard

**A tell, so a player can read what a pull did** (*defaults*), only **when at least one answers** (a first sighting that draws no one shows nothing new, or every sighting would shout): the caller says its call over its head (`say`, a new line `zombie.pullText`, "Hrraagh!", in `data/tuning.json` and `src/tuning.js` beside `groanText`), and each one it draws in shows a "!" bubble for a second, made as the cowering villager's bubble is (`entities/villager.js`, `cowerBubble`) through `say`. A guest sees both as it sees every line the dead say; nothing new crosses the wire. **The sound** is the moan `docs/40` already plays when one of the dead turns from calm to closing in (`sound-cues.js`, `moanCfg`), for the caller and each one drawn in; the bubbles are its twin. An unseen call in the dark writes the log's "Something groans in the dark." as an unseen groan does (`ui/speech.js`): heard, never drawing anyone (sound is never aggro).

## 5. Inside the laws, and the guest's side

- **The clock law:** nothing grows with time; a pull is a player's act.
- **Determinism:** the pull reads the dead in the order the world holds them and rolls nothing; it runs in the host's step (`updateZombie`), never in a client. The headless hash runs with Zombie aggro off, so it cannot see the pull: `check("pull")` is its proof.
- **The guest's side:** the hunt is the host's. A dead one reaches a guest as its whole record once and then as `slim` (`net.js`), which carries its `state` and its `line` and not `aggro`; the chase the joined ones turn to reaches a guest as their `state`, and the tells as their lines (`say`). `huntsPlayer` and `pullDue` stay on the host, as `aggro`, `lastSeen`, and `siege` do: saved with the dead one's record, outside the frame and outside `game.syncParts`' `dead` part (which holds none of the dead's hunting state). No wire field is added and `net.PROTOCOL` is not bumped. A guest's blow is resolved on the host, so a guest's pull is the host's, by the same rule.

## 6. Build step

1. **Sight-pull** (#__): `huntsPlayer` and `pullDue` on the dead (set, saved, cleared in `giveUp`), the call in `updateZombie` (the turn first-hand, `sightRange`, the line, the joined ones' `huntsPlayer`, `aggro`, and `lastSeen`), the tells (`zombie.pullText`, the "!" bubble, only when one answers), the lever in `data/tuning.json`, `src/tuning.js`, and `42`. `check("pull")`, staged so no two of the dead turn on one step: one struck at the edge of a group out of the others' line brings none; one struck in the middle brings those within the radius that see it and none beyond; a killing blow brings none; one that turned to a torch-bearer's flame first still pulls when it sees the player; a drawn-in one that then sees the player calls no one; a pull in the dark reaches less far than by day; with `pullRadius` 0 nothing joins; the call's bubble shows only when one answers; the guest sees the joined ones chase and both bubbles, and `check("sync")` passes in two tabs.
