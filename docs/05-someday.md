# 05 — Someday

Things Michael wants kept possible but not scheduled. Nothing here is a plan. The rule for code: **do not write anything in a way that forecloses these cheaply.** When a design choice would make one of them expensive, say so before making it.

Started 2026-10-08.

- **The big cart** (Michael, 2026-10-15): an immovable wagon that several horses pull, a big container you cannot lift, which the wheelbarrow had been mixed up with; the wheelbarrow is a container carried like a barrel (another wooden container's art, with or without more storage, as a crate is).

- **Quests and RPG-like elements.** Part of the original medieval wish. Never say never, but the game has to be playable, fun, and content-rich first. NPCs with lines, routines, and bags are the groundwork; a quest is a line plus a check plus a reward, and all three exist by then.
- **NPC relationships and trade.** A vendor you keep alive sells cheaper, "yadda yadda", miles down the road. Bags on NPCs are the groundwork.
- **Roofs.** Drawn and hidden when inside. Not for the probe, no real art yet. Nothing in the renderer may assume buildings are open to the sky forever.
- **Upper floors.** Single storey first. Since 2026-10-09 every lookup, chunk key, light cache, and entity carries a layer, always 0. Assessed after the WebGL renderer landed, from easiest to hardest: (1) data, a building's `floors` list placing layer-1 tiles, walls, and props the same way, plus a `stairs` opening; (2) drawing, every layer-1 vertex lifted one storey (64 px) and sorted as if one tile nearer on each axis, which is what a storey of height is worth in this projection; (3) hiding, every layer above the player's while the player is indoors, which is also the roof rule; (4) rooms and light per layer, which the keys allow and the flood fill needs told; (5) stairs as a two-tile ramp that moves an entity's layer, and the dead pathing across layers through them; (6) sight stays on your layer, other layers drawn present but fogged. The one new physics is light crossing floors: an upstairs window lighting the ground below. Nothing in the renderer or the data forbids any of it; stairs and cross-layer pathing are the bulk of the work, the renderer part is the smallest.
- **Building and expanding.** Find a castle and expand it. Kept open at zero cost by walls-as-tiles and flood-fill rooms. Also the dev tool: a creative mode that places tiles is how the village gets authored. Question 14 in `02-decisions.md`. **Built 2026-10-11** (`13`): the build palette on `N`, walls, doors, and windows paid in logs and planks (`data/build.json`), and removing what you built; Build is in every right-click menu since 2026-10-12. The dev-tool half is answered otherwise: the map is generated from a description per place (`tools/mapgen/`, `30` step 0), not placed in game.
- **Zones, or biomes.** Regions of the map with their own register: the harvest village now, a Tirisfal-gloomy wood later with blue and purple trees, the plague doctor, scarecrow men. A zone is a rectangle with a palette and a scatter mix, which the scatter rule already half is. Michael's rule: harvest first, until the farming game is fun.
- **The countryside.** Block storage allows the world to grow past the village. Authored places, generated fields between. Question 9 in `02-decisions.md`. **Ruled 2026-10-12:** the 1.0 map is a valley 256 by 256 with the river, the castle, the village, two farms, and the forest places (`29-world`), written by `tools/mapgen/valley.py` (`30` step 0); the wild still grows beyond it.
- **A zoom-out.** Integer scaling makes it awkward. Revisit if the village feels cramped at Zomboid's 100 percent. **Built 2026-10-10:** three zoom levels the GPU applies (wide 1.1, middle 1.8, close 2.7; `src/view.js`).
- **Infection, treatable.** **Started 2026-10-12 as a marker only (`src/infection.js`, `02`): a chance per landed bite, a moodle green to red over 1.5 days, no effect yet; Michael wants it back for the stakes, with fair ways to mitigate and a clock that is not under two days.** **Tied to combat difficulty (Michael, 2026-10-12): see `02`. If this is added, the dead's damage and numbers come down with it; if it never is, combat has to carry the danger alone.** The cozy parent's "not now, not never". Moodles leave the door open with a queasy icon that might be bad stew or might be the bite.
- **Armour.** More slots of the shield's shape. The sibling's post-1.0 note.
- **Warmth and cold.** The sibling had a cold moodle the fireplace answers. Here fire is already everything; a winter would make it more so. Not scheduled. **Ruled 2026-10-12:** no cold in 1.0, not until seasons (`30`).
- **Low-fantasy headroom.** A cursed blade, a staff, something in the woods. The setting allows it; nothing asks for it.
- **Animals.** Hunting, a goat, a chicken. The sibling's entity-registry idea. Farming comes first. **Ruled 2026-10-12:** two animals in 1.0, last in the big pass: chickens and deer (`30`).
- **The shuttered lantern.** A carried light you can hide without putting it out: the torch's upgrade, if the torch ever needs one. The carried-light term in `light.js` is a radius with an on flag, so a lantern is a second item with the same shape. Ruled out of Milestone 2 on 2026-10-09.
- **Candles.** The cozy parent's stealthy room light. Torches took their job here; a candle would be a tiny fire kind in the same table, and nothing stops one being added.
- **A great hall.** The hearth lights its whole room by one rule. A room big enough to make that silly wants a cap by room size, one line, when such a room exists.
- **Stub walls.** The near walls between the player and the camera drawn as low stumps instead of translucent full walls, the cozy parent's way. Lived on the `K` key beside the translucent walls from 2026-10-09 until the drawn walls came (2026-10-11), when the mode retired without art; Michael keeps the idea. Bringing it back needs cut-down segments per material and orientation (a cut cap, a door gap, window posts) and the stump drawing in `makeWall` (`src/render/walls.js`).
- **A character gear screen.** Hand slots exist from Milestone 2 with the `main`, `off`, `both` designation on every holdable item; a gear screen is a view of them plus armour slots later. **Built 2026-10-10** (`11`): the character screen on `C`, the two hands and the worn slots, the belt and the bag as worn items; armour slots wait for armour.
- **Burning: a character on fire** (Michael, 2026-10-12, "a flight of fancy, nowhere near a requirement": flaming arrows, a torch blow with a chance to set alight). **The visual was built the same day as a test** (`src/fire.js`): particles ride the figure, the sprite pulses orange, the body is a light (`light.carriers()`). **Made real 2026-10-15 (#14, `02`):** a status like bleeding capped at one (`src/burn.js`), only on the dead, from a torch's blow by chance, the fireball, and fire arrows dipped at a lit fire; the menu's Set on fire and Put out are gone. Still not built, from the list below: the dead noticing, a way out, a player burning, and spreading. What it would take to mean something: a cause (a torch blow, a flaming arrow), damage over time, the dead noticing, a way out (water, rolling, time), and how it spreads between characters, if at all. Claude's look-up, for the conversation later: fire that does not look bad in pixel art at this size is **particles, not a shader and not a painted flame layer**: a handful of small flame sprites (three or four frames, six to ten pixels) spawned at random points on the figure's silhouette, rising and dying over a third of a second, with a few ember pixels above and dark smoke pixels, the way Terraria's "On Fire!" and Stardew do it; plus **a flicker tint on the figure** (an orange-yellow wash pulsing) and, the part that sells it most, **the burning body as a moving light source** with the torch's flicker (`light.js` already carries the off-hand torch, so a burning zombie lighting the ground around it at night is nearly free). The arrow already draws a sprite at a height above a world point, which is what a particle is here, sorted with the figure. A painted "flame cloak" per facing looks like a sticker and a noise shader looks un-pixel; neither. A flaming arrow is a short trail of orange pixels and a light in flight. **Fire spreading to grass and thatch is a different and much bigger thing** (a map simulation): not implied by this, do not foreclose it, do not start it by accident.
- **Weapon durability** (Michael, 2026-10-12): one-way and permanent, wears only on a landed hit (never a miss), the item evaporates at zero, no repair, no dulling; the smith and crafting stations are the supply (`22`). Waits for them. **Built 2026-10-13 as wear by kills** (`30`, 5.2, step 3): each weapon's `kills` in `data/items.json`, the wear per landed hit derived from it, the entry gone at the end, a guest's weapon worn on the host; the anvil's recipes are the supply (`data/recipes.json`); `check("wear")`.
- **Health potions** (Michael, 2026-10-12): liked, "needs to be balanced around, so just an idea for now". Bleed and bandages (`02`, question 29b) are the first step of making health matter.
- **Bleed from sources other than bites**, and a real infection (effects, cures, mushrooms and brewing as a fair way to mitigate, a clock that is never under about two in-game days to turn; the marker's 1.5 days in `data/combat.json` is "about two" for now, Michael, 2026-10-12): after the marker (`02`); by the one-dial rule, combat is eased only if the result does not feel balanced.
- **Endless, a permanent game mode** (Michael, 2026-10-13): the horde of `36` with supply to keep up, so efficient play really can go on (the dead's pockets as a supply line, a delivery at dawn, bodies that clear when unseen), and with it a leaderboard and the community things a score invites (Steam's leaderboards, `35`). Distinct from the demo, whose supplies last a few days by design.
- **Villagers who remember** (Michael, 2026-10-13: wanted, "way later", after 1.0): first-meeting lines and "oh, you again", from a per-character record of what was heard (`34`, the `when` list), on the player, in the save and the sync check. It writes only the talker's own record, never the world, so talk's law holds. Kept open by stable node ids in `data/talk/`.
- **Scripted sequences** (Michael, 2026-10-13; a stretch goal, "what am I getting myself into"). Moments written ahead: at an hour of a given day a villager bursts through the door with news; the first time anyone walks into the crypt, the lid moves; the dead's arrival that ends the quiet first day of the long arc (`00`, `02`). **What one needs:** a beat list as data (`data/scripts/`), each beat a step for an entity by id: walk to a tile (the villagers' walking exists, `movement.repath`), face, say a line (`say`, and the bark machinery of `34`), open or shut a door, play an animation (`32`), spawn or wake the dead, wait for seconds or for a condition; and a trigger (an hour of a day, a place entered, a thing first seen, another beat done). **What it may do that talk may not:** write the world. A script sets world flags ("the dead have come", "the crypt is open"), and talk reads them, which is how the townsfolk come to speak of what happened (`34`'s `when`). **The rules it must keep:** it plays in the world, never as a cutscene (no camera taken, no input taken, no cut: pillar 1); it runs in the host's step as a sequence of verbs on ids, in the save and in the sync check, and a guest sees it through the records and the frames as it sees everything else, so it is for whoever is near, not for "the player"; and the clock law (`00`): a beat on a timer may bring news or flavour, but never a rise in danger, or it must be tied to something a player did (the arrival best of all when the players go to bed the first night, `02`). **Cost:** medium to large; the runner and the format are modest, and the content and the testing of every trigger with two players are the bulk. Built on `34` (talk) and `04` (villagers that follow a goal). **Kept open cheaply meanwhile:** entities addressed by id, villager movement callable with a goal, `say` generic, and world state that a flag could later join.
- **Co-op: a second human player.** **No longer someday: built and live since 2026-10-13** (`docs/31-multiplayer`, the relay at `wss://cmz-relay.fly.dev`), and **every addition and every change is now built for it in the same commit** (Michael, 2026-10-13; `CLAUDE.md`'s first rule, `31/08`). What follows is the feasibility look of 2026-10-12, kept as the record of how it was decided; its "single player first, this only breaks ties" is replaced. (Michael, 2026-10-12; a feasibility look, not a scope.) The question was whether the unified character model makes a second player possible, and whether sync would ruin the fun the way it does in Zomboid (where two players fighting one zombie make it jump about). **Verdict: feasible, large, and the Zomboid problem is avoidable by design; do the single-player vision first, keep the doors open.**
- **Multiplayer, the later three** (Michael, 2026-10-15: "leave them for later, but write them down"; the core is called Done without them, `02`). Nothing built since forecloses any of them; keep it so. (1) **Spectating.** A dead player watches until they choose New character or Leave world, through their own body's sight as `31/03` ruled (the room they died in, as a corpse would see it; no free camera, no new sight rule). Today the dead screen offers the two choices at once. Keep the body a character with a position and a sight, and the dead screen a screen over a world still drawn. (2) **Lighter frames on busy nights.** Two parts. An animation sent once, when it starts, as its name and its starting tick, the guest running its frames itself (today a moving character's record goes out at every frame change, rounded, `net.js` `roundAnim`); and an interest radius, each guest sent only the characters within a box round its own player (`31/04` named an 80-tile box), the rest leaving its roster and coming back whole. Measure first with `net.status().wire` on a night with the dead in numbers; build it only when a frame's weight is felt. Keep every record keyed by id and every list's membership a roster, so a character can leave a guest's view and come back. (3) **The bounded rewind.** A guest's swing or arrow is judged by the host against where things were when the guest saw them, up to 150 ms back (`31/04`), so a hit that looked clean on a slow link lands. Today the host judges against where things are now. Keep each character's last few positions cheap to keep (a ring of positions by tick), and the swing a command that carries the tick it was made at.
  - **What helps already:** a fixed 60 Hz step; the world's growth is deterministic from the seed, so only the changes from the authored map need to travel; every character is plain data with an id on one model; the sim had 18 `Math.random` calls when this was written (26 by the night of 2026-10-12; `23a` C1 lists them and `23` step 2 replaces them with one seeded generator); the map has about 16 mutators, so the world-edit surface is small; the renderer, the ground, the fog, and the light are per-client and never sync; the dead are slow (1 tile/s), so a hundred milliseconds of lag is a tenth of a tile.
  - **What blocks it:** "the player" is a singleton (about 480 mentions; the ones that matter are about ten: the dead's targeting and wake distance, chunk growth and the spawns it queues, the one carried light, the pause key); there is no server (`serve.py` is a static server with no WebSockets); hit-stop freezes game time, which one player's blow cannot do to another's sim; props have no ids (their tile serves); items, doors, boards, fires, and containers all need to become commands to one authority.
  - **The design that avoids Zomboid's glitch:** **one authority** (the host's browser runs the whole sim; a client sends inputs and receives snapshots and events), because Zomboid hands each zombie to whichever player is nearest, and two players fighting one zombie flips its owner. With one authority a zombie has one position and one hp, always. The cost is the client's: their hits land a round trip later, and the dead they see are about 100 ms old.
  - **Rough size, by tier (not time):** 0, a one-way viewer (the host's world rendered in a second browser from snapshots): small, and it proves the pipe and the interpolation. 1, a second walking player who sees the dead and is hunted by them: medium (the singleton work plus the net layer). 2, host-resolved melee with local swing prediction: medium. 3, doors, building, containers, fires, floor items, and inventory moves as host commands: **the largest by volume and the likeliest to cause duplication bugs**. 4, a bow with lag compensation (an arrow at 16 tiles/s is 1.6 tiles in 100 ms), joining mid-game, reconnecting, saves: medium to high. Overall larger than any one milestone so far, perhaps several milestones' worth; and **every feature after it carries a "how does this sync" cost**.
  - **Transport options:** WebRTC data channels with a one-time copy-paste handshake between two friends (no server, works through most home routers, no relay for the unlucky); or a small WebSocket relay beside `serve.py` (Python's standard library has no WebSocket, so a hand-written handshake of about a hundred lines or a package).
  - **The cheap test before committing:** two tabs on one machine with an artificial 80 to 120 ms delay between them, once tiers 1 and 2 exist; that is the real answer to whether it feels good, at a fraction of the whole.
  - **The standing rule (Michael, 2026-10-12, also in `CLAUDE.md`):** when a single-player choice forks and one path favours a future second player while the other bakes in the single perspective, all else equal take the multiplayer-friendly path. Single player is finished first; this only breaks ties.
  - **Keep it possible, for free, from now on:** no new `Math.random` or `performance.now` inside the simulation when a seeded or stepped value will do; every world change goes through the map's mutators or an event, never by poking tiles; keep ids on everything that moves; pass "the player" as an argument rather than reaching for a global; no feature that freezes or pauses the whole sim (hit-stop is the one that does today; its screen shake can stay local); keep each player's UI state (bag, belt, hands, camera) separate from the world's.
