# 06 — Art direction and the dev palette

Michael, 2026-10-09: spooky medieval, a bit haunted, never permanently night. Not Stardew's bright lawn; closer to Tirisfal Glades, a place where Halloween never quite ends. **Read since 2026-10-25 as the outer regions' register, not the valley's** (Michael: "Tirisfal was always for the outer regions"): the valley is cheery, start-as-Stardew, and each region beyond is spookier than the last (`00`; how bright the valley is drawn is #426). The dev art should carry that identity now, before real art, because a visual identity helps motivation. The character placeholders were kept at first; later the same day the player got worn yellowed linen with sleeves and brown breeches (no denim), and the dead stayed as they were.

## The genre test

Three AI reference pieces Michael made earlier set the vibe, not the art: a steel-blue knight with a crimson cape on black, a peasant zombie with olive-green skin and brown rags, dark outlines everywhere. The test for any colour in the world: **what would that peasant zombie be standing in?** Muted, cool-shifted, a little dusky. Saturation lives in a few accents (crimson, harvest yellow, firelight), not in the ground.

## Gloomwood: the two registers

Michael's clearest statement of the vibe (2026-10-09), word-vomit mood board and all. The working name he had on the reference art was **Gloomwood**. Two registers pull against each other and both should exist somehow:

- **The cosy register is aggressively autumn.** Harvest colours, pumpkins, ochre and amber. The north star: it can never get so grim that farming feels bad.
- **The gloomy register is Tirisfal.** Dark, earthy, blues and purples and greens mixed in. A green vial of poison. A plague doctor. Not as bright as Tirisfal itself.

It is a balancing act the whole way, and the answer is **by zone**: the two registers do not share a wood. Blue and purple canopies next to orange ones looked like Halloween candy (2026-10-09), so the first map is all harvest, and the gloomy register's trees wait on file for a zone of their own. More forest than plains, and the forest is scattered by rule rather than placed by hand.

## Rules for dev art

- **Walls are thick.** Thin walls read modern; thick walls read chonky and cosy. Since 2026-10-11 walls are **80 px tall**, centred on their edge line, **16 px thick in stone, 14 in wood, 18 in log** (the parent's were 6), and drawn by Claude Design (`17`).
- **Walls are wood planks, stone, or logs** (the logs are what the player builds), and since 2026-10-12 a **fence**, a low see-through wall with gates for doors (`wall_fence`, a generated dev wall, 36 px tall and 6 thick, until drawn; `30` step 0). Plaster is gone. Corners and wall ends get a drawn post, the heavier material winning where two meet (stone over log over wood), and a post also stands where the material changes along a straight wall.
- **Windows are holes with a frame, a sill, and wooden shutters.** No glass in this village. Closed shutters are two plank leaves meeting in the middle; open ones fold back almost flat against the outside wall (about 160 degrees, Michael 2026-10-11).
- **Doors are plank leaves with two dark metal strips.** Closed, the leaf fills the doorway; open, it stands at 90 degrees on the outside at the hinge jamb, drawn edge-on with its thickness showing. Boards over a door or window are drawn overlays, one to three planks, the same on every material.
- **Fittings are one wood everywhere** (`#5e4028`, strips `#2b2a32`), whatever the wall is made of.
- **The setting is dark medieval fantasy.** Skeletons, plague doctors, scarecrow men would fit. Never a ceiling.
- **Floors are wood or stone indoors; grass, dirt, farm, cobble, water, or shallow water outdoors** (the waters since 2026-10-12: water is walked by nothing, shallow water slowly by everyone). Re-decided 2026-10-11 (`15-ground.md`): cobblestone is allowed for squares and later roads, in dusky warm greys, never concrete; it is always laid in a dirt verge and never touches grass. Roads are dirt until authored otherwise. A farm tile is **dark tilled soil** with furrows, no plants (Michael, 2026-10-11: the crops supply the warmth; crops, the pumpkins among them, are sprites standing on it). Claude Design's soil arrived 2026-10-11, in four variants and two furrow directions (`floor_farm`, `floor_farm_y`, each drawn). **Crops** stand on it as interactable props, one sprite per crop and stage (potato, rye, cabbage, garlic, pumpkin; planted, growing, ready), their rows following the soil's furrows (`17`). The earth blends with ragged edges; laid floors are hard diamonds; there is no checker anywhere.
- **Grass is dull.** A greyed green with a hint of blue, not a mowed lawn. Since the ground kit (2026-10-11) the grass is Michael's drawn tiles with base `(75, 95, 61)`, tinted per chunk between lush and dry and darker under canopy (`15-ground.md`).
- **Fog is dusky purple at a light hand.** What you cannot see is shaded clearly but never distracts from what is there.
- **Backgrounds are near-black purple**, not blue-grey.
- **Only trees are seen through** (Michael, 2026-10-11: "that's really just for trees"; walls have their own fade). **The player behind a tree is seen through a soft hole in the canopy**, not through a faded tree, so overlapping canopies never stack into mud. **The dead are not**: a zombie behind a tree is hidden by it, and its health bar hides too, so a forest never gives laser vision (Michael, 2026-10-11).
- **The trees are drawn art** (Michael with Claude Design, 2026-10-11): 36 PNGs in `data/art/trees/`, two shapes (**oak**, round and layered; **spire**, conical and tiered), three sizes, six colours (green, yellow, orange, red, blue, purple), drawn natively at the size the game shows them, so no definition carries a `scale`. The game's tiers are the sheet's S, M, and L as `m`, `l`, and the giant `old` (the sheet's small tree stood as tall as the player and read too short, so there is no small tier); each definition in `data/sprites/` carries the trunk's anchor, its `collide` radius, its `wood`, its canopy's reach for the see-through hole, and `interactable`. The harvest forest (`tools/mapgen/valley.py`, `HARVEST`) weighs oak `m` and `l` at 1, oak giants 0.5, spire `m` and `l` 0.5, and spire giants 0.25, so giants are one tree in five and about a third of the forest is spires; the gloom colours are defined and placed in no map yet. Ids: `tree_oak_<m|l|old>` is the green oak, `tree_<orange|yellow|red|blue|purple>_<m|l|old>` the other oaks, `tree_spire_<colour>_<m|l|old>` the spires.
- **Tree canopies are lumpy, not round.** A jittered polygon, trunks taper with a root flare, not a rectangle (`src/placeholders.js`, `generateTree`, 2026-10-11). A cartoon-circle canopy reads "happy", and the same shapes have to carry Gloomwood's blue and purple trees later without a mood change, so the outline stays a little ragged on purpose.

## The palette

Colours, sizes, and collision radii live in `data/sprites/*.json` and in the drawn files under `data/art/`; when one changes, nothing here changes with it (the table this page carried until 2026-10-16 is `history/06-art-palette-2026-10-11.md`). What no file states is the direction: **grass is dull**, a greyed green with a hint of blue (the drawn tiles' base is `#4b5f3d`, tinted per chunk between lush and dry and darker under canopy); **fog is dusky purple at a light hand**; **the canvas background is near-black purple** (`#16121c`, `style.css`); **fittings are one wood everywhere** (`#5e4028`, strips `#2b2a32`), whatever the wall. Everything still generated is placeholder, and the drawn art came in through the same sprite definitions, files not code; a station or a piece of furniture with no art yet is a coloured box with its name drawn on it (`placeholder.label`; `30` step 1).
