# 51 — A depth buffer: the frame sorted per pixel, not per tile

**Status, 2026-10-27: built on `build/depth-buffer`, #530, steps 1 to 4 and 6; step 5, `besideWalls` removed, waits for `check("depth")` rows 1 to 4 to pass in the pane; ruled by Michael in session (`02`, 2026-10-26, A depth buffer); the renderer as it now is, `08`.** Read as built: a billboard's height is counted above its own ground point's row (the plane through that point), as the tree's arithmetic in section 3 counts it; the emit-order step is two buffer steps or 1/32 of a unit, whichever is more (0.25 at 16 bits); a quad goes to the translucent pass by its colour's alpha, an occluder's flag, or a soft sheet, per quad, which covers every case in section 4's list. ## 1. Why

Every frame today is one list of walls, posts, props, items, figures, arrows, and fire, sorted by a single number per drawable, the tile's depth `tx + ty` with a small offset by kind, and painted back to front (`render/scene.js`). That is right for a thing that stays inside its tile and wrong for anything that doesn't: a prop whose picture spills into the next tile is covered by that tile's wall; Object Highlight's outline is covered with it (the "two thirds of an outline" Michael sees); wide furniture, tall trees in front of houses, and props against walls clip. Figures alone get a correction (`besideWalls`, `render/entities.js`), a box test that clamps them between the walls around them, which is why people rarely clip and props often do. Upper floors (`49`) add lifted storeys to the same list.

A depth buffer ends the per-tile sort: each pixel keeps the depth of what it last drew and refuses anything farther. A figure half behind a wall is half behind it. Project Zomboid's renderer gives its 2D tiles depth for the same reason, so its 3D models cut into them.

## 2. The geometry

`art/01`: world X and Y run along the ground grid, one unit per screen pixel horizontally, a tile 32 by 32 units; Z is height in screen pixels. `screen x = X − Y`, `screen y = (X + Y) / 2 − Z`, and **depth = X + Y + Z toward the camera.** In grid units a tile is 1, so a point's depth in pixels is `(gx + gy) * 32 + height`. Two points overlap on screen only when one is at `(X + d, Y + d, Z + d)` from the other, `3d` deeper, so a nearer thing is always the larger depth.

**Depth is its own number, computed on the CPU from true geometry.** The scan showed why the vertices' existing `a_world` and `a_height` cannot serve as the depth: a wall face's world points are the light's sample points, set 0.2 to 0.4 tile inside the tile in front on purpose (`walls.js` ~438-444), and its heights count from the frame's bottom, not the floor; moving them would move the light. So **the vertex grows by one float, `depth`** (STRIDE 13 to 14, `a_depth`), set by every emitter; `a_world` and `a_height` go on serving the light and the night pool untouched.

## 3. The depth of each thing

The rule for a standing picture: **its depth plane passes through the front of what it stands for, not its centre.** A sprite's picture reaches to the near corner of its footprint (a prop's frame ends about 16 px below its tile centre on screen; a body's radius is 0.3 tile), and a plane through the centre would put every pixel past the centre behind the ground, the floor, or a wall the thing is in front of. With the front as the reference, a figure stopped 0.3 tile from a back wall is about 28 depth units in front of the wall's near face at every height, and clear of it.

- **A one-tile prop, a floor item, a fire: two face planes, split at the front corner.** One plane through the tile's near corner would sit 32 − T/2 units in front of a south or east wall's near face toward the wall's far end, so a bed just inside the house wall would show a strip through it from outside, and a figure before a chest's flank would lose a strip (the recheck's two cases, #520). So each slice's quad is **cut at the column of its footprint's front corner** `(tx + 0.5, ty + 0.5)` into two: the left half lies on the footprint's south-west face, `y = ty + 0.5`, and the right half on its south-east face, `x = tx + 0.5`, each with depth linear in screen x along its face (the face's two ends' depths), each pixel's depth being the face's ground depth at that column plus the pixel's height above the face's ground line. The near wall then wins by 1.5 T, the back wall loses by about 1.5 (64 − T), and a figure before the flank is in front by 48 − 1.5 δ. A two-tile piece is already sliced per tile (`src/CLAUDE.md`), each slice its two faces. **Trees and every canopy prop are the exception: a billboard through the footprint's front corner**, the figure's form, `(tx + ty + 1) * 32 + height`, constant across columns. Split planes extended past a wide canopy fall back 1.5 units a column, so a large oak (154 to 158 px wide) a tile clear of a house wall would lose its outer 20 px to the wall, which today's sort keeps; the billboard holds an adjacent oak to 64 − T (about 50 px right of the trunk) and one a tile clear to 128 − T, past any canopy, and a figure behind-right of a tree stays behind its canopy.
- **A figure** (a player, a dead one, a villager, an animal): the plane through `(gx + r, gy + r)`, `r` its radius (0.3), so `(gx + gy + 0.6) * 32 + height`. The dead's hits and bars are DOM and unaffected.
- **A wall face:** the true near face of the wall as a plane: for a north wall on `y = ty − 0.5` with thickness `T` in units, the face at `y = ty − 0.5 + T / 2`, its two ends' depths at `x = tx − 0.5` and `tx + 0.5`, plus height from 0 at the floor to the wall's height at the top (80 today, 96 with `52`). A west wall likewise on `x`. Posts: the near corner of the post's square. **Caps lie on the face's plane extended up**: the face sprite's own cap pixels are drawn there, so the cap redrawn on the sky takes the same plane plus the bias (below), never a horizontal plane of its own, which would sit up to 1.5 T farther and lose to the face (the 2026-10-12 lit-cap bug). **An open door leaf** stands at right angles to its wall and takes its own plane along its swing, through the hinge, its two ends' depths.
- **Arrows in flight:** the plane through their ground point plus their `z`.
- **The ground and the lifted floors** (`49`): the plane of their corners, height 0 or the storey's.
- **One depth surface per drawable, its quads layered by emit order.** Every quad a drawable emits after its base (a cap redrawn on the sky, a flame over a hearth or a torch tip, a held item, boards and leaves and shutters over a wall, a bracket's torch, the bees' near half, a floor item's halo, a burning body's flames and smoke, the swing outline's 1 px copy, Object Highlight's eight copies) takes **the same plane with a small bias** that increases with emit order (+0.25 a quad, *default*, two 16-bit steps at the range below), so today's layering decisions all stand and nothing loses to its own base. Object Highlight's copies and the swing outline take the base's plane minus the bias, behind it.
- **Biases between kinds** (depth units, *defaults*, in one table in `scene.js`): floor items +0.5 over the ground; posts +0.5 over the walls meeting at them (they cover the seam); a lifted floor +1 over the caps of the walls below it (they meet at 96 px exactly); props +1 and figures +2 over the ground. Everything else is its true geometry.

## 4. The two passes, and what is flat

A depth buffer and transparency do not mix: a half-transparent pixel still writes its depth and hides what is behind it. The delivered art is hard alpha (`art/01`; the scan found no soft pixel in the 723 PNGs under `data/art`), but some generated art is not, and several effects are translucent by design. So:

- **Pass 0, the flat things, depth write off.** The ground (its own draw calls, first, as today), then rugs, the tile mark, tracks, floor-lying arrows' pictures, and the arrows' shadows: everything that lies on the ground plane, in today's order, writing no depth. Nothing standing has drawn yet, so they need no test. (The landing marker's rings stay with its line, on top, as `emitMarker` draws them today.) **With `49`'s lifted floors, pass 0 runs layer by layer from the lowest:** after the ground and its flat things, each lifted layer in view draws its floor quads with depth write on and tested (they are standing geometry that must hide what is under them) and then its own flat things, write off and tested, which the floor's written depth protects from everything under it in pass 1.
- **Pass 1, opaque, depth write on**, the fragment shader discarding alpha under 0.5. Walls and their caps, posts, props, items' standing pictures, figures, arrows, fire particles that are hard. Drawn in today's key order (not front to back: with discard and blending that saves nothing and flips every tie).
- **Pass 2, translucent, depth test on, depth write off**, sorted by today's key, the shader's 0.004 discard only. Marked `translucent` on the drawable:
  - the near walls at `NEAR_WALL_ALPHA` 0.42, **their cut pieces included** (`walls.js` ~174, 283, drawn at 0.42 too), and the posts faded with them;
  - a faded storey's floor quads (`49` section 6), tested and writing none, as its walls;
  - the occluders: any drawable with `FLAG_OCCLUDER` (a canopy 64 px or taller), since the holes fade its alpha in the shader;
  - a dead one **dimmed by the dark** (`dim` < 1, `entities.js` ~216, `sight.js`'s `seenAlpha`), smoke, embers, splinters (`entities.js` ~170-176), a rising rock's dust (`props.js` ~139-147);
  - **the soft generated sheets**, whole: the campfire's, hearth's, torch post's, lantern post's, and snare's shadows and radial glows, the flame sheet's glow, and the burn sheet's smoke (`placeholders.js` ~593-1137, `fire.js` ~176) have alpha between 0 and 1 and would vanish under the test. Each such sheet carries `soft: true` from its generator, and **every quad on a soft sheet goes to pass 2, keeping its drawable's plane and bias**, per quad and not per drawable: a wall's bracket torch (`walls.js` ~502) and a figure's carried torch tip (`entities.js` ~291) emit `flame` quads from pass-1 drawables, and their halos would be cut by the alpha test otherwise. They keep their place in the picture (pass 2 is depth-tested) and occlude nothing behind them drawn later in pass 2, which today's sort already orders. When those sheets are redrawn hard, the flag goes.
- **Last, on top, depth test off:** the placement ghost (its tint and its geometry, in one place), the remove-ghost (as `emitGhost` draws it today, `props.js` ~70), the landing marker (its rings and its line), and the hit shapes, as today (`scene.js` ~183-185; *default*, Primary's recommendation: a ghost hidden behind a wall is useless).
- **The fog** (a per-fragment mix), **the night pool** (`poolAt`), **the legibility floor** (`FLAG_STANDING`), and **the lights** are per fragment in the shader and work the same in every pass. **The debug views** are unchanged.

## 5. WebGL

- `gl.depthFunc(gl.LEQUAL)` set explicitly (the default is `LESS`, which gives ties to the first drawn, the wrong way for every bias here); `gl.enable(gl.DEPTH_TEST)` in `setup()`, so it survives a lost context; `gl.depthMask(true)` **before every `gl.clear`** (the clear honours the mask, and pass 2 leaves it off); the colour and depth cleared together.
- **The range follows the camera, not the light area, and is ±4096:** `u_depthBase = (cam.gx + cam.gy) * 32 − 4096`, span 8192, and `z_ndc = 1 − 2 (depth − u_depthBase) / 8192`, so the whole −1 to 1 of clip space is used and larger depth (nearer) is the smaller z that LEQUAL keeps, with `gl.depthRange(0, 1)`. A visible pixel's depth relative to the camera is about 2 s + 3 Z (s world pixels below the screen's centre, Z its height: a height counts three times at a fixed screen row), so a 3840 by 2160 window at zoom 1.1 (half-height 982 CSS pixels, `view.js`) reaches 2252 for the top of a 96 px wall at the bottom edge and 2444 for a 160 px tree; ±2048 would cut their tops, ±4096 holds them with cellars and three storeys (`49`) to spare. WebGL2 clips outside the range, so the base must move with the camera every frame, never with the chunked light area. (Sizing the half-range from the canvas each frame, `h / zoom + 3 Hmax` plus a margin, is the alternative if precision ever wants it.)
- **Precision:** the context's default depth is guaranteed only 16 bits (65536 steps over 8192 units is 0.125 unit a step); the bias above is 0.25 a quad, two steps at that worst case, and most browsers give 24 bits (0.0005 a step); the build reads `gl.getParameter(gl.DEPTH_BITS)` and sizes the bias to two steps of what it got.
- The context stays `{ antialias: false, premultipliedAlpha: true, alpha: false }`; the zoom (`view.js`) scales screen positions only.

## 8. The proof: `check("depth")`

A new instrument on `?nosave&mute` with the tab sized (1280 by 800; `testing.md`), on the proving ground, reading canvas pixels at the places it builds and comparing them with what must show there (the prop's colour where the prop must be, the wall's where the wall must; the frame read straight after the draw, as `check("frame")` does):
1. **A figure half behind a wall segment** (on the far side of a west wall, a tile along it): the near pixels the wall's, the far ones the figure's.
2. **A figure against a back wall is whole** (stopped by the collision at the wall): every row of it shows.
3. **A prop against a wall shows whole, with its whole outline** (Object Highlight on it): no column of it is the wall's colour.
4. **A two-tile table beside a wall**, both slices whole; a cupboard's bottom rows show over the floor.
5. **A tree in front of a house**: canopy pixels over the house's wall; a figure two tiles before the tree over its trunk; **a large oak on the tile against the house's south wall, and one a tile clear of it**, whole in the columns 30 to 75 px right of the trunk over the wall's face (the billboard case).
6. **A figure walks through a door**: in front, in the doorway, behind.
7. **Overlays stand:** a lit wall cap at night shows lit over its own face (the 2026-10-12 cap bug), a hearth's flame over the hearth, a torch's flame over its bracket, the swing outline green round a targeted dead one, a held sword over the hand.
8. **The faded near wall and its cuts** fade (0.42) over the room; **the canopy hole** cuts round the player behind a tree.
9. **The soft sheets show:** a campfire's glow and shadow, a torch post's halo at night, a wall bracket's torch halo, and a carried torch's halo (the per-quad cases).
10. **The flat things show whole:** a floor item, a lying arrow and its shadow, the tile mark, tracks.
11. **The fog** dims the unseen, **the night pool** climbs a wall, **the legibility floor** holds a figure's brightness; the ghost and the marker draw on top.
12. **No flicker, and the range holds:** two frames of the same still scene are byte-identical; in a 3840 by 2160 window at zoom 1.1, a wall and a tree rooted just below the bottom edge draw their tops (the range).
13. **Cost:** frame time on the busiest view (the square at night, a torch lit, twenty of the dead in view) before and after, by `performance.now()` round `renderScene`, within 10%; pass 2's drawable count printed.
14. **A prop just inside a south wall is hidden by it from outside** (a bed against the house's south wall, seen from the yard): no column of the bed shows through the wall, crisp or faded.
15. **A figure before a prop's flank is whole** (at the west end of a chest, 0.3 before it): every column of the figure shows.

`check("frame")` takes a new reference on the first run after the change, as its protocol says. `check("sheets")` is untouched.
